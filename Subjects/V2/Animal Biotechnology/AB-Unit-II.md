## 2.1 Physical and Nutritional Requirements of Cells

Animal cell culture, the process of growing animal cells *in vitro*, requires a carefully controlled environment that mimics the physiological conditions within a living organism. This includes meeting both physical and nutritional needs to ensure cell survival, growth, proliferation, and function.

### 2.1.1 Natural Media

Natural media represent the earliest forms of cell culture media. They are derived from biological sources and provide a complex mixture of nutrients and undefined factors essential for cell growth. While less commonly used today for routine culture due to batch-to-batch variability and ethical concerns, understanding natural media is crucial for historical context and appreciating the evolution of cell culture techniques.

**2.1.1.1 Basal Salt Solutions (BSS) - Types and Composition**

Basal Salt Solutions (BSS) are the simplest form of cell culture media. They are aqueous solutions of inorganic salts designed to maintain the **osmotic balance** and **pH** of the culture environment, mimicking the ionic composition of animal body fluids. BSS alone **do not support cell proliferation** but are vital for cell survival in short-term experiments, washing cells, and preparing more complex media.

**Types of Basal Salt Solutions:**

Several types of BSS exist, differing slightly in their ionic composition and buffering capacity to suit different cell types and experimental needs. Common examples include:

- **Balanced Salt Solutions (BSS):** A general term for solutions designed to maintain physiological conditions.
- **Dulbecco’s Phosphate-Buffered Saline (DPBS or D-PBS):** One of the most widely used BSS, often used for washing cells, diluting reagents, and as a base for more complex media. DPBS is typically isotonic and buffered to a physiological pH (around 7.2-7.4).
- **Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS):** Similar to DPBS but includes additional components like glucose, phenol red pH indicator, and may contain calcium and magnesium. HBSS is often used for short-term maintenance of tissues and organs outside the body, and for procedures requiring calcium and magnesium ions.
- **Earle’s Balanced Salt Solution (EBSS):** Differs from HBSS primarily in bicarbonate concentration. EBSS is designed to be used in incubators with a controlled CO₂ atmosphere, which is necessary for bicarbonate buffering.

**Composition of Basal Salt Solutions (General Components):**

BSS typically contain the following inorganic salts dissolved in high-purity water:

- **Sodium Chloride (NaCl):** Primary salt for maintaining **osmolarity** and ionic balance, mimicking extracellular fluid.
- **Potassium Chloride (KCl):** Important for maintaining **intracellular ionic environment** and membrane potential.
- **Sodium Phosphate (Na₂HPO₄, NaH₂PO₄):** Acts as a **buffer** to maintain pH around physiological levels. Phosphate buffer is effective but has lower buffering capacity compared to bicarbonate.
- **Potassium Phosphate (KH₂PO₄):** Contributes to **buffering capacity** and provides potassium ions.
- **Calcium Chloride (CaCl₂):** Essential for **cell adhesion**, membrane function, and signaling pathways in some cell types. Often omitted in media used for suspension cultures to prevent cell clumping.
- **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄):** Important cofactor for many **enzymatic reactions** and stabilizes ribosomes.
- **Sodium Bicarbonate (NaHCO₃):** A major **physiological buffer** in animal systems. Used in some BSS, particularly EBSS, when CO₂ atmosphere is controlled.
- **Glucose (Dextrose):** A simple **sugar** added to some BSS, particularly HBSS, to provide a minimal energy source for short-term cell maintenance.
- **Phenol Red:** A **pH indicator** added to some BSS (like HBSS) to visually monitor pH changes in the media. Phenol red is red at physiological pH, yellow at acidic pH, and purple at alkaline pH.
