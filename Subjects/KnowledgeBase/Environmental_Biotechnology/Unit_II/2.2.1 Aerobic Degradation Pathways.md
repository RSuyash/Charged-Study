# 2.2.1 Aerobic Degradation Pathways

[[2.2 Aerobic and Anaerobic Degradation Pathways in Microbes|Aerobic degradation pathways]] occur in the presence of oxygen (O₂), which serves as the terminal electron acceptor. These pathways are generally efficient and often lead to the complete [[Mineralization]] of organic compounds. They primarily involve oxidative reactions catalyzed by [[1.2.1 Aerobic Microorganisms]].

## [[2.2.1.1 Oxidative Pathways]]

Aerobic biodegradation is mainly oxidative, using enzymes like oxygenases and dehydrogenases.

-   **Key Reactions:**
    -   *Hydroxylation:* Adds -OH groups (mono-/di-oxygenases). Increases solubility, susceptibility to attack.
    -   *Dehydrogenation:* Removes H atoms (dehydrogenases). Converts alcohols to aldehydes/ketones, increases reactivity.
    -   *Oxygenation:* Incorporates O₂ atoms.
        -   *Monooxygenases:* Incorporate one O atom (other reduced to H₂O). Attack inert compounds (alkanes, aromatics).
        -   *Dioxygenases:* Incorporate both O atoms. Crucial for aromatic ring cleavage.
    -   *Epoxidation:* Forms epoxides from double bonds (mono-/di-oxygenases). Reactive intermediates.
    -   *Hydration:* Adds H₂O across double bonds/epoxides (hydratases, epoxide hydrolases). Increases solubility.
    -   *Decarboxylation:* Removes -COOH as CO₂ (decarboxylases).
-   **Example (Alkane Degradation):** Terminal Oxidation → Alcohol → Aldehyde → Fatty Acid → Beta-oxidation → Krebs Cycle → CO₂ + H₂O.

## [[2.2.1.2 Ring Cleavage Mechanisms]]

Aerobic degradation of aromatic compounds requires specific ring cleavage mechanisms, typically initiated by dioxygenases.

-   **Initial Step:** Dioxygenation forms *cis*-dihydrodiols, which are then dehydrogenated to catechols (or substituted catechols).
-   **Cleavage Pathways:**
    -   ***Ortho* Cleavage:** Catalyzed by Catechol 1,2-dioxygenase (C12O). Cleaves between C1-C2 (adjacent to -OH groups) → *cis,cis*-Muconic acid → Krebs cycle intermediates (Succinyl-CoA, Acetyl-CoA).
    -   ***Meta* Cleavage:** Catalyzed by Catechol 2,3-dioxygenase (C23O). Cleaves between C2-C3 → 2-Hydroxymuconic semialdehyde → Krebs cycle intermediates (Pyruvate, Acetyl-CoA).

Aerobic pathways are central to the function of treatment systems like [[Activated Sludge Process (ASP)]], [[Trickling Filters (TFs)]], and [[Rotating Biological Contactors (RBCs)]].
