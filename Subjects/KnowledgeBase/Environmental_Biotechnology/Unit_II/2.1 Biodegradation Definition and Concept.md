# 2.1 Biodegradation: Definition and Concept

## 2.1.1 Definition and Concept

**Biodegradation** is the breakdown of organic substances into smaller molecules by living organisms, primarily [[Microorganisms]] (bacteria, fungi, archaea). It's a natural process essential for nutrient cycling and waste removal, harnessed in [[Environmental Biotechnology]] for remediation.

![Fig. Chemical Biodegradation Process](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-II/Unit II 1a53a995df87804e836fd3532ab56044/image.png>)
*Fig. Chemical Biodegradation Process*

**Key Concepts:**

-   **Biological Agents:** Mediated mainly by microorganisms using their enzymes.
-   **Enzymatic Reactions:** Relies on specific microbial enzymes (hydrolases, oxygenases, etc.).
-   **Mineralization:** Complete biodegradation converts organics into inorganics (CO₂, H₂O, N₂, salts).
-   **Biotransformation:** Incomplete breakdown, modifying structure without full mineralization. Metabolites may be less, equally, or more toxic.
-   **Cometabolism:** Degradation of a non-growth substrate by enzymes produced for a different growth substrate. Important for pollutants microbes can't use as primary food/energy source.

## 2.1.2 Mechanisms and Pathways

Biodegradation mechanisms vary based on the compound and microbes involved.

**General Mechanisms:**

1.  **Enzymatic Hydrolysis:** Breakdown of polymers (polysaccharides, proteins, lipids) into monomers by extracellular hydrolases.
2.  **Oxidation/Reduction:** Transformation via oxygenases, dehydrogenases (aerobic) or reductases (anaerobic). Introduces functional groups, breaks bonds.
3.  **Ring Cleavage:** Specific mechanisms for aromatic compounds.
    -   *Aerobic:* Dioxygenases cleave rings (*ortho* or *meta* pathways).
    -   *Anaerobic:* More complex reductive pathways.
4.  **Beta-oxidation:** Degradation of fatty acids.
5.  **Central Metabolism:** Intermediates channeled into glycolysis, Krebs cycle.
6.  **Amino Acid Metabolism:** Deamination, carbon skeletons enter central pathways.
7.  **[[Methanogenesis]] (Anaerobic):** Final step converting simple compounds to CH₄ + CO₂.

**Pathways:** Can be linear, branched, convergent, or divergent series of enzymatic reactions.

## 2.1.3 Factors Affecting Biodegradation

Rate and extent depend on intrinsic (substrate, microbes) and extrinsic (environmental) factors.

![Fig. Factors Affecting Biodegradation](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-II/Unit II 1a53a995df87804e836fd3532ab56044/image 1.png>)
*Fig. Factors Affecting Biodegradation*

**1. Intrinsic Factors:**
    -   *Substrate Properties:* Chemical structure (simple vs. complex, halogenation, branching), size, solubility/bioavailability, concentration (too low/high), toxicity.
    -   *Microbial Community:* Diversity, presence of specific degraders/enzymes, acclimation/adaptation potential, population size/activity, interactions (synergism, competition), enzyme production/activity, motility/chemotaxis, biofilm formation.

**2. Extrinsic Factors:**
    -   *Oxygen Availability:* Aerobic vs. Anaerobic pathways.
    -   *Temperature:* Affects microbial/enzyme activity (optimal ranges).
    -   *pH:* Affects microbial/enzyme activity (optimal usually near neutral).
    -   *Nutrient Availability:* C, N, P, minerals essential for microbial growth.
    -   *Moisture Content:* Water essential for metabolism/transport.
    -   *Redox Potential:* Influences dominant microbial processes.
    -   *Electron Acceptors (Anaerobic):* Availability of NO₃⁻, SO₄²⁻, Fe³⁺, etc.
    -   *Inhibitors/Toxins:* Heavy metals, high pollutant concentrations.
    -   *Mixing/Hydrodynamics:* Affects distribution of substrates, nutrients, O₂.
    -   *Pressure:* Relevant in deep environments.
    -   *Light:* Can influence surface processes (photodegradation).
    -   *Solid Phase Characteristics (Soil/Sediment):* Texture, porosity, organic matter affect bioavailability, microbial distribution.

Understanding these factors is key to optimizing bioremediation and wastewater treatment.
