# Composting (MSW)

Composting is an [[1.3 Aerobic Biological Treatments|aerobic biological process]] used as one of the [[2.4.3 Biological Treatment Methods (MSW)|biological treatment methods]] for the organic fraction of [[2.4 Municipal Solid Waste Management|MSW]]. [[1.2.1 Aerobic Microorganisms|Microorganisms]] (bacteria, fungi, actinomycetes) decompose organic waste under controlled aerobic conditions, producing a stable, humus-like soil amendment called **compost**.

## Process Design

1.  **Feedstock Preparation:** Organic fraction (food waste, yard waste) from [[2.4.2 Waste Segregation and Recycling|source segregation]] is shredded/chipped. Bulking agents (wood chips) added for porosity/aeration.
2.  **Composting Methods:**
    *   *Windrow Composting:* Waste piled in long rows (windrows), periodically turned for aeration/mixing. Simple, common for large volumes.
    *   *Aerated Static Pile:* Waste piled statically, air blown through pipes. Faster, better control than windrows.
    *   *In-Vessel Composting:* Enclosed reactors/vessels. Most controlled (temp, aeration, moisture), fastest, highest capital cost.
3.  **Curing:** After active composting, material cured (weeks/months) in piles/windrows for further stabilization and maturation.

## Stages of Composting

![Fig. Four Stages of Composting](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-II/Unit II 1a53a995df87804e836fd3532ab56044/image 7.png>)
*Fig. Four Stages of Composting*

1.  **Mesophilic Phase:** Initial phase, temp rises (20-45°C). Mesophiles break down readily degradable organics.
2.  **Thermophilic Phase:** Active phase, temp rises significantly (50-70°C+). Thermophiles break down complex polymers (cellulose, proteins, lipids). High temps kill pathogens/weed seeds.
3.  **Cooling/Mesophilic Phase (Curing):** Temp decreases. Mesophiles re-establish. Further stabilization, humification.
4.  **Maturation Phase:** Compost stabilizes into humus-like product.

## Operational Parameters and Control

-   **Carbon-to-Nitrogen Ratio (C:N):** Optimal ~25:1 to 30:1. Adjust feedstock mix.
-   **Aeration/Oxygen:** Maintain aerobic conditions (sufficient O₂). Control via turning or forced aeration.
-   **Moisture Content:** Optimal ~40-60%. Control via initial mixing and potentially adding water.
-   **Temperature:** Monitor to ensure thermophilic range is reached. Control via aeration/turning.
-   **Particle Size/Porosity:** Ensure adequate structure for airflow using appropriate shredding and bulking agents.
-   **pH:** Generally self-regulating (slightly acidic to neutral).
-   **Turning Frequency (Windrows):** Regular turning essential.
