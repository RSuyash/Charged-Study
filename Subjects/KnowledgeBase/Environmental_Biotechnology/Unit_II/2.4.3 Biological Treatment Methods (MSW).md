# 2.4.3 Biological Treatment Methods (MSW)

Biological treatment methods are crucial for managing the organic fraction of [[2.4 Municipal Solid Waste Management|Municipal Solid Waste (MSW)]], diverting biodegradable waste from landfills and converting it into valuable products. These methods rely on microbial activity.

The two primary biological treatment methods for MSW are:

1.  **[[Composting (MSW)]] (Aerobic):** Microorganisms decompose organic waste in the presence of oxygen, producing compost (a soil amendment).
2.  **[[Anaerobic Digestion (MSW)]] (Anaerobic):** Microorganisms break down organic waste in the absence of oxygen, producing biogas (renewable energy) and digestate (fertilizer/soil amendment).

These methods offer sustainable alternatives to landfilling the organic fraction of MSW, contributing to resource recovery and reducing environmental impacts like greenhouse gas emissions (methane from landfills) and leachate generation. Effective [[2.4.2 Waste Segregation and Recycling|source segregation]] is key to providing suitable organic feedstock for these processes.
