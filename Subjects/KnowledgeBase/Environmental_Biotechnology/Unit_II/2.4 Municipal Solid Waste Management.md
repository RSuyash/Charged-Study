# 2.4 Municipal Solid Waste Management

Municipal Solid Waste (MSW) management addresses the challenge of handling waste generated from households, commercial establishments, institutions, and municipal services. Effective MSW management aims to minimize environmental impacts, conserve resources, and protect public health. [[Environmental Biotechnology]] plays a role through biological treatment methods.

![image.png](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-II/Unit II 1a53a995df87804e836fd3532ab56044/image 4.png>)

Key aspects include:

-   [[2.4.1 Composition of Municipal Solid Waste]]: Understanding the heterogeneous mix of organic and inorganic materials.
-   [[2.4.2 Waste Segregation and Recycling]]: Crucial first steps for resource recovery and efficient treatment.
-   [[2.4.3 Biological Treatment Methods (MSW)]]: Managing the organic fraction via [[Composting (MSW)]] and [[Anaerobic Digestion (MSW)]].

Integrated Solid Waste Management (ISWM) often involves a combination of source reduction, recycling, biological treatment, waste-to-energy (incineration), and landfilling.
