# 2.2.1.1 Oxidative Pathways

Oxidative pathways are the primary mechanisms used by [[1.2.1 Aerobic Microorganisms]] in [[2.2.1 Aerobic Degradation Pathways|aerobic biodegradation]]. These pathways utilize molecular oxygen (O₂) directly or indirectly to break down organic pollutants.

## Key Oxidative Reactions

Key reactions involved include:
-   **Hydroxylation:** Addition of -OH groups.
-   **Dehydrogenation:** Removal of H atoms.
-   **Oxygenation:** Incorporation of O atoms (via monooxygenases or dioxygenases).
-   **Epoxidation:** Formation of epoxides.
-   **Hydration:** Addition of water.
-   **Decarboxylation:** Removal of -COOH as CO₂.

These reactions, often catalyzed by oxygenases and dehydrogenases, increase the polarity and reactivity of pollutants, facilitating their breakdown and eventual entry into central metabolic pathways like the Krebs cycle for [[Mineralization]].

*(See [[2.2.1 Aerobic Degradation Pathways]] for more details on specific reactions and examples like alkane degradation).*
