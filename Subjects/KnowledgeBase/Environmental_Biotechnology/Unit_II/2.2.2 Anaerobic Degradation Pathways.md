# 2.2.2 Anaerobic Degradation Pathways

[[2.2 Aerobic and Anaerobic Degradation Pathways in Microbes|Anaerobic degradation pathways]] operate in the absence of oxygen (anoxic conditions). [[1.2.2 Anaerobic Microorganisms]] utilize alternative electron acceptors like nitrate (NO₃⁻), sulfate (SO₄²⁻), iron(III) (Fe³⁺), or carbon dioxide (CO₂) for respiration, or employ [[Fermentative Pathways|fermentation]]. These pathways are generally slower and yield less energy than [[2.2.1 Aerobic Degradation Pathways|aerobic pathways]] but are crucial in environments like sediments, waterlogged soils, and [[Anaerobic Digestion|anaerobic digesters]].

## Key Anaerobic Processes

1.  **Hydrolysis:** Breakdown of complex polymers into monomers by extracellular enzymes (similar to aerobic).
2.  **[[Fermentative Pathways]]:** Partial oxidation of organic compounds (typically monomers) without external electron acceptors. Produces ATP via substrate-level phosphorylation and yields fermentation products like volatile fatty acids (VFAs), alcohols, H₂, and CO₂. Essential first step in converting complex organics to simpler substrates for other anaerobes.
3.  **Acetogenesis:** Conversion of VFAs (except acetate) and alcohols into acetate, H₂, and CO₂. Often requires [[Syntrophic Bacteria|syntrophy]] with H₂-consuming organisms (like methanogens) to be thermodynamically feasible.
4.  **Terminal Electron Accepting Processes:** Utilize the products of fermentation and acetogenesis.
    *   **[[Methanogenic Pathways]]:** Final step in many anaerobic environments. [[Methanogenic Archaea]] convert acetate and H₂/CO₂ into methane (CH₄) and CO₂ ([[Biogas]]). Includes acetoclastic and hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis.
    *   **Sulfate Reduction:** Sulfate-reducing bacteria (SRB) use sulfate as the electron acceptor, reducing it to sulfide (S²⁻), while oxidizing organic compounds or H₂. Common in marine sediments.
    *   **Denitrification:** Denitrifying bacteria use nitrate as the electron acceptor, reducing it sequentially to nitrogen gas (N₂), while oxidizing organic compounds or H₂. Important for nitrogen removal in wastewater treatment.
    *   **Iron(III) Reduction:** Iron-reducing bacteria (IRB) use Fe(III) as the electron acceptor, reducing it to Fe(II), while oxidizing organic compounds or H₂. Important in soils and sediments.
    *   *(Other acceptors like manganese(IV), fumarate, etc., are also used by specific microbes).*

## Anaerobic Degradation of Specific Compounds

-   **Aromatic Compounds:** Degradation is more complex than aerobic pathways. Often involves initial activation (e.g., addition to fumarate), followed by ring reduction and reductive cleavage pathways.
-   **Halogenated Compounds:** Reductive dehalogenation (removal of halogen, replaced by H) is a key anaerobic mechanism, often carried out by specialized bacteria like *Dehalococcoides*.

Anaerobic pathways form complex food webs where the products of one microbial group serve as substrates for the next, often involving crucial syntrophic interactions.
