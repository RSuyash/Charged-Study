# Fermentative Pathways

Fermentation is an [[2.2.2 Anaerobic Degradation Pathways|anaerobic metabolic process]] where microorganisms degrade organic compounds (typically carbohydrates) to generate ATP energy **without using an external electron acceptor** (like O₂, NO₃⁻, SO₄²⁻). Organic compounds serve as both electron donors and acceptors.

## General Characteristics

-   **No External Electron Acceptor:** Distinguishes fermentation from anaerobic respiration.
-   **Substrate-Level Phosphorylation:** Primary mode of ATP generation (less efficient than oxidative phosphorylation).
-   **Partial Oxidation:** Substrates are only partially oxidized; end products are still organic compounds.
-   **Redox Balance:** Internal redox balance maintained by transferring electrons from intermediates to other organic intermediates, regenerating NAD⁺ from NADH.
-   **Diverse End Products:** Different pathways yield various products:
    -   *Volatile Fatty Acids (VFAs):* Acetate, propionate, butyrate, lactate.
    -   *Alcohols:* Ethanol, butanol.
    -   *Gases:* Hydrogen (H₂), Carbon Dioxide (CO₂).
    -   *Other Organic Acids:* Succinate, formate.
-   **Role in Anaerobic Food Chain:** Fermentative bacteria (acidogens) perform initial breakdown (acidogenesis) in [[Anaerobic Digestion]], converting monomers (from hydrolysis) into substrates (VFAs, alcohols, H₂) used by acetogens and methanogens. They also help establish anaerobic conditions.
-   **[[Syntrophic Bacteria|Syntrophy]]:** Fermenters often work syntrophically with other anaerobes.

## Examples of Fermentative Pathways

-   **Ethanol Fermentation (*Saccharomyces*):** Glucose → 2 Ethanol + 2 CO₂
-   **Lactic Acid Fermentation (*Lactobacillus*):** Glucose → 2 Lactic acid
-   **Mixed Acid Fermentation (*E. coli*):** Glucose → Lactate + Acetate + Succinate + Ethanol + CO₂ + H₂
-   **Butyric Acid Fermentation (*Clostridium*):** Glucose → Butyric acid + 2 CO₂ + 2 H₂
-   **Propionic Acid Fermentation (*Propionibacterium*):** Lactate/Glucose → Propionic acid + Acetic acid + CO₂

Fermentation is a fundamental anaerobic process crucial for the initial stages of organic matter breakdown in the absence of oxygen.
