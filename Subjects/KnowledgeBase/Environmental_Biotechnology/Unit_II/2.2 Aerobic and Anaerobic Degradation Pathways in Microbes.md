# 2.2 Aerobic and Anaerobic Degradation Pathways in Microbes

[[Microorganisms]] employ distinct metabolic pathways for [[2.1 Biodegradation Definition and Concept|biodegradation]] depending on the presence or absence of oxygen.

## [[2.2.1 Aerobic Degradation Pathways]]

-   **Conditions:** Occur in the presence of oxygen (O₂).
-   **Electron Acceptor:** Oxygen is the terminal electron acceptor.
-   **Efficiency:** Generally faster and more energetically favorable, often leading to complete [[Mineralization]].
-   **Key Mechanisms:** Primarily oxidative, involving oxygenases (mono- & di-) and dehydrogenases. Includes hydroxylation, dehydrogenation, oxygenation, epoxidation, hydration, decarboxylation.
-   **Ring Cleavage:** Aromatic rings cleaved via dioxygenases (*ortho* or *meta* pathways).
-   **Examples:** Aerobic degradation of alkanes, alkenes, aromatic hydrocarbons.

## [[2.2.2 Anaerobic Degradation Pathways]]

-   **Conditions:** Occur in the absence of oxygen (anoxic environments).
-   **Electron Acceptors:** Alternative electron acceptors used (nitrate, sulfate, Fe(III), CO₂).
-   **Efficiency:** Generally slower and less energetically favorable than aerobic pathways.
-   **Key Mechanisms:** Involve hydrolysis, fermentation, acetogenesis, and methanogenesis (or other terminal electron accepting processes like denitrification, sulfate reduction). Activation steps (e.g., fumarate addition) often required for inert compounds. Ring cleavage involves reductive pathways.
-   **[[Fermentative Pathways]]:** Partial oxidation without external electron acceptors, producing VFAs, alcohols, H₂, CO₂.
-   **[[Methanogenic Pathways]]:** Final step in many anaerobic environments, converting acetate and H₂/CO₂ to methane (CH₄). Relies heavily on [[Syntrophic Bacteria|syntrophy]].
-   **Examples:** Anaerobic degradation of complex organic matter in sediments, digesters; reductive dechlorination of organochlorines.

The specific pathways utilized depend on the pollutant's structure, the available electron acceptors, and the metabolic capabilities of the microbial community present.
