# 2.4.1 Composition of Municipal Solid Waste

[[2.4 Municipal Solid Waste Management|Municipal Solid Waste (MSW)]] is a heterogeneous mix generated from households, businesses, etc. Its composition varies significantly based on location, socioeconomic factors, season, and management practices. Understanding composition is crucial for designing effective management strategies.

## Typical MSW Components

1.  **Organic Waste (Biodegradable Fraction):** Often the largest component (40-60%+).
    *   *Food Waste:* Scraps, uneaten food. Highly biodegradable, causes odor/GHG in landfills.
    *   *Yard Waste:* Grass, leaves, branches. Readily biodegradable, suitable for [[Composting (MSW)]].
    *   *Paper/Cardboard:* Recyclable, remainder is biodegradable (slower if coated). (15-30%)
    *   *Wood:* Scraps, furniture. Biodegradable (rate varies).
    *   *Textiles:* Natural fibers (cotton, wool) are biodegradable; synthetics (polyester) less so.
2.  **Inorganic Waste (Non-Biodegradable Fraction):**
    *   *Plastics:* Significant, growing fraction (PET, HDPE, PVC, etc.). Most not biodegradable. (10-15%)
    *   *Glass:* Bottles, jars. Recyclable, non-biodegradable. (5-10%)
    *   *Metals:* Ferrous (steel) and non-ferrous (aluminum). Recyclable, non-biodegradable. (5-10%)
    *   *Construction & Demolition (C&D) Debris:* Bricks, concrete, wood, drywall. Largely inert.
    *   *Household Hazardous Waste (HHW):* Paints, solvents, pesticides, batteries, e-waste. Require special handling/disposal.
3.  **Other Components:** (5-15%)
    *   *Composite Materials:* Difficult to recycle (e.g., Tetra Pak).
    *   *Special Wastes:* Bulky items (furniture), tires.

## Variations in Composition

-   **Geographic/Economic:** Developed countries often have more packaging/plastics; developing countries more organics.
-   **Socioeconomic:** Income levels affect consumption patterns and waste types.
-   **Seasonal:** Yard waste varies; food waste may increase during holidays.
-   **Management Practices:** Effective [[2.4.2 Waste Segregation and Recycling|segregation/recycling]] alters composition reaching final disposal.

Knowing the specific composition of local MSW is vital for planning appropriate collection, treatment (recycling, composting, anaerobic digestion), and disposal methods.
