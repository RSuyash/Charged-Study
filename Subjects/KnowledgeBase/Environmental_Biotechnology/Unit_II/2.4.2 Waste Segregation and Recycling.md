# 2.4.2 Waste Segregation and Recycling

Waste segregation at source and recycling are fundamental components of sustainable [[2.4 Municipal Solid Waste Management|MSW management]].

## Waste Segregation

Separating different waste fractions at the point of generation (source).

![Fig. Waste Segregation](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-II/Unit II 1a53a995df87804e836fd3532ab56044/image 6.png>)
*Fig. Waste Segregation*

-   **Importance:**
    -   Enhances **Recycling** efficiency and value (reduces contamination).
    -   Improves **[[2.4.3 Biological Treatment Methods (MSW)|Biological Treatment]]** (composting, anaerobic digestion) by providing clean organic feedstock.
    -   Reduces **Landfill** volume and environmental impact (leachate, GHG).
    -   Facilitates safe handling/disposal of **Household Hazardous Waste (HHW)**.
-   **Common Categories:**
    -   *Recyclables:* Paper/cardboard, plastics (specific types), glass, metals.
    *   *Organic Waste:* Food scraps, yard waste.
    *   *HHW:* Chemicals, batteries, e-waste, sharps.
    *   *General/Residual Waste:* Non-recyclable, non-compostable, non-hazardous fraction (to be minimized).
-   **Methods:** Color-coded bins/bags, public awareness campaigns, separate curbside collection, drop-off centers, incentive programs.

## Recycling

Collecting and processing used materials to manufacture new products.

-   **Process:** Collection → Sorting (at Materials Recovery Facility - MRF) → Cleaning → Processing (pulping paper, melting plastic/glass/metal) → Manufacturing new products.
-   **Benefits:**
    -   Resource Conservation (reduces need for virgin materials).
    -   Energy Savings (less energy than manufacturing from raw materials).
    -   Pollution Reduction (less pollution from extraction/processing).
    -   Landfill Diversion.
    -   Economic Benefits (jobs, revenue from recycled materials).
-   **Challenges:**
    -   Contamination of recyclables reduces quality/value.
    -   Variable quality of collected materials.
    -   Market fluctuations for recycled materials affect economic viability.
    -   Requires investment in infrastructure/technology.
    -   Public confusion ("Wish-cycling" non-recyclables).

Effective segregation and recycling are key to moving towards a circular economy model for waste management.
