# 2.3.1 Mechanisms and Pathways (Hydrocarbons)

The [[2.3 Biodegradation of Hydrocarbons|biodegradation of hydrocarbons]] involves diverse microbial mechanisms and pathways, differing for various hydrocarbon types and between aerobic and anaerobic conditions.

## 1. Aerobic Biodegradation

Generally faster and more complete, using O₂.

![Fig. General pathways of aerobic biodegradation of hydrocarbons](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-II/Unit II 1a53a995df87804e836fd3532ab56044/image 2.png>)
*Fig. General pathways of aerobic biodegradation of hydrocarbons*

-   **Alkanes (Saturated):**
    -   *Terminal Oxidation (Most Common):* Alkane monooxygenase hydroxylates terminal C → Primary Alcohol → Aldehyde (via alcohol dehydrogenase) → Fatty Acid (via aldehyde dehydrogenase) → [[Beta-oxidation]] → Krebs Cycle.
    -   *Subterminal Oxidation:* Less common, hydroxylation at non-terminal C.
-   **Alkenes (Unsaturated):**
    -   *Epoxidation:* Alkene mono-/di-oxygenase adds O across double bond → Epoxide.
    -   *Hydrolysis:* Epoxide hydrolase converts epoxide → *vicinal* diol.
    -   *Further Metabolism:* Diol metabolized via various pathways.
-   **Aromatic Hydrocarbons (Benzene, Toluene, etc.):**
    -   *Dioxygenation:* Aromatic dioxygenases add O₂ → *cis*-Dihydrodiols.
    -   *Dehydrogenation:* Dihydrodiol dehydrogenases oxidize dihydrodiols → Catechols.
    -   *[[2.2.1.2 Ring Cleavage Mechanisms (Aerobic)|Ring Cleavage]]:* Catechols cleaved by dioxygenases (*ortho* or *meta* pathways) → Krebs cycle intermediates.
-   **Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs):**
    -   *Initial Steps:* Similar dioxygenation to aromatics → *cis*-dihydrodiols.
    -   *Sequential Ring Cleavage:* More complex due to fused rings. Degradation proceeds ring by ring. Higher MW / angular PAHs are more recalcitrant.

## 2. Anaerobic Biodegradation

Slower, uses alternative electron acceptors (NO₃⁻, SO₄²⁻, Fe³⁺, CO₂).

![Fig. Anaerobic biodegradation of saturated and aromatic hydrocarbons](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-II/Unit II 1a53a995df87804e836fd3532ab56044/image 3.png>)
*Fig. Anaerobic biodegradation of saturated and aromatic hydrocarbons*

-   **Activation Step (Common):** Often involves addition to fumarate via glycyl radical enzymes (e.g., benzylsuccinate synthase for toluene). This activates the relatively inert hydrocarbon for anaerobic attack.
-   **Oxidation Coupled to Electron Acceptor Reduction:**
    -   *Nitrate Reduction ([[Denitrification]]):* Activated hydrocarbon oxidized, NO₃⁻ reduced to N₂.
    -   *Sulfate Reduction:* Activated hydrocarbon oxidized, SO₄²⁻ reduced to S²⁻ (important in marine sediments).
    -   *Iron(III) Reduction:* Activated hydrocarbon oxidized, Fe³⁺ reduced to Fe²⁺ (important in anoxic soils/sediments).
-   **[[Methanogenesis]]:** Terminal process using smaller intermediates (acetate, H₂/CO₂) produced by other anaerobes.
-   **Anaerobic Aromatic Degradation:** Complex pathways involving activation (fumarate addition), ring reduction, and reductive ring cleavage mechanisms. Carboxylation can also occur.

Understanding these specific pathways is key for designing effective [[Bioremediation]] strategies.
