# Composting (Ex-situ Bioremediation)

Composting, when applied as an **[[Ex-situ Bioremediation|ex-situ bioremediation]]** method, utilizes controlled [[1.3 Aerobic Biological Treatments|aerobic biological decomposition]] processes, similar to [[Composting (MSW)|MSW composting]], to treat contaminated solid materials, particularly soil and organic waste. It essentially functions as a **solid-state [[Bioreactors (Bioremediation)|bioreactor]]**.

## Adaptations for Bioremediation

-   **Feedstock:** Contaminated soil/sediment is the primary material, often mixed with organic amendments (bulking agents like wood chips, green waste, manure) to improve structure, porosity (aeration), C:N ratio, and microbial activity.
-   **Target Pollutants:** Effective for biodegradable organic pollutants in soil/solids:
    -   [[2.3 Biodegradation of Hydrocarbons|Hydrocarbons]] (Petroleum - TPH, PAHs).
    -   [[4.2.1 Pesticide Degradation|Pesticides]] / [[4.2.2 Herbicide Degradation|Herbicides]].
    -   Explosives (TNT, RDX - may need pre-treatment).
-   **Enhanced Conditions:** Conditions optimized specifically for pollutant degradation:
    -   *Nutrient Amendment:* Control C:N:P ratio for microbial needs.
    *   *[[Bioaugmentation]]:* Optionally add specific pollutant-degrading microbes.
    *   *Bulking Agents:* Selected to improve aeration and potentially enhance degradation (e.g., lignin-rich materials for PAHs).
    *   *Temperature Control:* Maintain thermophilic temperatures (50-70°C+) for faster degradation and pathogen kill.
-   **Leachate Management:** Leachate from composting contaminated material may contain pollutants and requires proper collection/treatment.
-   **Compost Quality/End Use:** Final compost tested for residual pollutants to determine suitability for reuse (e.g., soil amendment, landfill cover). Must meet regulatory limits.

## Applications

-   *Ex-situ* treatment of excavated contaminated soil.
-   Brownfield site remediation.
-   Treatment of contaminated dredged sediments (after dewatering).
-   Can be combined with phytoremediation (using compost to amend soil).

## Advantages & Limitations

-   **Advantages:** Relatively low-cost (vs. other ex-situ), environmentally friendly, produces potentially beneficial end product (compost), effective for moderate contamination/large volumes.
-   **Limitations:** Requires excavation/transport, treatment times can be long (weeks/months), less effective for high contamination or recalcitrant pollutants, requires land area, potential odors/leachate need management, compost end-use may be restricted by residual contaminants.

Ex-situ composting provides a robust biological treatment option for certain types of contaminated soils and solid wastes.
