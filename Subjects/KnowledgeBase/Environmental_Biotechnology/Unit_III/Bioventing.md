# Bioventing

Bioventing is an **[[In-situ Bioremediation|in-situ bioremediation]]** technology primarily used for remediating unsaturated soil (vadose zone) contaminated with volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and some semi-volatile organic compounds (SVOCs), particularly petroleum hydrocarbons.

*Fig. Diagrammatic representation of in situ bioventing technique*

## Principle

Bioventing enhances natural [[2.2.1 Aerobic Degradation Pathways|aerobic biodegradation]] by increasing **oxygen availability** in the subsurface, which is often the limiting factor. Air is injected at low flow rates into the contaminated soil.

## Process Design and Operation

1.  **Air Injection Wells:** Installed into the vadose zone (vertical or horizontal).
2.  **Airflow/Oxygen Supply:** Air injected at low flow rates increases O₂ concentration in soil pores, stimulating indigenous aerobic microbes.
3.  **Volatilization & Biodegradation:** Airflow promotes volatilization of VOCs, which can then be biodegraded in the soil gas phase or as they migrate. The primary removal mechanism is intended to be biodegradation, not just volatilization (unlike Soil Vapor Extraction - SVE).
4.  **Monitoring Wells:** Track contaminant levels, O₂, soil gas composition, microbial activity.
5.  **Vapor Extraction (Optional):** SVE wells may be used to control vapor migration and capture excess VOCs for treatment (e.g., activated carbon).
6.  **Nutrient Amendment (Optional):** Nutrients (N, P) can be added via injection or infiltration if limiting.

## Operational Parameters and Control

-   **Air Injection Rate:** Optimized for O₂ supply vs. energy use/drying. Low flow rates distinguish it from SVE.
-   **Air Injection Pressure:** Typically low.
-   **Well Spacing/Design:** Based on site geology, soil permeability, contaminant distribution.
-   **Monitoring:** O₂, VOCs (soil gas, soil), moisture, temperature.
-   **Nutrient/Moisture Control:** Amendments added if needed.

## Applications

-   Soil contaminated with VOCs (fuels like gasoline/diesel, solvents like BTEX, chlorinated solvents).
-   Leaking Underground Storage Tank (LUST) sites.
-   Industrial sites, brownfields.
-   Vadose zone treatment only.

## Advantages

-   *In-situ* treatment (minimal excavation/cost).
-   Relatively low cost and energy use.
-   Enhances natural biodegradation.
-   Minimal site disruption.

## Limitations

-   Limited to unsaturated zone.
-   Less effective for non-volatile/poorly volatile compounds.
-   Can be slow.
-   Effectiveness depends on soil permeability/air distribution.
-   May require vapor control/treatment.

Bioventing focuses on stimulating *in-situ* biodegradation by providing oxygen, differing from SVE which primarily focuses on physical removal via volatilization.
