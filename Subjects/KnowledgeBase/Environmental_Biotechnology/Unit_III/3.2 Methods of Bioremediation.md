# 3.2 Methods of Bioremediation

[[3.1 Introduction, Definition, and Concept (Bioremediation)|Bioremediation]] methods are broadly classified based on where the treatment occurs relative to the contaminated site.

## 1. [[In-situ Bioremediation]]

-   **Definition:** Treatment occurs *in place* at the contaminated site (soil, groundwater, sediment) without excavation or removal.
-   **Advantages:** Generally less disruptive, more cost-effective, environmentally friendly than ex-situ.
-   **Methods:**
    -   [[Bioventing]]: Enhances aerobic degradation in unsaturated soil by injecting air (oxygen).
    -   [[Biosparging]]: Enhances aerobic degradation in saturated soil/groundwater by injecting air below the water table. Often combined with Soil Vapor Extraction (SVE).
    -   [[Bioaugmentation]]: Adding specific pollutant-degrading microorganisms to enhance degradation. Used when indigenous microbes are insufficient or lack capability.
    -   *Biostimulation:* Adding nutrients (N, P) or electron acceptors/donors (O₂, NO₃⁻) to stimulate indigenous microbial activity (often part of bioventing/biosparging).
    -   *Phytoremediation:* Using plants to remove, degrade, or contain contaminants (not detailed in this specific source section but relevant).
    -   *Monitored Natural Attenuation (MNA):* Relying on natural processes (including intrinsic bioremediation) while monitoring contaminant levels.

## 2. [[Ex-situ Bioremediation]]

-   **Definition:** Contaminated material (soil, sediment, water) is excavated or removed and treated elsewhere in engineered systems.
-   **Advantages:** Offers greater control over conditions (temperature, pH, nutrients, oxygen), potentially faster rates, higher efficiency, applicable to wider range of contaminants/soil types.
-   **Disadvantages:** More costly and disruptive (excavation, transport), potential for contaminant release during handling.
-   **Methods:**
    -   [[Landfarming]]: Soil spread in thin layers, tilled, amended for aerobic degradation. Simple, low-cost for large volumes.
    -   [[Bioreactors]]: Engineered containment systems (slurry reactors, solid-state/composting reactors, SBRs, MBRs) providing optimized, controlled conditions. Faster, more efficient, higher cost/complexity.
    -   [[Biopiles]]: Soil piled, amended, often aerated. Contained ex-situ method.
    -   [[Composting (Ex-situ)]]: Using composting principles to treat contaminated soil/solid waste, often mixed with organic amendments.

The choice between in-situ and ex-situ methods depends on site characteristics, contaminant type/concentration, remediation goals, cost, time constraints, and regulatory requirements. Integrated approaches combining different methods are often employed.
