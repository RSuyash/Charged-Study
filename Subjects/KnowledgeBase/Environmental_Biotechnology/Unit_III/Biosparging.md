# Biosparging

Biosparging is an **[[In-situ Bioremediation|in-situ bioremediation]]** technology used to treat groundwater and saturated soil contaminated with volatile and semi-volatile organic compounds ([[VOCs]], SVOCs), particularly petroleum hydrocarbons and solvents. It enhances [[2.2.1 Aerobic Degradation Pathways|aerobic biodegradation]] by delivering oxygen directly into the saturated zone.

## Principle

Air is injected below the water table (saturated zone). Rising air bubbles transfer oxygen to the groundwater, stimulating indigenous aerobic microorganisms to degrade dissolved and sorbed contaminants. It differs from [[Bioventing]], which targets the unsaturated zone.

## Process Design and Operation

1.  **Air Sparge Wells:** Installed into the saturated zone below the water table.
2.  **Air Injection:** Air injected through sparge wells rises, creating channels and increasing dissolved oxygen (DO) in groundwater.
3.  **Enhanced Aerobic Biodegradation:** Increased DO stimulates aerobic microbes to degrade pollutants.
4.  **Volatilization/Air Stripping (Co-benefit):** Rising air bubbles strip VOCs from groundwater into the vapor phase.
5.  **Monitoring Wells:** Track contaminants, DO, groundwater flow, etc.
6.  **Soil Vapor Extraction (SVE) System (Often Combined):** SVE wells in the unsaturated zone capture volatilized VOCs stripped by biosparging. Extracted vapors may require treatment (e.g., activated carbon). Biosparging + SVE provides comprehensive treatment for both saturated and unsaturated zones.
7.  **Nutrient Amendment (Optional):** Nutrients (N, P) added via injection wells or with air if limiting.

## Operational Parameters and Control

-   **Air Injection Rate:** Optimized for O₂ delivery/stripping vs. energy/mounding.
-   **Air Injection Pressure:** Typically low to moderate.
-   **Well Spacing/Design:** Based on aquifer characteristics (hydraulic conductivity), contaminant distribution, radius of influence.
-   **Monitoring:** DO, VOCs (groundwater, soil vapor), groundwater elevation.
-   **SVE Operation (if used):** Coordinated with biosparging.
-   **Nutrient Delivery:** Controlled addition if used.
-   **Groundwater Flow:** Understanding flow patterns is important.

## Applications

-   Groundwater contaminated with VOCs (fuels, solvents like BTEX, chlorinated solvents).
-   Leaking Underground Storage Tank (LUST) sites.
-   Industrial sites, brownfields with groundwater contamination.
-   Saturated zone treatment.

## Advantages

-   *In-situ* treatment of groundwater/saturated soil.
-   Relatively low cost/energy use.
-   Enhances natural aerobic biodegradation.
-   Can be combined with SVE for comprehensive VOC removal.

## Limitations

-   Less effective for non-volatile compounds.
-   Can be slow.
-   Effectiveness depends on aquifer characteristics (permeability, heterogeneity) and air distribution.
-   May require SVE/vapor treatment.
-   Potential for groundwater mounding or air breakthrough if poorly designed/operated.

Biosparging is a common and effective technology for stimulating aerobic biodegradation in contaminated groundwater.
