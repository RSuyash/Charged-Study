# 1.1.1.2 Sedimentation and Flotation

Following [[1.1.1.1 Screening and Grit Removal]], sedimentation and flotation are key [[1.1.1 Primary Treatment|primary treatment]] processes used to remove suspended solids and floatable materials from wastewater.

## Sedimentation (Settling)

-   **Principle:** Gravity-based separation of suspended solids heavier than water. Wastewater held in quiescent conditions allows solids to settle to the bottom.
-   **Process Design:** Sedimentation basins/tanks provide settling time.
    -   *Rectangular Basins:* Simple, horizontal flow, sludge scraped to hopper.
    *   *Circular Basins (Clarifiers):* Often use rotating scrapers for sludge collection in central hopper.
    *   *Inclined Plate/Tube Settlers:* Increase settling area in smaller footprint, reducing settling distance.
-   **Operation/Control:**
    -   *Detention Time:* Time wastewater held (typically 1.5-2.5 hours for primary). Longer time = better removal (but larger tanks).
    *   *Surface Overflow Rate (SOR):* Flow rate per unit surface area. Lower SOR = better settling.
    *   *Solids Loading Rate:* Mass of solids per unit area per time. Avoid overloading.
    *   *Sludge Removal:* Regular removal of settled primary sludge is crucial.

## Flotation

-   **Principle:** Removes suspended solids and materials lighter than water (oil, grease) by attaching air bubbles to particles, causing them to float. Floated material (scum) is skimmed off.
-   **Process Design:**
    -   *Dissolved Air Flotation (DAF):* Most common. Air dissolved under pressure, released at atmospheric pressure creating fine bubbles.
    *   *Induced Air Flotation (IAF):* Air drawn in mechanically (impellers). Creates larger bubbles, less efficient for fine particles but good for oil/grease.
    *   *Vacuum Flotation:* Pressure reduced to create bubbles (less common).
-   **Operation/Control:**
    -   *Air-to-Solids Ratio (A/S):* Optimal ratio crucial for efficiency.
    -   *Recycle Ratio (DAF):* Proportion of treated effluent recycled/saturated with air.
    *   *Pressure (DAF):* Higher pressure = finer bubbles.
    -   *Chemical Addition:* Coagulants/flocculants often added to enhance particle aggregation/flotation.

Sedimentation is standard for removing settleable solids in municipal wastewater, while flotation is more common for industrial wastewater containing oils, grease, or light suspended solids.
