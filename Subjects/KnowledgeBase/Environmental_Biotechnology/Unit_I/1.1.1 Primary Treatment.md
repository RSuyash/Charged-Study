# 1.1.1 Primary Treatment

Primary treatment is the initial stage of [[1.1 Domestic (Municipal) and Industrial Waste Water Treatments|wastewater treatment]], focusing on the **physical removal** of large solids and suspended materials. Its main goal is to reduce the load on subsequent treatment stages and protect equipment.

![Fig. Primary Treatment Process](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-I/Unit I 1a53a995df8780f69720e68a76b33240/image 1.png>)
*Fig. Primary Treatment Process*

Key processes in primary treatment include:

-   [[1.1.1.1 Screening and Grit Removal]]: Removes large debris and heavy inorganic solids.
-   [[1.1.1.2 Sedimentation and Flotation]]: Removes settleable suspended solids and floatable materials like oil and grease.

Primary treatment typically removes about 50-70% of suspended solids and 25-40% of Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) from municipal wastewater. The solids removed during primary treatment form **primary sludge**, which requires further treatment and disposal.
