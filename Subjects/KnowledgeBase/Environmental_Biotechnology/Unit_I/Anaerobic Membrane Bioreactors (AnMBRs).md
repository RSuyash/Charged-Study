# Anaerobic Membrane Bioreactors (AnMBRs)

Anaerobic Membrane Bioreactors (AnMBRs) are an advanced [[1.4 Anaerobic Biological Treatments|anaerobic treatment]] technology combining [[Anaerobic Digestion]] with [[1.1.3.2 Membrane Filtration|membrane filtration]] (typically UF or MF) within a single reactor system. The source text specifically mentions Air Lift AnMBRs, but the concept is broader.

![Fig. Air Lift Membrane Bioreactors](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-I/Unit I 1a53a995df8780f69720e68a76b33240/image 9.png>)
*Fig. Air Lift Membrane Bioreactors*

## [[1.4.1.1 Principles and Process Design (AnMBRs)]]

-   **Principles:** Combine anaerobic breakdown of organics (producing [[Biogas]]) with membrane separation. Membranes provide a physical barrier for complete biomass retention, allowing high biomass concentrations and long SRTs decoupled from HRT. Produces high-quality, solids-free effluent. Air lift (in Air Lift AnMBRs) uses injected air for mixing and membrane scouring.
-   **Process Design:** Anaerobic reactor containing submerged membrane modules (hollow fiber, flat sheet). Includes systems for permeate extraction, biogas collection, and potentially air lift circulation/scouring.

## [[1.4.1.2 Operational Parameters and Control (AnMBRs)]]

-   **Key Parameters:** Temperature (Mesophilic/Thermophilic), pH (neutral), VFAs, Alkalinity, HRT (shorter), SRT (longer), OLR, Membrane Flux (lower than aerobic MBRs), TMP, Air Flow (for air lift/scouring), Biogas production.
-   **Control:** Temperature/pH control, membrane fouling control (backwashing, chemical cleaning, air scouring, relaxation), biogas monitoring/utilization.

AnMBRs offer high treatment efficiency and excellent effluent quality, particularly for high-strength industrial wastewater, enabling water reuse and energy recovery (biogas). Membrane fouling is a key operational challenge.
