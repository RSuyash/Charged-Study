# 1.1.2.2 Anaerobic Biological Processes

Anaerobic biological processes are part of [[1.1.2 Secondary Treatment]] used in [[1.1 Domestic (Municipal) and Industrial Waste Water Treatments|wastewater treatment]], operating in the **absence of oxygen**. They are particularly suitable for treating high-strength organic wastewater and for stabilizing sludge produced during aerobic treatment. These processes involve a consortium of [[1.2.2 Anaerobic Microorganisms|anaerobic microorganisms]] that break down organic matter, ultimately producing biogas (methane + CO₂) and stabilized sludge.

## Key Stages in Anaerobic Digestion

Anaerobic breakdown of complex organic matter occurs in sequential stages:

1.  **Hydrolysis:** Complex polymers (carbohydrates, proteins, lipids) broken down into soluble monomers (sugars, amino acids, fatty acids) by hydrolytic bacteria using extracellular enzymes.
2.  **Acidogenesis (Fermentation):** Monomers converted by acidogenic bacteria into volatile fatty acids (VFAs - acetate, propionate, butyrate), alcohols, H₂, and CO₂.
3.  **Acetogenesis:** Acetogenic bacteria convert VFAs (except acetate) and alcohols into acetate, H₂, and CO₂. Often involves [[Syntrophic Bacteria]].
4.  **Methanogenesis:** [[Methanogenic Archaea]] convert acetate (acetoclastic methanogenesis) and H₂/CO₂ (hydrogenotrophic methanogenesis) into methane (CH₄) and CO₂. This is the final, crucial step producing biogas.

## Common Anaerobic Biological Processes

-   **[[Anaerobic Digestion|Anaerobic Digesters]]:** Closed reactors for sludge stabilization and treating concentrated organic wastes. Maintain anaerobic conditions and optimal temperature (mesophilic or thermophilic). Produce biogas (renewable energy) and stabilized sludge (biosolids).
-   **[[UASB Reactors]] (Upflow Anaerobic Sludge Blanket):** High-rate reactors for soluble industrial wastewater. Wastewater flows upward through a dense granular sludge bed containing highly active anaerobic microbes. Biogas collected at top.
-   **[[Anaerobic Membrane Bioreactors (AnMBRs)]]:** Combine anaerobic digestion with membrane filtration (MF/UF) for complete biomass retention and high effluent quality. Allows high solids concentration and long SRTs.

## Operational Parameters and Control

Key factors influencing anaerobic processes:

-   **Temperature:** Mesophilic (30-40°C) or Thermophilic (50-60°C). Mesophilic is more common/stable for wastewater.
-   **pH:** Optimal near neutral (6.5-7.5) for methanogenesis. Acidification inhibits process.
-   **Volatile Fatty Acids (VFAs):** Monitor concentration; accumulation indicates imbalance.
-   **Alkalinity:** Provides buffering capacity against VFA accumulation.
-   **Hydraulic Retention Time (HRT):** Typically longer than aerobic (days to weeks).
-   **Organic Loading Rate (OLR):** Amount of organic matter fed per volume per time. Must be controlled to avoid overload.
-   **Nutrient Balance:** Lower requirements than aerobic, but trace nutrients needed.
-   **Biogas Production/Composition:** Key performance indicators (rate, CH₄ content).
-   **Absence of Oxygen:** Strict anaerobic conditions must be maintained.

Anaerobic processes offer advantages like energy recovery (biogas) and reduced sludge production compared to aerobic processes, especially for high-strength wastes.
