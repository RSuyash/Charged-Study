# 1.2.1 Aerobic Microorganisms

[[1.2 Important Microorganisms in Waste Water Treatment|Aerobic microorganisms]] require oxygen for their metabolism and are central to [[1.1.2.1 Aerobic Biological Processes|aerobic wastewater treatment]]. They oxidize organic pollutants, converting them into CO₂, water, and new biomass.

Key groups include:

-   **[[Bacteria (Aerobic Wastewater Treatment)]]:** The primary workhorses.
    -   *Heterotrophic Bacteria:* Degrade a wide range of organic compounds (e.g., *Pseudomonas*, *Zoogloea*, *Bacillus*, *Acinetobacter*). *Zoogloea* are important floc-formers in [[Activated Sludge Process (ASP)|ASP]]. *Acinetobacter* can be involved in phosphorus removal.
    -   *Nitrifying Bacteria:* Convert ammonia to nitrate (nitrification).
        -   *Ammonia-Oxidizing Bacteria (AOB):* e.g., *Nitrosomonas* (NH₃ → NO₂⁻).
        -   *Nitrite-Oxidizing Bacteria (NOB):* e.g., *Nitrobacter* (NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻).
-   **[[Fungi (Aerobic Wastewater Treatment)]]:** Can degrade complex organic matter.
    -   *Filamentous Fungi:* e.g., *Trichoderma*, *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*. Degrade cellulose, lignin, complex organics. Can be important in treating industrial wastes.
    -   *Yeasts:* e.g., *Geotrichum*. Can degrade hydrocarbons, phenols. Found in activated sludge.
-   **Protozoa and Metazoa:** Higher organisms that graze on bacteria, contributing to effluent clarification and sludge settling. Examples include amoebas, ciliates (e.g., *Vorticella*), flagellates, and rotifers. Their presence often indicates a healthy, stable aerobic treatment process.

The specific community composition depends on wastewater characteristics and operating conditions (e.g., SRT, F/M ratio, DO).
