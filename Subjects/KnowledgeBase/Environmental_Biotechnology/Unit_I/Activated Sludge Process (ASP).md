# Activated Sludge Process (ASP)

The Activated Sludge Process (ASP), developed around 1914, is the most widely used [[1.3 Aerobic Biological Treatments|aerobic biological treatment]] method for [[1.1 Domestic (Municipal) and Industrial Waste Water Treatments|wastewater treatment]] globally. It's a core part of [[1.1.2 Secondary Treatment]].

![Fig. Activated Sludge Process](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-I/Unit I 1a53a995df8780f69720e68a76b33240/image 6.png>)
*Fig. Activated Sludge Process*

## [[1.3.1.1 Principles and Process Design (ASP)]]

-   **Principles:** Relies on a mixed community of [[1.2.1 Aerobic Microorganisms|aerobic microorganisms]] (activated sludge) suspended in wastewater to oxidize organic pollutants. Key features include floc formation, sludge recirculation (RAS), and aeration.
-   **Process Design:** Consists of an aeration basin (where biological oxidation occurs) followed by a secondary sedimentation tank (clarifier) for separating biomass from treated effluent. Includes RAS and WAS (Waste Activated Sludge) systems. Various configurations exist (plug flow, complete mix, SBR, oxidation ditch).

## [[1.3.1.2 Operational Parameters and Control (ASP)]]

-   **Key Parameters:** Dissolved Oxygen (DO), Nutrient Balance (N:P), pH, Temperature, Sludge Volume Index (SVI), Solids Retention Time (SRT)/Mean Cell Residence Time (MCRT), Food-to-Microorganism Ratio (F/M), RAS Rate, WAS Rate.
-   **Control:** Requires careful monitoring and control of these parameters using aeration adjustments, chemical dosing, sludge wasting rates, and process monitoring systems (SCADA) to maintain efficiency and stability. Microscopic examination helps assess sludge health.

ASP is a robust and effective process for removing [[BOD]] and suspended solids but requires careful operational control.
