# 1.2.2 Anaerobic Microorganisms

[[1.2 Important Microorganisms in Waste Water Treatment|Anaerobic microorganisms]] thrive in the absence of oxygen and are essential for [[1.1.2.2 Anaerobic Biological Processes|anaerobic wastewater treatment]] and [[Anaerobic Digestion|digestion]]. They break down complex organic matter through a series of interdependent biochemical reactions, ultimately producing [[Biogas]].

Key groups involved in the anaerobic food chain:

1.  **Hydrolytic Bacteria:** Secrete extracellular enzymes (cellulases, proteases, lipases) to break down complex polymers (carbohydrates, proteins, lipids) into soluble monomers (sugars, amino acids, fatty acids). Examples: *Clostridium*, *Bacteroides*, *Streptococcus*.
2.  **Acidogenic Bacteria (Fermenters):** Convert monomers into a mixture of volatile fatty acids (VFAs - acetate, propionate, butyrate), alcohols, H₂, and CO₂ through [[Fermentative Pathways|fermentation]]. Examples: *Escherichia*, *Enterobacter*, *Lactobacillus*.
3.  **Acetogenic Bacteria:** Convert VFAs (except acetate) and alcohols into acetate, H₂, and CO₂. Often operate in syntrophy with methanogens. Examples: *Syntrophobacter*, *Syntrophomonas*.
4.  **[[Methanogenic Archaea]]:** Strictly anaerobic archaea responsible for the final step, [[Methanogenesis]]. Convert acetate and H₂/CO₂ into methane (CH₄) and CO₂.
    -   *Acetoclastic Methanogens:* Convert acetate → CH₄ + CO₂ (e.g., *Methanosarcina*, *Methanosaeta*).
    -   *Hydrogenotrophic Methanogens:* Convert H₂ + CO₂ → CH₄ + H₂O (e.g., *Methanobacterium*, *Methanococcus*). Crucial for consuming H₂ produced by acetogens ([[Syntrophic Bacteria]]).
5.  **[[Fungi (Anaerobic Wastewater Treatment)]]:** Less dominant than bacteria/archaea, but some yeasts (*Saccharomyces*, *Pichia*) can ferment sugars, and specialized anaerobic fungi (Neocallimastigomycota) are potent degraders of plant biomass (cellulose).

This complex consortium works together, often in syntrophic relationships, to achieve the complete breakdown of organic matter under anaerobic conditions.
