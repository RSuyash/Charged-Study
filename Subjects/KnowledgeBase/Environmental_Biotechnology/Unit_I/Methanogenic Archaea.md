# Methanogenic Archaea

Methanogenic Archaea (or Methanogens) are a unique group of microorganisms belonging to the domain Archaea, distinct from [[Bacteria (Anaerobic Wastewater Treatment)|Bacteria]]. They are strictly anaerobic and perform the final, crucial step of [[1.1.2.2 Anaerobic Biological Processes|anaerobic digestion]] and organic matter breakdown: **[[Methanogenesis]]**.

## Role in Anaerobic Digestion

Methanogens convert simple substrates, produced by hydrolytic, acidogenic, and acetogenic bacteria, into methane (CH₄) and carbon dioxide (CO₂), the main components of [[Biogas]].

## Key Methanogenic Pathways

1.  **Acetoclastic Methanogenesis:** Cleavage of acetate to CH₄ and CO₂.
    `CH₃COOH → CH₄ + CO₂`
    -   *Organisms:* *Methanosarcina*, *Methanosaeta* (*Methanothrix*). *Methanosaeta* dominate at low acetate concentrations due to higher affinity; *Methanosarcina* dominate at higher acetate concentrations and are more versatile.
2.  **Hydrogenotrophic Methanogenesis:** Reduction of CO₂ using hydrogen (H₂) as the electron donor.
    `CO₂ + 4 H₂ → CH₄ + 2 H₂O`
    -   *Organisms:* *Methanobacterium*, *Methanococcus*, *Methanospirillum*.
    -   *Syntrophy:* Crucial for consuming H₂ produced by acetogenic and [[Syntrophic Bacteria]], keeping H₂ partial pressure low and making upstream reactions (like VFA oxidation) thermodynamically favorable.
3.  **Methylotrophic Methanogenesis:** Utilization of methylated compounds (methanol, methylamines).
    -   *Organisms:* Some *Methanosarcina* species.

## Importance

-   **Terminal Step:** Responsible for the final conversion of organic matter to biogas in anaerobic environments.
-   **Syntrophy:** Essential partners for acetogenic and syntrophic bacteria by consuming H₂.
-   **Biogas Production:** Generate methane, a valuable renewable energy source.
-   **Sensitivity:** Methanogens are generally slow-growing and sensitive to environmental conditions like pH changes (optimal 6.5-7.5), temperature fluctuations, and toxic substances (e.g., high VFA levels, ammonia, sulfides). Maintaining stable conditions is vital for efficient anaerobic digestion.
