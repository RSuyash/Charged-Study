# 1.1.2 Secondary Treatment

Secondary treatment follows [[1.1.1 Primary Treatment]] in [[1.1 Domestic (Municipal) and Industrial Waste Water Treatments|wastewater treatment]]. Its primary goal is to remove biodegradable organic matter (measured as [[BOD]] and [[COD]]) and suspended solids remaining after primary treatment. This stage predominantly utilizes **biological processes**, harnessing microorganisms to break down pollutants.

![Fig. Secondary Treatment Process](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-I/Unit I 1a53a995df8780f69720e68a76b33240/image 3.png>)
*Fig. Secondary Treatment Process*

Secondary treatment methods are broadly classified based on oxygen requirements:

-   [[1.1.2.1 Aerobic Biological Processes]]: Require oxygen (e.g., [[Activated Sludge Process (ASP)]], [[Trickling Filters (TFs)]], [[Rotating Biological Contactors (RBCs)]], [[Aerated Lagoons]]).
-   [[1.1.2.2 Anaerobic Biological Processes]]: Occur in the absence of oxygen (e.g., [[Anaerobic Digestion]], [[UASB Reactors]], [[Anaerobic Membrane Bioreactors (AnMBRs)]]). Anaerobic processes are often used for high-strength wastewater or sludge treatment.

After biological treatment, a secondary clarification step (sedimentation) is typically required to separate the microbial biomass (secondary sludge) from the treated effluent.
