# 1.3.1.1 Principles and Process Design (ASP)

This note details the core principles and design components of the [[Activated Sludge Process (ASP)]].

## Principles of ASP

-   **Biological Oxidation:** Utilizes a mixed community of [[1.2.1 Aerobic Microorganisms]] (activated sludge) to aerobically degrade organic pollutants ([[BOD]], [[COD]]).
-   **Floc Formation:** Microorganisms aggregate into flocs (aided by EPS - extracellular polymeric substances), which are essential for settling and biomass separation.
-   **Suspended Growth:** Microorganisms are kept suspended in the wastewater within the aeration basin.
-   **Sludge Recirculation (RAS):** Settled activated sludge from the secondary clarifier is recycled back to the aeration basin (Return Activated Sludge - RAS) to maintain high microbial concentration (MLSS) for efficient treatment.
-   **Aeration:** Oxygen supplied (diffused air or surface aerators) to maintain aerobic conditions for microbial respiration.

## Process Design Components

1.  **Aeration Basin (Reactor):** Where biological oxidation occurs.
    *   *Configurations:*
        *   **Conventional Plug Flow:** Long, narrow basin; substrate concentration gradient.
        *   **Complete Mix (CSTR):** Rapid mixing; uniform substrate concentration.
        *   **Step Feed:** Influent added at multiple points.
        *   **Sequencing Batch Reactor (SBR):** Fill-and-draw system; all steps (fill, react, settle, decant, idle) in one tank sequentially. Flexible, good for smaller plants/variable flows.
        *   **Oxidation Ditch:** Continuous loop reactor, often with extended aeration times. Good for nutrient removal.
    *   *Aeration Systems:*
        *   **Diffused Air:** Air pumped through diffusers (fine or coarse bubble) at basin bottom.
        *   **Surface Aerators:** Mechanical devices agitating surface (vertical turbines, horizontal paddles).

2.  **Secondary Sedimentation Tank (Clarifier):** Separates activated sludge biomass from treated effluent via gravity settling.
    *   *Design:* Rectangular or circular, designed for quiescent conditions.
    *   *Sludge Collection:* Settled sludge collected (scrapers) and split into RAS and WAS.

3.  **Return Activated Sludge (RAS) System:** Pumps/piping to recycle sludge back to the aeration basin. RAS flow rate is a key control parameter.

4.  **Waste Activated Sludge (WAS) System:** System to remove excess biomass generated during treatment. WAS rate controls SRT/MCRT. WAS sent for sludge treatment.

## Key Design Parameters

-   **Hydraulic Retention Time (HRT):** Time wastewater spends in aeration basin (e.g., 4-8 hrs conventional, 18-36 hrs extended aeration).
-   **Solids Retention Time (SRT) / Mean Cell Residence Time (MCRT):** Average time biomass stays in the system (e.g., 5-15 days conventional, 20-30 days extended aeration). Critical for biomass concentration, sludge production, nitrification. Controlled by WAS rate.
-   **Food-to-Microorganism Ratio (F/M):** Organic load (BOD) relative to biomass (MLVSS) (e.g., 0.2-0.4 kg BOD/kg MLVSS·d conventional). Affects sludge settling and effluent quality.
-   **Mixed Liquor Suspended Solids (MLSS):** Biomass concentration in aeration basin (e.g., 2000-4000 mg/L conventional). Maintained by RAS rate.
-   **Mixed Liquor Volatile Suspended Solids (MLVSS):** Organic fraction of MLSS, represents active biomass (MLVSS/MLSS ≈ 0.7-0.8).
-   **Aeration Rate:** Based on oxygen demand (wastewater strength, biomass concentration) and target DO level.

Proper design considering these parameters is crucial for achieving efficient and stable ASP operation.
