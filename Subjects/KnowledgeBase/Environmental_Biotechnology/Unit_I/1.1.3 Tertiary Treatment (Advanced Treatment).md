# 1.1.3 Tertiary Treatment (Advanced Treatment)

Tertiary treatment, or advanced wastewater treatment, is the final stage following [[1.1.2 Secondary Treatment]]. It aims to remove specific pollutants not effectively removed previously, such as residual suspended solids, nutrients (nitrogen, phosphorus), pathogens, and refractory organic compounds. Tertiary treatment is often required to meet stringent effluent quality standards for discharge into sensitive environments or for water reuse applications.

Key tertiary treatment processes include:

-   **Nutrient Removal:** Processes specifically designed to remove nitrogen (nitrification/denitrification) and phosphorus (chemical precipitation, enhanced biological phosphorus removal - EBPR).
-   **[[1.1.3.1 Advanced Oxidation Processes (AOPs)]]:** Chemical processes using highly reactive radicals (e.g., hydroxyl radicals) to degrade refractory organic pollutants.
-   **[[1.1.3.2 Membrane Filtration]]:** Physical separation using membranes (MF, UF, NF, RO) to remove fine suspended solids, microorganisms, and dissolved substances.
-   **[[1.1.3.3 Disinfection]]:** Inactivation or killing of pathogenic microorganisms (bacteria, viruses, protozoa) using methods like chlorination, UV irradiation, or ozonation.
-   **Activated Carbon Adsorption:** Removal of dissolved organic compounds and trace pollutants by adsorption onto activated carbon.

The specific combination of tertiary treatment processes depends on the effluent quality requirements and the pollutants targeted for removal.
