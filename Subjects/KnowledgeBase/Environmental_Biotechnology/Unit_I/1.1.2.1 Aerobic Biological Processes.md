# 1.1.2.1 Aerobic Biological Processes

Aerobic biological processes are a core part of [[1.1.2 Secondary Treatment]] in [[1.1 Domestic (Municipal) and Industrial Waste Water Treatments|wastewater treatment]]. They utilize [[1.2.1 Aerobic Microorganisms|aerobic microorganisms]] (primarily bacteria) that require oxygen to consume and oxidize biodegradable organic pollutants, significantly reducing Biochemical Oxygen Demand ([[BOD]]) and Chemical Oxygen Demand ([[COD]]).

## Principle

Microorganisms use dissolved oxygen (DO) as an electron acceptor to metabolize organic matter, converting it into carbon dioxide, water, and new microbial biomass (sludge).

## Common Aerobic Biological Processes

-   **[[Activated Sludge Process (ASP)]]:** Most widely used. Wastewater mixed with microbial culture (activated sludge) in an aerated basin. Biomass settled in a secondary clarifier, part recycled (RAS), excess wasted (WAS). Variations include conventional, extended aeration, SBR, oxidation ditch, MBR.
-   **[[Trickling Filters (TFs)]]:** Wastewater trickles over media (rock, plastic) covered with microbial biofilm. Air circulates, providing oxygen. Older technology, still used.
-   **[[Rotating Biological Contactors (RBCs)]]:** Rotating discs partially submerged in wastewater develop biofilm. Rotation exposes biofilm alternately to wastewater (substrate uptake) and air (oxygen).
-   **[[Aerated Lagoons]]:** Large, shallow basins aerated artificially (surface or diffused air). Simpler, less energy-intensive than ASP, but require more land and may be less efficient.

## Operational Parameters and Control

Key factors influencing aerobic processes:

-   **Dissolved Oxygen (DO):** Crucial, typically maintained at 2-4 mg/L via aeration control.
-   **Nutrient Balance:** Microbes need N & P (BOD:N:P ≈ 100:5:1). Supplementation may be needed.
-   **pH:** Optimal near neutral (6.5-7.5). Adjusted if necessary.
-   **Temperature:** Affects microbial activity (optimal usually 20-40°C).
-   **Sludge Retention Time (SRT) / Mean Cell Residence Time (MCRT):** Average time microbes stay in the system. Controls biomass concentration and treatment efficiency (esp. in ASP).
-   **Food-to-Microorganism Ratio (F/M):** Ratio of organic load (BOD) to biomass (MLVSS). Affects growth rate and sludge production.
-   **Hydraulic Retention Time (HRT):** Average time wastewater stays in the treatment unit.

Effective operation requires monitoring and control of these parameters to maintain a healthy microbial population and achieve desired effluent quality.
