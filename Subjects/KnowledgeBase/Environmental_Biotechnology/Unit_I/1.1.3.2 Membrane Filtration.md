# 1.1.3.2 Membrane Filtration

Membrane filtration processes are pressure-driven separation techniques used in [[1.1.3 Tertiary Treatment (Advanced Treatment)|tertiary wastewater treatment]]. They employ semi-permeable membranes to physically separate pollutants from water based primarily on size exclusion.

## Principle

Pressure forces water (permeate) through a membrane while retaining pollutants (retentate) larger than the membrane's pores.

## Common Membrane Filtration Processes

Ordered by decreasing pore size / increasing resolution:

1.  **Microfiltration (MF):**
    *   **Pore Size:** 0.1 - 10 µm.
    *   **Removes:** Suspended solids, bacteria, protozoa.
    *   **Applications:** Pre-treatment for NF/RO, clarification, bacteria removal.

2.  **Ultrafiltration (UF):**
    *   **Pore Size:** 0.01 - 0.1 µm.
    *   **Removes:** Viruses, colloids, macromolecules (proteins), plus MF removals.
    *   **Applications:** Pre-treatment for RO, virus removal, protein concentration/purification ([[Ultrafiltration (UF)|UF in DSP]]), clarification.

3.  **Nanofiltration (NF):**
    *   **Pore Size:** 0.001 - 0.01 µm.
    *   **Removes:** Multivalent ions (hardness - Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺; sulfates), some dissolved organics, viruses, plus UF removals.
    *   **Applications:** Water softening, color removal, removal of specific dissolved pollutants.

4.  **Reverse Osmosis (RO):**
    *   **Pore Size:** < 0.001 µm (essentially non-porous).
    *   **Removes:** Virtually all dissolved salts, minerals, small organic molecules, plus NF removals.
    *   **Mechanism:** Requires high pressure to overcome osmotic pressure; separation via size exclusion and solution-diffusion.
    *   **Applications:** Desalination, water reuse, producing high-purity water (e.g., [[Water for Injection (WFI)|WFI]]), removal of salts/micropollutants. See [[Reverse Osmosis (RO)|RO in DSP]].

## Process Design and Operation

-   **Membrane Type/Material:** Polymeric (e.g., polysulfone, polyamide), ceramic.
-   **Configurations:** Plate-and-frame, spiral-wound, hollow fiber.
-   **Transmembrane Pressure (TMP):** Driving force; higher TMP increases flux but also fouling.
-   **Flux:** Permeate flow rate per unit membrane area (L/m²·h). Affected by TMP, fouling, feed characteristics.
-   **Recovery Rate:** % of feed recovered as permeate. Higher recovery = less waste, but higher concentrate concentration and fouling potential.
-   **Fouling Control:** Major challenge. Accumulation of material on membrane surface reduces flux/efficiency. Strategies include:
    *   *Pre-treatment:* Remove solids/foulants before membrane.
    *   *Backwashing:* Reverse flow to dislodge foulants.
    *   *Chemical Cleaning:* Use chemicals (acid, base, detergents, enzymes) to remove fouling layers.
    *   *Air Scouring:* Air bubbles scrub membrane surface.

Membrane filtration provides high-quality effluent suitable for reuse or discharge into sensitive waters.
