# 1.2 Important Microorganisms in Waste Water Treatment

Microorganisms are the essential biological agents driving the breakdown and removal of pollutants in [[1.1 Domestic (Municipal) and Industrial Waste Water Treatments|wastewater treatment]] processes, particularly in [[1.1.2 Secondary Treatment]]. Different types thrive under aerobic or anaerobic conditions, playing specific roles.

Key groups include:

-   [[1.2.1 Aerobic Microorganisms]]
    -   [[Bacteria (Aerobic Wastewater Treatment)]] (e.g., *Pseudomonas*, *Nitrosomonas*, *Nitrobacter*, *Acinetobacter*, *Zoogloea*, *Bacillus*)
    -   [[Fungi (Aerobic Wastewater Treatment)]] (e.g., *Trichoderma*, *Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Geotrichum*)
-   [[1.2.2 Anaerobic Microorganisms]]
    -   [[Bacteria (Anaerobic Wastewater Treatment)]] (e.g., Hydrolytic, Acidogenic, Acetogenic, [[Syntrophic Bacteria]])
    -   [[Methanogenic Archaea]] (e.g., *Methanosarcina*, *Methanosaeta*, *Methanobacterium*)
    -   [[Fungi (Anaerobic Wastewater Treatment)]] (e.g., *Saccharomyces*, *Pichia*, Anaerobic Fungi - Neocallimastigomycota)

Understanding the roles and requirements of these microbial communities is crucial for designing and operating effective biological wastewater treatment systems. Their growth and metabolic activities are influenced by various factors, including [[1.2.3 Principles of Growth and Plasmid-Borne Metabolic Activities]].
