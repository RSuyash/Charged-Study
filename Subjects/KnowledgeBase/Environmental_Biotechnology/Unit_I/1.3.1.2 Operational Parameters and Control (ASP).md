# 1.3.1.2 Operational Parameters and Control (ASP)

Effective operation of the [[Activated Sludge Process (ASP)]] requires careful monitoring and control of several key parameters to maintain process stability and achieve desired effluent quality.

## Key Operational Parameters

-   **Dissolved Oxygen (DO):**
    -   *Target:* Typically 2-4 mg/L in the aeration basin for aerobic treatment.
    -   *Control:* Adjust aeration rates (air flow, aerator speed). Monitored using DO probes.
    -   *Importance:* Essential for microbial respiration and [[BOD]] removal. Low DO leads to poor treatment, potential bulking; high DO wastes energy.
-   **Nutrient Balance (N:P Ratio):**
    -   *Target:* Approximate BOD:N:P ratio of 100:5:1 needed for microbial growth.
    -   *Control:* Monitor influent/effluent nutrients. Supplement N or P if deficient (common in some industrial wastes).
    -   *Importance:* Nutrient limitation hinders microbial activity and treatment efficiency.
-   **pH:**
    -   *Target:* Near neutral range (6.5-7.5) for optimal microbial activity.
    *   *Control:* Monitor pH. Adjust using chemicals (lime, acid) if influent pH is extreme or biological processes (like nitrification) cause shifts.
-   **Temperature:**
    *   *Target:* Optimal usually in mesophilic range (20-40°C).
    *   *Control:* Typically not actively controlled in municipal plants, but monitored. Extreme temperatures affect microbial activity and sludge settling.
-   **Sludge Volume Index (SVI):**
    *   *Definition:* Volume (mL) occupied by 1 gram of activated sludge after 30 min settling. Measures sludge settleability.
    *   *Target:* Low SVI (<80-100 mL/g) indicates good settling. High SVI (>150 mL/g) indicates poor settling (**sludge bulking**), often caused by filamentous bacteria overgrowth.
    *   *Control:* Monitored regularly. Control strategies for bulking involve adjusting DO, F/M ratio, SRT, or adding selectors/chemicals.
-   **Solids Retention Time (SRT) / Mean Cell Residence Time (MCRT):**
    *   *Definition:* Average time biomass solids remain in the system.
    *   *Control:* Primarily controlled by the Waste Activated Sludge (WAS) flow rate. `SRT = (Mass of solids in system) / (Mass of solids wasted per day)`.
    *   *Importance:* Critical parameter affecting biomass concentration, sludge age distribution, nitrification potential (longer SRT needed), and sludge production (longer SRT = less sludge).
-   **Food-to-Microorganism Ratio (F/M):**
    *   *Definition:* Ratio of incoming organic load (BOD) to the amount of active biomass (MLVSS) in the aeration basin. `F/M = (Influent BOD * Flow Rate) / (MLVSS * Aeration Basin Volume)`.
    *   *Control:* Adjusted indirectly via SRT/WAS rate or by managing influent load.
    *   *Importance:* Affects microbial growth rate, sludge characteristics (settling), and effluent quality. Optimal range depends on ASP configuration (e.g., 0.2-0.4 for conventional).
-   **Return Activated Sludge (RAS) Rate:**
    *   *Definition:* Flow rate of sludge recycled from secondary clarifier to aeration basin.
    *   *Control:* Adjusted via RAS pumps.
    *   *Importance:* Maintains desired Mixed Liquor Suspended Solids ([[MLSS]]) concentration in aeration basin, influences clarifier sludge blanket level.
-   **Waste Activated Sludge (WAS) Rate:**
    *   *Definition:* Flow rate of excess sludge removed from the system.
    *   *Control:* Adjusted via WAS pumps. Primary control for SRT/MCRT and F/M.
-   **Microscopic Examination:**
    *   *Purpose:* Assess health/composition of microbial community (floc structure, presence/type of filamentous bacteria, protozoa, rotifers).
    *   *Importance:* Indicator of process health. Filamentous bulking, pin floc, or absence of higher life forms can indicate operational problems.

## Control Strategies

-   **Automated Control:** Use of sensors (DO, pH, nutrient analyzers) linked to control systems (PLCs, SCADA) to automatically adjust aeration, chemical dosing, pump rates.
-   **Monitoring:** Regular sampling and analysis of influent, aeration basin (MLSS, MLVSS, DO, SVI), secondary clarifier (sludge blanket, effluent TSS, BOD), and WAS/RAS streams.
-   **Troubleshooting:** Operators need expertise to diagnose and correct common issues like sludge bulking, foaming, poor settling, nitrification problems, based on monitoring data and microscopic examination.

Effective control of these parameters is key to maintaining a stable and efficient Activated Sludge Process.
