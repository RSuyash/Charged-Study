# 1.3.2.2 Operational Parameters and Control (RBCs)

Effective operation of [[Rotating Biological Contactors (RBCs)]] involves monitoring and controlling key parameters to ensure stable performance and efficient wastewater treatment.

## Key Operational Parameters

-   **Rotation Speed:**
    -   *Target:* Typically 1-2 rpm.
    *   *Control:* Maintain within design range via drive system. Adjustments may optimize biofilm growth/sloughing.
    *   *Importance:* Affects oxygen transfer, substrate uptake time, shear forces on biofilm.
-   **Hydraulic Loading Rate (HLR):**
    *   *Definition:* Wastewater flow rate per unit media surface area.
    *   *Control:* Monitor influent flow, adjust if needed to stay within design HLR (e.g., 0.02-0.08 m³/m²·d). Flow equalization upstream helps maintain consistency.
    *   *Importance:* Overloading reduces treatment efficiency.
-   **Organic Loading Rate (OLR):**
    *   *Definition:* Mass of [[BOD]] applied per unit media surface area per time.
    *   *Control:* Monitor influent BOD and flow. Adjustments may be needed if OLR exceeds design (e.g., 5-20 g BOD/m²·d).
    *   *Importance:* Key parameter influencing treatment efficiency.
-   **Dissolved Oxygen (DO):**
    *   *Monitoring:* DO in the bulk liquid can be monitored.
    *   *Control:* Primarily controlled by atmospheric exposure during rotation. Ensure adequate ventilation if covered. Low DO can indicate overloading or insufficient rotation/exposure.
-   **Biofilm Thickness and Condition:**
    *   *Monitoring:* Regular visual inspection. Healthy biofilm is typically brownish, uniform thickness.
    *   *Control:* Excessive growth can lead to clogging, reduced O₂ transfer, shaft stress. Sloughing is natural; ensure secondary clarifier removes sloughed solids effectively. Rotation speed can influence sloughing.
-   **Temperature:**
    *   *Monitoring:* Monitor wastewater temperature.
    *   *Importance:* Affects microbial activity. Performance decreases at low temperatures. Covers can help mitigate temperature drops.
-   **pH:**
    *   *Monitoring:* Monitor influent/effluent pH.
    *   *Importance:* Maintain near neutral (6.5-7.5) for optimal microbial activity. Usually less critical to control than in [[Activated Sludge Process (ASP)|ASP]] unless influent pH is extreme.
-   **Nutrient Balance:**
    *   *Monitoring:* Check influent N:P ratio if necessary.
    *   *Importance:* Ensure adequate nutrients for biofilm growth. Usually sufficient in municipal wastewater.

## Control Strategies

-   **Rotation Speed Control:** Maintain constant speed or adjust based on monitoring.
-   **Flow Control:** Use flow equalization if influent flow varies significantly.
-   **Biofilm Monitoring:** Regular visual checks.
-   **Sloughed Biomass Removal:** Ensure efficient secondary clarifier operation.
-   **Maintenance:** Regular checks/maintenance of drive system, bearings, discs, clarifier.
-   **Troubleshooting:** Address issues like excessive biofilm, clogging, shaft problems, reduced efficiency.

RBCs are relatively simple to operate compared to ASP, with primary control focused on rotation speed and managing influent loading rates.
