# Fungi (Aerobic Wastewater Treatment)

Fungi, including yeasts and filamentous fungi, are [[1.2.1 Aerobic Microorganisms]] that can play a role in [[1.1.2.1 Aerobic Biological Processes|aerobic wastewater treatment]], although [[Bacteria (Aerobic Wastewater Treatment)|bacteria]] are typically the dominant players in conventional systems like [[Activated Sludge Process (ASP)|ASP]]. Fungi possess unique enzymatic capabilities that allow them to degrade complex organic matter.

## Key Roles and Examples

-   **Filamentous Fungi:**
    -   **Role:** Known for their ability to secrete a wide range of extracellular enzymes (hydrolases, oxidases) capable of degrading complex, often recalcitrant organic polymers like cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin, and certain industrial pollutants. They can tolerate lower pH and nutrient-limited conditions better than some bacteria.
    -   **Examples:**
        -   ***Trichoderma*:** Degrades cellulose and other complex carbohydrates. Can be involved in breaking down plant-based materials. Some species used in bioremediation.
        -   ***Aspergillus*:** Metabolically versatile, degrades diverse substrates (complex carbohydrates, proteins, some recalcitrant compounds). Important in treating some industrial wastewaters. *Aspergillus niger* is used industrially for enzyme production.
        -   ***Penicillium*:** Some species degrade organic pollutants.
        -   *White-Rot Fungi* (e.g., *Phanerochaete chrysosporium*): Produce powerful lignin-degrading enzymes (lignin peroxidases, manganese peroxidases) that can also degrade a wide range of recalcitrant pollutants like PAHs, PCBs, and dyes. Often studied for specialized bioremediation applications.

-   **Yeasts:**
    -   **Role:** Unicellular fungi that primarily ferment sugars but can also grow aerobically. Some yeasts can degrade specific organic pollutants.
    -   **Examples:**
        -   ***Geotrichum*:** Yeast-like fungi capable of degrading hydrocarbons and phenolic compounds. Found in activated sludge.
        -   *Candida*, *Rhodotorula*: Other yeasts sometimes found in wastewater systems, potentially contributing to organic matter removal.

## Considerations in Wastewater Treatment

-   **Growth Conditions:** Fungi often prefer slightly acidic pH conditions compared to bacteria.
-   **Filamentous Growth:** The filamentous growth habit of molds can sometimes cause problems in ASP, contributing to sludge bulking (poor settling) if they become dominant. However, in biofilm systems like [[Trickling Filters (TFs)]], fungal growth can be beneficial.
-   **Enzyme Production:** Fungi are excellent producers of extracellular enzymes, making them valuable for treating wastewaters containing complex polymers or recalcitrant compounds.
-   **Bioremediation:** Specific fungal strains are often explored for targeted bioremediation of industrial pollutants that are resistant to bacterial degradation.

While not always the dominant group in municipal wastewater treatment, fungi contribute significantly to the overall biodegradation capacity, especially for complex organic matter and in specific industrial wastewater scenarios.
