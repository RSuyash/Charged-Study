# Rotating Biological Contactors (RBCs)

Rotating Biological Contactors (RBCs) are an [[1.3 Aerobic Biological Treatments|aerobic biological treatment]] method used in [[1.1.2 Secondary Treatment|secondary wastewater treatment]]. Developed initially in the early 1900s with significant advancements in the mid-20th century, RBCs utilize a biofilm growing on rotating discs.

## [[1.3.2.1 Principles and Process Design (RBCs)]]

-   **Principles:** Biofilm-based, attached growth system. Microorganisms grow on rotating discs partially submerged in wastewater. Rotation provides alternating exposure to wastewater (substrate uptake) and air (oxygen uptake). Excess biofilm sloughs off and is removed in a secondary clarifier.
-   **Process Design:** Consists of closely spaced discs (plastic) on a horizontal shaft rotating slowly (1-2 rpm) within a tank. Often designed in multiple stages. Requires primary clarification upstream and secondary clarification downstream.

## [[1.3.2.2 Operational Parameters and Control (RBCs)]]

-   **Key Parameters:** Rotation Speed, Hydraulic Loading Rate (HLR), Organic Loading Rate (OLR), Biofilm Thickness/Condition, Temperature, pH.
-   **Control:** Primarily involves controlling rotation speed and influent flow (HLR/OLR). Monitoring biofilm condition and secondary clarifier performance is important.

RBCs offer advantages like simple operation and resistance to shock loads but can be susceptible to issues like shaft failure or media damage if not properly maintained.
