# 4.3.1.1 Structure and Function (CYP450)

This note details the structural features and enzymatic function of the [[4.3.1 Cytochrome P450 System|Cytochrome P450 (CYP450)]] enzymes.

## Structure of CYP450 Enzymes

-   **Heme Prosthetic Group:** Iron-containing porphyrin ring. Site of oxygen activation and substrate oxidation. Iron coordinated by a cysteine thiolate ligand (defining feature).
-   **Protein Structure:** Membrane-bound proteins (ER in eukaryotes, plasma membrane in prokaryotes). Single polypeptide chains (~45-60 kDa).
-   **Active Site:** Near the heme iron. Variable structure among isoforms allows for broad substrate range.
-   **Reductase Partner:** Eukaryotes need NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductase (CPR) to transfer electrons. Prokaryotes use various reductase systems (ferredoxins, flavodoxins).

## Function (Monooxygenase Activity)

CYP450s are **monooxygenases** (mixed-function oxidases). They catalyze:

`Substrate (RH) + O₂ + NADPH + H⁺ → Product (ROH) + H₂O + NADP⁺`

**Key Functional Aspects:**

-   **Broad Substrate Specificity:** Metabolize diverse endogenous and [[Xenobiotics]].
-   **[[4.3.2 Phase I Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Oxidation Reactions]]:** Hydroxylation, epoxidation, dealkylation, oxidation.
-   **Role in Drug Metabolism:** Major enzymes in Phase I drug metabolism (inactivation, activation, toxic metabolites).
-   **Role in Endogenous Metabolism:** Steroid hormone synthesis, fatty acid metabolism, bile acid synthesis.
