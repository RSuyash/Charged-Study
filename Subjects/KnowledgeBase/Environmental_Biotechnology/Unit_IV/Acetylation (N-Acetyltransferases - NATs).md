# Acetylation (N-Acetyltransferases - NATs)

Acetylation is a [[4.3.3 Phase II Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase II conjugation pathway]] catalyzed by N-acetyltransferases (NATs). It involves the transfer of an acetyl group (-COCH₃) from acetyl-CoA to an amino group (-NH₂) on a [[Xenobiotics|xenobiotic]] or [[4.3.2 Phase I Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase I metabolite]].

## Key Features

-   **Enzymes:** N-Acetyltransferases (NATs) are cytosolic enzymes. Humans have NAT1 and NAT2 isoforms, with genetic polymorphisms affecting activity.
-   **Conjugating Agent:** Acetyl-CoA is the activated acetyl donor.
-   **Functional Groups Targeted:** Primarily aromatic amines, hydrazines, sulfonamides.
-   **Conjugate Properties:** Acetylation can affect water solubility and biological activity.
-   **Reaction:**
    `Xenobiotic-NH₂ + Acetyl-CoA --[NAT]--> Xenobiotic-NH-COCH₃ + CoA`
-   **Examples:** Metabolism of some drugs (isoniazid, sulfonamides).
-   **Polymorphism:** NAT enzymes are polymorphic, leading to "slow acetylators" and "rapid acetylators" with different drug responses.

Acetylation is not always a detoxification pathway; it can sometimes activate compounds or alter their properties.
