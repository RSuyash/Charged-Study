# 4.3.1.2 Induction and Regulation of Cytochrome P450 System

The expression and activity of [[4.3.1 Cytochrome P450 System|CYP450 enzymes]] are tightly regulated and can be induced or inhibited by various factors. This regulation is crucial for maintaining homeostasis and modulating responses to [[Xenobiotics]] and drugs.

## Induction of CYP450 Enzymes

-   **Xenobiotic Induction:** Exposure to drugs, pollutants, dietary components can induce specific CYP450 isoforms. This enhances metabolism of xenobiotics.
-   **Transcriptional Regulation:** Primarily occurs at the transcriptional level (increased gene transcription/mRNA synthesis).
-   **Nuclear Receptors:** Induction is often mediated by ligand-activated nuclear receptors:
    -   **AhR (Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor):** Activated by PAHs, dioxins, PCBs. Induces CYP1A1, CYP1A2, CYP1B1 (PAH/dioxin metabolism).
    -   **CAR (Constitutive Androstane Receptor):** Activated by phenobarbital, steroids, drugs. Induces CYP2B6, CYP2C isoforms (drug metabolism/detoxification).
    -   **PXR (Pregnane X Receptor) / SXR (Steroid and Xenobiotic Receptor):** Activated by diverse xenobiotics, steroids, bile acids. Induces CYP3A4 (most abundant in human liver), CYP2C isoforms, and transporters.
    -   **PPARs (Peroxisome Proliferator-Activated Receptors):** Activated by fatty acids, lipids, drugs. Regulate fatty acid metabolism, glucose homeostasis, inflammation, and some CYP450s.
-   **Mechanism (Simplified Example - AhR):**
    1.  Ligand (e.g., PAH) binds cytosolic AhR.
    2.  AhR activation and translocation to nucleus.
    3.  AhR dimerizes with ARNT (Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor Nuclear Translocator).
    4.  AhR-ARNT binds to DREs (Dioxin Response Elements) in promoter regions of CYP1A1, CYP1A2, CYP1B1 genes.
    5.  Transcriptional activation → increased mRNA/protein levels.

## Regulation of CYP450 Enzymes

-   **Constitutive Expression:** Some isoforms are expressed at basal levels (endogenous metabolism).
-   **Developmental/Tissue-Specific Regulation:** Expression varies by developmental stage, tissue type (liver is major site).
-   **Hormonal Regulation:** Steroids, thyroid hormones, growth hormone can influence expression/activity.
-   **Genetic Polymorphisms:** Variations in CYP450 genes cause inter-individual variability in expression/activity, affecting drug metabolism. Individuals classified as poor, intermediate, extensive, or ultrarapid metabolizers.
-   **Inhibition:** CYP450 activity can be inhibited by drugs/xenobiotics, leading to drug-drug interactions (increased levels of co-administered drugs). Inhibition can be competitive or mechanism-based.

Understanding CYP450 induction and regulation is crucial for predicting drug interactions, assessing toxicity, and understanding individual variability in drug response.
