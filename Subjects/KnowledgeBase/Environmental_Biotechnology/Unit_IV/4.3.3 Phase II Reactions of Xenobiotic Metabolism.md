# 4.3.3 Phase II Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism

[[4.3 Metabolism of Xenobiotics|Phase II reactions]], also known as **conjugation reactions**, are the second major phase of xenobiotic metabolism. They typically follow [[4.3.2 Phase I Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase I reactions]] and involve conjugating Phase I metabolites (or sometimes parent xenobiotics) with endogenous, hydrophilic molecules.

## General Outcomes

Phase II reactions generally result in:

-   **Increased Water Solubility:** Addition of polar groups (glucuronic acid, sulfate, glutathione, etc.).
-   **Detoxification:** Often inactivates xenobiotics by masking reactive groups.
-   **Facilitated Excretion:** Conjugates are larger, more polar, less membrane-permeable, facilitating excretion (urine or bile).

## Key Characteristics

-   **Transferase Enzymes:** Catalyzed by transferases (UDP-glucuronosyltransferases - UGTs, sulfotransferases - SULTs, glutathione S-transferases - GSTs, N-acetyltransferases - NATs, methyltransferases).
-   **Activated Conjugating Agents:** Require activated cofactors (UDPGA for glucuronidation, PAPS for sulfation, GSH for glutathione conjugation, acetyl-CoA for acetylation, SAM for methylation).
-   **Specific Functional Groups:** Target -OH, -COOH, -NH₂, -SH groups.
-   **Increased Molecular Weight/Polarity:** Significantly alters physicochemical properties.
-   **Detoxification (Generally):** Usually inactivates, but some conjugates can be reactivated or contribute to toxicity.

## Major Phase II Conjugation Pathways

-   **[[Glucuronidation (UDP-Glucuronosyltransferases - UGTs)]]:** Transfer of glucuronic acid from UDPGA.
-   **[[Sulfation (Sulfotransferases - SULTs)]]:** Transfer of a sulfo group (-SO₃H) from PAPS.
-   **Glutathione Conjugation (Glutathione S-transferases - GSTs):** Conjugation with glutathione (GSH).
-   **Acetylation (N-Acetyltransferases - NATs):** Acetylation of amino groups using acetyl-CoA.
-   **Methylation (Methyltransferases):** Transfer of a methyl group (-CH₃) from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM).

Phase II reactions are crucial for eliminating xenobiotics, but the specific pathways and enzymes involved vary depending on the xenobiotic and organism.
