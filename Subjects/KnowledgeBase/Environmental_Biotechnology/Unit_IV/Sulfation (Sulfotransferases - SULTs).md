# Sulfation (Sulfotransferases - SULTs)

Sulfation is another important [[4.3.3 Phase II Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase II conjugation pathway]], catalyzed by sulfotransferases (SULTs). It involves transferring a sulfo group (-SO₃H) from 3'-phosphoadenosine-5'-phosphosulfate (PAPS) to a [[Xenobiotics|xenobiotic]] or [[4.3.2 Phase I Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase I metabolite]].

## Key Features

-   **Enzymes:** Sulfotransferases (SULTs) are cytosolic enzymes. Humans have multiple isoforms (SULT1A1, SULT1E1, SULT2A1) with varying substrate specificities.
-   **Conjugating Agent:** 3'-Phosphoadenosine-5'-phosphosulfate (PAPS) is the activated sulfate form, synthesized from ATP and inorganic sulfate.
-   **Functional Groups Targeted:** SULTs catalyze sulfation of:
    -   Hydroxyl Groups (-OH): Phenols, alcohols, steroids. Phenolic -OH groups are particularly good substrates.
    -   Amino Groups (-NH₂): Aromatic amines (less common).
-   **Conjugate Properties:** Sulfate conjugates are typically:
    -   *More Water-Soluble:* Sulfate is highly polar/charged.
    -   *Often Inactive:* Sulfation generally reduces activity.
    -   *Readily Excreted:* Efficiently excreted via urine due to polarity/charge.
-   **Reaction:**
    `Xenobiotic-OH (or -NH₂) + PAPS --[SULT]--> Xenobiotic-O-Sulfate (or -N-Sulfate) + PAP`
-   **Examples:** Steroid hormones (DHEA, estrogen), neurotransmitters (dopamine, norepinephrine), acetaminophen, minoxidil.
-   **High-Affinity, Low-Capacity Pathway:** SULTs have high affinity for substrates but can be saturated at low concentrations due to limited PAPS. Important for metabolizing low concentrations of compounds.

Sulfation is a key detoxification pathway, but also plays roles in regulating the activity of endogenous compounds.
