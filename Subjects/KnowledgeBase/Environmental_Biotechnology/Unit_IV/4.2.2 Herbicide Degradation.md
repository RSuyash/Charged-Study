# 4.2.2 Herbicide Degradation

Herbicides are a major class of [[Xenobiotics]] used to control unwanted vegetation. Similar to [[4.2.1 Pesticide Degradation|pesticides]], they can persist in the environment and pose risks. [[Biodegradation]] is a key process for herbicide removal and detoxification.

## 4.2.2.1 Principles and Mechanisms of Herbicide Degradation

-   **Microbial Metabolism:** Bacteria and fungi are the main agents.
-   **Enzymatic Degradation Pathways:** Diverse pathways (hydrolysis, oxidation, reduction, dehalogenation, ring cleavage) depending on herbicide structure.
-   **Detoxification and Mineralization:** Aims for complete [[Mineralization]], but biotransformation is also common.
-   **Co-metabolism:** Microbes transform herbicides while growing on other substrates.
-   **Environmental Factors:** Oxygen, temperature, pH, nutrients, etc., influence degradation.

## 4.2.2.2 Examples of Herbicide Degradation

-   **Atrazine (Triazine Herbicide):**
    *   *Aerobic Degradation:* Hydrolysis (AtzA) → Hydroxyatrazine → Deamination (AtzB) → N-isopropylammelide → Hydrolysis (AtzC) → Cyanuric acid → Metabolism (AtzDEF) → CO₂ + NH₃.
    *   *Microorganisms:* *Pseudomonas* sp. strain ADP, *Arthrobacter*, *Nocardioides*, *Mesorhizobium* (possessing *atz* genes).
-   **Glyphosate (Broad-Spectrum Herbicide):**
    *   *Pathways:*
        *   GOX Pathway: Glyphosate Oxidoreductase (GOX) cleaves C-N bond → Sarcosine + Glyoxylate + Phosphate.
        *   C-P Lyase Pathway: C-P Lyase cleaves C-P bond → Sarcosine + Inorganic Phosphate.
    *   *Microorganisms:* *Arthrobacter*, *Pseudomonas*, *Bacillus*, *Flavobacterium*.
    *   *Metabolite:* AMPA (aminomethylphosphonic acid) is a common, more persistent metabolite.

Understanding these pathways is crucial for developing [[Bioremediation]] strategies for herbicide-contaminated environments.
