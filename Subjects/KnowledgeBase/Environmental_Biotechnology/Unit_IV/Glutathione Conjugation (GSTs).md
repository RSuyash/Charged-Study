# Glutathione Conjugation (Glutathione S-transferases - GSTs)

Glutathione conjugation is a [[4.3.3 Phase II Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase II conjugation pathway]] catalyzed by glutathione S-transferases (GSTs). It involves the conjugation of [[Glutathione]] (GSH), a tripeptide antioxidant, to electrophilic [[Xenobiotics]] or [[4.3.2 Phase I Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase I metabolites]].

## Key Features

-   **Enzymes:** Glutathione S-transferases (GSTs) are a family of cytosolic and microsomal enzymes.
-   **Conjugating Agent:** Glutathione (GSH) is a tripeptide (glutamate-cysteine-glycine) that acts as a nucleophile.
-   **Reaction:** GSTs catalyze the nucleophilic attack of GSH's sulfur atom on electrophilic centers in the xenobiotic, forming a glutathione conjugate.
-   **Detoxification:** Often detoxifies reactive compounds and protects against [[Oxidative Stress]].
-   **Excretion:** Glutathione conjugates are further processed and excreted via the mercapturic acid pathway.

Glutathione conjugation is an important detoxification mechanism, particularly for electrophilic compounds and reactive metabolites.
