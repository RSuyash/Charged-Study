# Methylation (Methyltransferases)

Methylation is a [[4.3.3 Phase II Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase II conjugation pathway]] catalyzed by methyltransferases. It involves the transfer of a methyl group (-CH₃) from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to a [[Xenobiotics|xenobiotic]] or [[4.3.2 Phase I Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase I metabolite]].

## Key Features

-   **Enzymes:** Methyltransferases are a diverse group. Important examples include Catechol-O-Methyltransferase (COMT) and Thiopurine Methyltransferase (TPMT).
-   **Conjugating Agent:** S-Adenosylmethionine (SAM) (AdoMet) is the activated methyl donor.
-   **Functional Groups Targeted:** Hydroxyl (-OH) groups (O-methylation), amino (-NH₂) groups (N-methylation), thiol (-SH) groups (S-methylation).
-   **Conjugate Properties:** Methylation can increase or decrease lipophilicity, and its effect on biological activity varies.
-   **Reaction:**
    `Xenobiotic-OH (or -NH₂, -SH) + SAM --[Methyltransferase]--> Xenobiotic-O-Methyl (or -N-Methyl, -S-Methyl) + SAH`
-   **Examples:** Catecholamines (dopamine to 3-O-methyldopa), histamine to N-methylhistamine, thiopurine drugs (azathioprine to 6-methylmercaptopurine).
-   **Not Always Detoxification:** Can produce metabolites with altered activity or increased toxicity (e.g., methylation of arsenic).

Methylation's role in xenobiotic metabolism is complex and not always a simple detoxification pathway.
