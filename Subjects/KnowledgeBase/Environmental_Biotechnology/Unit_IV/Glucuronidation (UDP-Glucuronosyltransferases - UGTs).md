# Glucuronidation (UDP-Glucuronosyltransferases - UGTs)

Glucuronidation is a major [[4.3.3 Phase II Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase II conjugation pathway]], catalyzed by UDP-glucuronosyltransferases (UGTs). It involves transferring glucuronic acid from UDP-glucuronic acid (UDPGA) to a [[Xenobiotics|xenobiotic]] or [[4.3.2 Phase I Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase I metabolite]]. It is a key detoxification pathway.

## Key Features

-   **Enzymes:** UDP-Glucuronosyltransferases (UGTs) are a superfamily localized primarily in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). Humans have multiple isoforms (UGT1, UGT2, UGT3) with varying substrate specificities.
-   **Conjugating Agent:** UDP-Glucuronic Acid (UDPGA) is the activated form of glucuronic acid, synthesized from UDP-glucose.
-   **Functional Groups Targeted:** UGTs catalyze glucuronidation of:
    -   Hydroxyl Groups (-OH): Phenols, alcohols, hydroxylated metabolites.
    -   Carboxyl Groups (-COOH): Carboxylic acids, some drugs.
    -   Amino Groups (-NH₂): Aromatic amines (less common).
    -   Thiol Groups (-SH): Thiols and sulfhydryl groups.
-   **Conjugate Properties:** Glucuronide conjugates are typically:
    -   *More Water-Soluble:* Glucuronic acid is a large, polar sugar.
    -   *Less Pharmacologically Active:* Often inactivates drugs.
    -   *Readily Excreted:* Efficiently excreted via urine and bile.
-   **Reaction:**
    `Xenobiotic-OH (or -COOH, -NH₂, -SH) + UDPGA --[UGT]--> Xenobiotic-O-Glucuronide (or -COO-Glucuronide, -N-Glucuronide, -S-Glucuronide) + UDP`
-   **Examples:** Morphine, acetaminophen (paracetamol), bilirubin (endogenous), steroids.
-   **Enterohepatic Circulation:** Some glucuronides excreted in bile can be hydrolyzed by bacterial β-glucuronidases in the intestine, releasing the parent compound for reabsorption, prolonging drug action.

Glucuronidation is a vital detoxification pathway, increasing water solubility and facilitating excretion of many compounds.
