# 4.3.1 Cytochrome P450 System

The Cytochrome P450 (CYP450) system is a superfamily of heme-containing monooxygenase enzymes found across life domains. In mammals, it's abundant in the liver and crucial for metabolizing endogenous compounds and [[Xenobiotics]], playing roles in both detoxification and bioactivation.

## [[4.3.1.1 Structure and Function (CYP450)]]

-   **Structure:** Heme prosthetic group (Fe-containing porphyrin), membrane-bound (ER in eukaryotes, plasma membrane in prokaryotes), reductase partner (NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductase - CPR in eukaryotes).
    ![Fig. Structure of CYP450](<../../../V2/Environmental Biotechnology/EB-Unit-IV/Unit IV 1a53a995df8780ae96ceefb751038bc0/image 9.png>)
    *Fig. CYP450 Structure*
-   **Function:** Monooxygenases (mixed-function oxidases). Catalyze monooxygenation: `RH + O₂ + NADPH + H⁺ → ROH + H₂O + NADP⁺`.

## Key Functional Aspects

-   **Broad Substrate Specificity:** Metabolize diverse compounds (endogenous steroids/fatty acids and xenobiotics).
-   **Oxidation Reactions:** Hydroxylation, epoxidation, dealkylation, oxidation.
-   **Role in Drug Metabolism:** Key enzymes in [[4.3.2 Phase I Reactions of Xenobiotic Metabolism|Phase I]] drug metabolism (inactivation, activation, toxic metabolite formation).
-   **Role in Endogenous Metabolism:** Steroid hormone synthesis, fatty acid metabolism, bile acid synthesis.

## [[4.3.1.2 Induction and Regulation of Cytochrome P450 System]]

-   **Induction:** Xenobiotic exposure can induce CYP450 expression (transcriptional regulation via nuclear receptors like AhR, CAR, PXR, PPARs).
-   **Regulation:** Constitutive expression, developmental/tissue-specific regulation, hormonal regulation, genetic polymorphisms (affecting activity), inhibition by drugs/xenobiotics.

Understanding CYP450s is crucial for predicting drug interactions, assessing toxicity, and understanding individual variability in drug response.
