# Monolayer Cultures

Monolayer cultures are a type of [[2.2.2 Types of Cell Cultures|cell culture]] characterized by **adherent growth**, where cells attach to a solid surface and grow as a single layer (monolayer).

(C) Cells lose height and starts flattening after a few days of culturing. Goblet cells starts losing its secretory capacity first. (D) Columnar cells lose their cilia and goblet cells have completely undergone squamous transformation. (E) Complete squamous transformation of all epithelial cells occurs within 2-3 weeks.](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-II/Unit II 1a53a995df87801995e5f528b8bf2f56/image 16.png>)
*Fig. Monolayer cell culture example (respiratory epithelium)*

## Characteristics

-   **Adherent Growth:** Cells are anchorage-dependent, requiring attachment to a substrate (e.g., tissue culture-treated plastic) for survival and growth.
-   **Attachment Dependence:** Mediated by cell adhesion molecules (integrins) interacting with ECM components on the surface or secreted by cells.
-   **Limited Surface Area:** Growth is limited by the available surface area of the culture vessel. Proliferation slows/stops at confluence due to contact inhibition.
-   **Cell Morphology:** Cells typically exhibit a flattened, spread-out morphology (e.g., fibroblast-like, epithelial-like). Morphology is easily observed.
-   **Cell-Cell Contacts:** Cells form junctions (adherens, tight, gap junctions), mediating communication and tissue-like organization.
-   **Diffusion Limitations:** Nutrient/oxygen diffusion can become limited in dense monolayers.

## Maintenance

-   **Culture Vessels:** Tissue culture flasks (T-flasks), dishes (Petri), multiwell plates, roller bottles (for larger scale). Surfaces are treated to promote attachment.
-   **Subculturing (Passaging):** Essential to prevent overgrowth.
    1.  **Detach Adherent Cells:** Use enzymatic (Trypsin-EDTA, Accutase) or mechanical (cell scrapers, pipetting for loosely adherent cells) methods. Neutralize enzymes after detachment.
    2.  **[[2.2.1.2 Viable Cell Count Methods|Count Viable Cells]]:** Determine cell concentration/viability.
    3.  **Dilute and Re-seed:** Dilute cell suspension to desired seeding density in fresh medium and transfer to new vessels.
    4.  **Incubation:** Incubate under optimal conditions ([[2.1.3 Environmental Factors]]).
-   **Monitoring:**
    *   **Microscopic Examination:** Regularly check morphology, confluency, contamination using [[1.2.3 Microscopes]].
    *   **Media Changes:** Regularly (e.g., every 2-3 days) replenish nutrients, remove waste.
    *   **Viable Cell Counts:** Periodically monitor growth/viability.
    *   **Prevent Overconfluency:** Subculture before confluence to avoid contact inhibition/stress.
-   **[[2.1 Basic Aseptic Techniques|Aseptic Technique]]:** Strictly adhere to prevent contamination.

## Advantages

-   **Easy Microscopic Observation:** 2D growth allows easy visualization of morphology/behavior.
-   **Defined Cell Environment:** Cells directly exposed to medium, allowing better control and easier experimental manipulation.
-   **Widely Used/Established:** Common technique with extensive protocols.
-   **Suitable for Adherent Cells:** Natural method for anchorage-dependent cells.

## Limitations

-   **Limited Surface Area:** Restricts achievable cell densities.
-   **Labor-Intensive (Large Scale):** Manual passaging of multiple vessels can be laborious.
-   **Diffusion Limitations (Dense Cultures):** Can lead to nutrient/oxygen gradients.
-   **Less Representative of *In Vivo* Tissue:** 2D culture may not fully mimic 3D tissue architecture and interactions.
