# Suspension Cultures

Suspension cultures are a type of [[2.2.2 Types of Cell Cultures|cell culture]] characterized by **anchorage-independent growth**, where cells grow freely suspended in the liquid culture medium without attaching to a surface.

## Characteristics

-   **Anchorage-Independent Growth:** Cells do not require attachment to a substrate. Suitable for cell lines adapted for suspension growth (e.g., hematopoietic cells, hybridomas, some engineered lines).
-   **Homogenous Environment:** Uniform access to nutrients and oxygen for all cells.
-   **Higher Cell Densities:** Growth not limited by surface area, allowing significantly higher densities than [[Monolayer Cultures]]. Essential for large-scale production.
-   **Scalable:** Readily scalable for large-scale bioprocessing in bioreactors (thousands of liters). Preferred method for industrial production (biopharmaceuticals, vaccines).
-   **Cell Morphology:** Cells typically exhibit a rounded/spherical morphology.
-   **Cell Aggregation:** Cells may form aggregates or clumps depending on cell type and conditions.

## Maintenance

Requires techniques focused on maintaining suspension, controlling density, mixing, and aeration.

-   **Culture Vessels:**
    *   *Erlenmeyer Flasks (Shake Flasks):* Small-scale, placed on orbital shakers for agitation/aeration.
    *   *Spinner Flasks:* Glass/plastic flasks with magnetic stir bar or impeller for gentle agitation. Small to medium scale.
    *   *Suspension Bioreactors (Stirred-Tank):* Large-scale (liters to 100,000+ L). Equipped with:
        *   Agitation System (Impellers)
        *   Aeration System (Spargers, Surface Aeration)
        *   Temperature Control
        *   [[2.1.3.1 pH and Buffering Systems|pH Control]]
        *   Dissolved Oxygen (DO) Control
        *   Foam Control
        *   Sampling/Harvest Systems
    *   *Types of Bioreactor Operation:* Batch, Fed-Batch (common for high productivity), Continuous (Perfusion, Chemostat - highest productivity, complex).

-   **Subculturing (Passaging):** Simpler than monolayer; no detachment needed.
    1.  **[[2.2.1.2 Viable Cell Count Methods|Count Viable Cells]]:** Determine concentration/viability.
    2.  **Calculate Dilution:** Determine volume needed for desired seeding density.
    3.  **Transfer/Dilute:** Transfer calculated volume to new vessels with fresh medium.
    4.  **Incubation:** Incubate under appropriate conditions (shaker, bioreactor).

-   **Monitoring:**
    *   **Microscopic Examination:** Periodically check morphology, aggregation, viability, contamination.
    *   **Viable Cell Counts:** Regularly monitor growth rate/viability. Generate growth curves.
    *   **pH and DO:** Monitor, especially in bioreactors. Adjust aeration/agitation/buffer addition.
    *   **Metabolic Monitoring:** Monitor glucose consumption, lactate production.
    *   **Preventing Aggregation (if undesirable):** Use low Ca++/Mg++ media, anti-clumping agents (Pluronic F-68), optimize agitation.

-   **[[2.1 Basic Aseptic Techniques|Aseptic Technique]]:** Maintain strict sterility.

## Advantages

-   **High Cell Densities:** Suitable for large-scale production.
-   **Scalability:** Readily scalable for industrial bioreactors.
-   **Homogenous Environment:** Uniform access to nutrients/oxygen.
-   **Easier Subculturing/Handling:** Simpler than detaching adherent cells.
-   **Suitable for Shear-Tolerant Cells:** Well-suited for cells that can withstand hydrodynamic forces.

## Limitations

-   **Not Suitable for Anchorage-Dependent Cells:** Requires suspension-adapted lines.
-   **Shear Stress:** Agitation/aeration can damage fragile cells. Requires optimization or shear-protective additives.
-   **Cell Aggregation:** Can hinder nutrient/oxygen diffusion and affect viability/homogeneity.
-   **Microscopic Observation:** Can be more challenging due to cell movement.
