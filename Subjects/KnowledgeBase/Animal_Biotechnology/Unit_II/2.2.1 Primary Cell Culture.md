# 2.2.1 Primary Cell Culture

[[Primary Cell Culture]] refers to the *in vitro* culture of cells **directly isolated** from living animal tissues. These cultures represent the initial passage of cells derived from an organism and generally retain characteristics similar to the tissue of origin.

Key aspects of primary cell culture include:

-   [[2.2.1.1 Isolation and Separation of Cells from Tissues]]
-   [[2.2.1.2 Viable Cell Count Methods]]
-   [[2.2.1.3 Maintenance of Primary Cell Culture]]

Primary cell cultures are distinct from [[3.1.2 Permanent Established Cell Lines]] due to their **finite lifespan** ([[3.1.1 Primary Cell Lines]]). They are valuable models for studying normal cell biology and tissue-specific functions but can be challenging to establish and maintain.
