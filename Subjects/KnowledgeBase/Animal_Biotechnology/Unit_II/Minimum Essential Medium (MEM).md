# Minimum Essential Medium (MEM)

Minimum Essential Medium (MEM), developed by Harry Eagle, is a chemically defined medium providing the **minimum** essential nutrients required for the growth of many mammalian cell types in [[Animal Cell Culture]]. It's a significant step up from [[Basal Salt Solutions (BSS)]] and is widely used as a **basal medium**, often supplemented further.

## Components of MEM

MEM contains a defined mixture of:

-   **[[Basal Salt Solutions (BSS)|Basal Salt Solution]]:** Typically based on Earle’s Balanced Salt Solution (EBSS) for osmotic balance, pH buffering, and essential inorganic ions.
-   **Essential Amino Acids (8 or 9):** L-Arginine, L-Cystine/Cysteine, L-Histidine, L-Isoleucine, L-Leucine, L-Lysine, L-Methionine, L-Phenylalanine, L-Threonine, L-Tryptophan, L-Valine. These cannot be synthesized sufficiently by mammalian cells.
-   **Vitamins (Essential):** Choline chloride, Folic acid, *myo*-Inositol, Nicotinamide, Pantothenate, Pyridoxal HCl, Riboflavin, Thiamine HCl. Essential cofactors for metabolic processes.
-   **Glucose:** Primary carbon and energy source.
-   **Salts and Inorganic Ions:** From the BSS component.
-   **Bicarbonate Buffer (NaHCO₃):** Physiological buffer requiring a controlled [[CO2 Control|CO₂ atmosphere]] (typically 5%). See [[2.1.3.1 pH and Buffering Systems]].
-   **Phenol Red:** pH indicator.

## Components NOT Typically Included (Often Supplemented)

-   **Non-Essential Amino Acids:** L-Alanine, L-Asparagine, L-Aspartic acid, L-Glutamic acid, Glycine, L-Proline, L-Serine, L-Tyrosine. Supplementation can enhance growth for some cell types.
-   **Serum or Serum Replacements:** MEM is typically serum-free in basal form. [[2.1.2.1 Serum-Dependent Media|Serum]] (e.g., FBS) is often added (5-20%) to provide growth factors, hormones, attachment factors, lipids, etc., necessary for optimal growth of many cell types.
-   **Growth Factors and Hormones:** Specific factors (EGF, FGF, Insulin) often added for specific cell types or functions.
-   **Lipids and Fatty Acids:** Essential membrane components/signaling molecules, often provided by serum or specific supplements in serum-free media.
-   **Trace Elements:** Selenium, zinc, copper, etc., may be added.
-   **Antibiotics and Antimycotics:** Often added to prevent contamination, but use is generally minimized. See [[2.1 Types of Antibiotics]].

## Applications

-   **Basal Medium:** Supports survival/growth of many adherent and suspension mammalian cell lines (fibroblasts, epithelial cells, some tumor lines).
-   **Foundation for Complex Media:** Supplemented with serum, growth factors, etc., to create specialized media.
-   **Cytotoxicity/Short-Term Assays:** Used serum-free or low-serum for cytotoxicity assays, viral assays where long-term proliferation isn't the goal.
-   **Virus Propagation:** Used for propagating many viruses in cell culture.
-   **Metabolic Studies:** Provides a chemically defined base for studying cellular metabolism.

## Advantages

-   **Chemically Defined Basal Medium:** Known components, better reproducibility than [[2.1.1 Natural Media|natural media]].
-   **Widely Used/Characterized:** Extensive literature and established protocols.
-   **Versatile:** Can be supplemented/modified for various applications.
-   **Commercially Available:** Readily available in powder or liquid form.

## Limitations

-   **Serum Dependence (Often):** Basal MEM typically requires serum for optimal growth of most cell types, introducing undefined components and variability.
-   **Limited Support for Specialized Cells:** May not be optimal for fastidious cells requiring specific factors not in the basal formulation.
-   **Nutrient Limitations (High Density):** May become nutrient-limited in high-density cultures.

MEM remains a fundamental basal medium, but often requires modification for specialized cell culture needs.
