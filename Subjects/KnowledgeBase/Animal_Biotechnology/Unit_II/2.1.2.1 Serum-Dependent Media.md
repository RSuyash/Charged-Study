# 2.1.2.1 Serum-Dependent Media

Serum-dependent media are a type of [[2.1.2 Defined Media]] that still require supplementation with **animal serum**, typically Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS), for optimal cell growth and proliferation in [[Animal Cell Culture]]. While the basal medium components (like [[Minimum Essential Medium (MEM)]], DMEM, RPMI 1640) are chemically defined, the serum itself is a complex, undefined biological fluid. These media represent a transitional step towards fully defined, [[2.1.2.2 Serum-Independent Media|serum-free media]].

## Serum (Typically Fetal Bovine Serum - FBS)

Serum is obtained from animal blood (usually fetal bovine) and is a rich, complex mixture containing:
-   **Growth Factors:** Stimulate cell proliferation/survival (EGF, FGF, PDGF, IGF).
-   **Hormones:** Steroid and peptide hormones regulating cell functions.
-   **Attachment Factors:** Proteins (fibronectin, laminin, vitronectin) promoting cell adhesion.
-   **Transport Proteins:** Albumin, transferrin (transport lipids, hormones, etc.).
-   **Lipids and Fatty Acids:** Essential membrane components and signaling molecules.
-   **Vitamins and Minerals:** Additional nutrients.
-   **Buffering Capacity:** Serum proteins contribute to pH stability.
-   **Protease Inhibitors:** Protect cells/proteins from degradation.

## Advantages of Serum-Dependent Defined Media

-   **Improved Consistency (vs. Natural Media):** Using a defined basal medium reduces variability compared to fully undefined [[2.1.1 Natural Media|natural media]].
-   **Better Control (Basal Nutrients):** Precise control over defined components (amino acids, vitamins, salts, glucose).
-   **Supports Growth of Many Cell Types:** Serum provides a broad spectrum of factors supporting diverse cell types.
-   **Established Protocols/Availability:** Widely used (e.g., serum-supplemented MEM/DMEM), well-characterized, commercially available.

## Disadvantages of Serum-Dependent Defined Media

-   **Serum is Undefined/Complex:** Introduces batch-to-batch variability affecting reproducibility. Contains thousands of uncharacterized components.
-   **Ethical Concerns (FBS):** Use of fetal bovine serum raises animal welfare concerns. Drive towards serum reduction/replacement.
-   **Risk of Contamination:** Serum carries risk of viruses, mycoplasma, prions, other adventitious agents despite screening.
-   **High Cost:** Serum (especially FBS) is a significant cost component.
-   **Interference with Downstream Processing:** High concentration of serum proteins complicates purification of bioproducts, increasing costs.
-   **Batch-to-Batch Variability of Serum:** Even tested batches vary in growth factors, hormones, etc., leading to inconsistencies.
-   **Potential Immunogenicity (Human Therapeutics):** Animal serum components may pose immunogenicity risks if not completely removed during purification.

Despite disadvantages, serum-dependent media remain widely used, especially in research and for cells difficult to grow serum-free. However, the trend is towards serum-free and chemically defined media, particularly in biopharmaceutical production.
