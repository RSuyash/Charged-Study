# Embryonic Cell Cultures

Embryonic cell cultures are derived from **embryonic tissues**, typically early-stage embryos (e.g., blastocysts). They are unique due to their **pluripotency** - the potential to differentiate into any cell type of the adult organism. [[Embryonic Stem Cells (ESCs)]] are the most well-known type.

## Derivation of Embryonic Stem Cell (ESC) Cultures

1.  **Source:** Early-stage embryos (blastocysts) from animal models or surplus human IVF embryos (under strict ethical guidelines).
2.  **Isolation of Inner Cell Mass (ICM):** Outer layer (trophoblast) removed, pluripotent ICM isolated.
3.  **Culture Conditions:**
    *   *Feeder Layer Culture (Traditional):* ICM cultured on inactivated mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) providing growth factors/ECM.
    *   *Feeder-Free Culture (Modern):* Preferred for clinical use. Culture on vessels coated with defined ECM (Matrigel, laminin, vitronectin) in chemically defined, [[2.1.2.2 Serum-Independent Media|serum-free media]].
4.  **Culture Medium:** Specialized serum-free media supplemented with factors to maintain pluripotency and inhibit differentiation:
    *   *bFGF (basic Fibroblast Growth Factor):* Key for self-renewal/pluripotency.
    *   *LIF (Leukemia Inhibitory Factor):* Essential for mouse ESCs (less critical for human ESCs).
    *   *TGF-β/Activin Signaling Modulators.*
    *   *Small Molecule Inhibitors:* Block differentiation pathways (e.g., GSK3i, MEKi, ROCKi).
5.  **Passaging/Expansion:** Regular passaging (mechanical or enzymatic) to prevent differentiation and maintain undifferentiated state. Avoid single-cell dissociation in some cases.
6.  **Characterization:** Rigorous verification of pluripotent state, genetic stability, absence of differentiation:
    *   *Morphology:* Characteristic colony morphology.
    *   *Pluripotency Marker Expression:* Check key markers (Oct4, Sox2, Nanog, SSEA-4, Tra-1-60) via immunofluorescence, flow cytometry, gene expression.
    *   *Karyotyping:* Monitor chromosome stability.
    *   *Differentiation Potential Assays:* Verify ability to differentiate into all three germ layers (*in vitro* differentiation, *in vivo* teratoma formation assay).
    *   *Sterility Testing:* Check for microbial contamination.

## Types of Embryonic Cell Cultures

-   **[[Embryonic Stem Cells (ESCs)]]:** Derived from ICM of blastocysts. Pluripotent, indefinite self-renewal under proper conditions.
-   **Embryonic Germ Cells (EGCs):** Derived from primordial germ cells. Also pluripotent, similar properties to ESCs, but less commonly used.

## Applications

-   **Basic Research (Developmental Biology):** Models for early development, differentiation, gene regulation.
-   **Drug Discovery/Toxicity Testing:** ESC-derived differentiated cells (cardiomyocytes, neurons, hepatocytes) for screening and testing.
-   **Regenerative Medicine/Cell Therapy:** Potential to differentiate into any cell type for treating diseases (Parkinson's, diabetes, spinal cord injury, heart disease). [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells (iPSCs)]] are closely related and also used.
-   **Disease Modeling:** Patient-specific iPSCs create *in vitro* models for studying diseases and developing personalized medicine.
-   **Genetic Engineering/Genome Editing:** Amenable to modification (CRISPR-Cas9) for research and therapeutic applications.

Embryonic cell cultures (ESCs, iPSCs) are powerful tools but raise ethical considerations (embryo use, tumorigenicity potential). Careful oversight and safety protocols are essential.
