# 2.2.1.1 Isolation and Separation of Cells from Tissues

Isolating [[3.1.1 Primary Cell Lines|primary cells]] from tissues involves dissociating cells from the tissue matrix and separating the desired cell population. Methods depend on tissue type, desired cells, and application. [[2.1 Basic Aseptic Techniques|Aseptic technique]] is crucial throughout.

## General Steps

1.  **Tissue Collection:**
    *   **Source:** Animal biopsy, post-mortem tissue, embryonic tissue, or commercially available primary cells. Ethical considerations apply.
    *   **Sterile Collection:** Use sterile instruments, containers, transport media (e.g., [[Basal Salt Solutions (BSS)|BSS]] or media with antibiotics).
    *   **Transport/Processing:** Transport in sterile medium, process ASAP for optimal viability.

2.  **Mechanical Dissection and Disaggregation:**
    *   **Purpose:** Reduce tissue size, initiate cell separation.
    *   **Methods:**
        *   *Mincing/Chopping:* Finely mince tissue (1-2 mm³) with sterile scalpels/scissors to increase surface area for enzymes.
        *   *Sieving/Filtration:* Pass minced tissue through sterile sieves/filters to disaggregate further.
        *   *Gentle Homogenization:* Less common, can damage cells. Often combined with enzymatic digestion.

3.  **Enzymatic Digestion of Extracellular Matrix (ECM):**
    *   **Purpose:** Digest ECM components holding cells together, releasing individual cells/small clusters. Crucial for most solid tissues.
    *   **Enzymes Used:**
        *   *Collagenase:* Digests collagen (abundant ECM protein). Essential for connective tissue, skin, bone.
        *   *Trypsin:* Serine protease, cleaves peptide bonds (cell surface/ECM proteins). Widely used but can be harsh.
        *   *Dispase (Neutral Protease):* Digests basement membrane components, cell surface proteins. Milder than trypsin, good for epithelial cells.
        *   *Hyaluronidase:* Digests hyaluronic acid (ECM glycosaminoglycan). Aids disaggregation.
        *   *DNase:* Digests DNA released from lysed cells, preventing clumping and reducing viscosity.
    *   **Procedure:**
        1.  Prepare sterile enzyme solution(s) in buffer (e.g., [[Basal Salt Solutions (BSS)|HBSS]]). Optimize enzyme type/concentration for tissue.
        2.  Incubate minced tissue with enzyme solution (shaking water bath/incubator, 37°C, 30 min to hours).
        3.  Gentle agitation during digestion aids dispersion/enzyme penetration.
        4.  Neutralize enzyme activity (add serum-containing medium or dilute/wash).
        5.  Wash cells (media/BSS) to remove enzyme/debris. Centrifuge and resuspend.
        6.  Filter (optional) through cell strainers for single-cell suspension.

4.  **Cell Separation and Purification (Optional):**
    *   **Purpose:** Purify desired cell population from heterogeneous mixture.
    *   **Methods:**
        *   *Differential Centrifugation:* Separates based on size/density differences at varying speeds. Initial enrichment, low purity.
        *   *Density Gradient Centrifugation:* Separates based on density using gradients (Ficoll, Percoll). Cells migrate to isopycnic positions. Better density separation.
        *   *Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting (FACS):* Powerful separation based on fluorescence. Cells labeled with fluorescent antibodies/dyes targeting specific markers. High purity sorting.
        *   *Magnetic Cell Sorting (MACS):* Uses magnetic beads conjugated to antibodies binding specific markers. Magnet retains labeled cells. Enrichment/purification.
        *   *Selective Adhesion:* Separates based on differential adhesion to culture vessels/substrates (e.g., fibroblasts vs. epithelial cells).
        *   *Selective Culture Media:* Media promoting desired cell growth while inhibiting others. Enrichment over time.

5.  **Viable Cell Count and Characterization:**
    *   **[[2.2.1.2 Viable Cell Count Methods|Viable Cell Count]]:** Determine number of living cells (e.g., Trypan Blue exclusion) for standardizing cultures.
    *   **Cell Characterization:** Verify identity, purity, viability, function.
        *   *Microscopic Examination:* Morphology, homogeneity.
        *   *Flow Cytometry:* Cell surface markers, cell cycle status.
        *   *Immunocytochemistry/Immunohistochemistry:* Staining for cell-type specific markers.
        *   *Functional Assays:* Verify characteristic functions.
        *   *Karyotyping:* Assess chromosome number/structure.
