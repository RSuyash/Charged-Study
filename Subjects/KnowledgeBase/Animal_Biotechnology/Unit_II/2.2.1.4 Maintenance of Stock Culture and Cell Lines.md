# 2.2.1.4 Maintenance of Stock Culture and Cell Lines

Maintaining stock cultures and [[3.1.2 Permanent Established Cell Lines|established cell lines]] is essential for ensuring a continuous, reliable supply of cells for research, bioproduction, and other [[Animal Cell Culture]] applications. Proper maintenance preserves cells at specific passage numbers in a stable, healthy state. While discussed under [[2.2.1 Primary Cell Culture]] in the source, these techniques are crucial for all continuously cultured lines.

## Key Maintenance Techniques

1.  **Regular Subculturing (Passaging):**
    *   **Routine Schedule:** Establish based on cell line growth rate/density (e.g., every 2-3 days to weekly+).
    *   **Timing:** Passage cells during logarithmic (exponential) growth phase, before confluence/stationary phase, to maintain health and growth characteristics. Avoid passaging overgrown/stressed cells.
    *   **Consistent Seeding Density:** Use consistent density for reproducible growth rates. Calculate based on viable counts and desired split ratio.
    *   **Document Passage Number:** Crucial for tracking cell history. Limit passage number for finite lines ([[3.1.1 Primary Cell Lines]]) to avoid senescence/drift. Monitor established lines for drift over high passages.

2.  **Regular Media Changes:**
    *   **Replenishment:** Change media regularly (e.g., every 2-3 days) to replenish nutrients, remove waste, maintain optimal pH/composition. Adjust frequency based on density/growth rate.
    *   **Consistency:** Use the same well-characterized medium and supplements for routine maintenance. Use batch-tested serum if applicable.

3.  **Incubation Conditions Monitoring/Control:**
    *   **Routine Checks:** Regularly monitor/record incubator conditions ([[2.1.3.4 Temperature Control|temperature]], [[2.1.3.2 CO2 and Bicarbonate Regulation|CO₂]], [[Humidity Control|humidity]]). Calibrate periodically.
    *   **Stability:** Maintain stable, optimal conditions (e.g., 37°C, 5% CO₂, >90% humidity for most mammalian lines).

4.  **Cell Health and Viability Monitoring:**
    *   **Microscopic Examination:** Routinely check morphology, growth patterns, confluency, signs of contamination/stress/phenotypic changes using [[1.2.3 Microscopes]].
    *   **[[2.2.1.2 Viable Cell Count Methods|Viable Cell Counts]]:** Periodically monitor growth rates and viability trends (e.g., at each passage). Track doubling times/growth curves.
    *   **Growth Curve Analysis:** Generate periodically to monitor proliferation characteristics and detect changes.

5.  **[[Cryopreservation]] and Cell Banking:**
    *   **Cryopreserve Regularly:** Freeze stock cultures at early passages to create cell banks. Ensures stable source, minimizes risk of senescence, drift, contamination.
    *   **Master Cell Bank (MCB) / Working Cell Bank (WCB):** Establish characterized/tested MCB (early passage). Prepare WCBs from MCB for routine use. Ensures quality-controlled cell source.
    *   **Proper Procedures:** Follow validated cryopreservation/thawing protocols (cryoprotectants, controlled freezing, rapid thawing) to maximize survival.

6.  **Genetic Stability Monitoring (Especially for Cell Lines):**
    *   **Karyotyping:** Periodically check chromosome number/structure to monitor for abnormalities/instability.
    *   **STR Profiling/DNA Fingerprinting:** Verify cell line identity, detect cross-contamination. Crucial for reproducibility.
    *   **Growth/Phenotype Monitoring:** Track growth rates, morphology, key markers for signs of drift.

7.  **Contamination Prevention and Control:**
    *   **Strict [[2.1 Basic Aseptic Techniques|Aseptic Technique]]:** Rigorous adherence is essential.
    *   **Regular Contamination Checks:** Monitor for bacteria, fungi, mycoplasma (visual, microscopic, PCR assays).
    *   **Quarantine/Isolate:** Immediately isolate contaminated cultures, dispose properly.
    *   **Root Cause Analysis/CAPA:** Investigate contamination source, implement corrective/preventive actions.

8.  **Record Keeping and Documentation:**
    *   **Detailed Records:** Maintain comprehensive records (cell line name, passage #, dates, media, cryo/thaw info, counts, viability, growth curves, contamination checks, characterization data, deviations).
    *   **Format:** Electronic (databases, software) or paper notebooks. Ensure data integrity, traceability, secure storage.

Consistent and meticulous maintenance, including proper cell banking and quality control, is essential for reliable and reproducible results in cell culture applications.
