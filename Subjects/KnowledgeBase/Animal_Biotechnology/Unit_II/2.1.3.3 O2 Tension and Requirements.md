# 2.1.3.3 O2 Tension and Requirements

Oxygen (O₂) tension, or partial pressure (pO₂), is a critical [[2.1.3 Environmental Factors|environmental factor]] in [[Animal Cell Culture]]. Most animal cells rely on aerobic respiration for energy, making oxygen essential for survival, growth, and function. Maintaining appropriate O₂ tension is crucial.

## Importance of Oxygen Tension

-   **Aerobic Respiration:** Oxygen is the terminal electron acceptor in the mitochondrial electron transport chain, essential for efficient ATP generation via oxidative phosphorylation.
-   **Metabolism/Energy:** Required for oxidizing glucose, fatty acids, amino acids. Insufficient O₂ limits energy production.
-   **Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) / Oxidative Stress:** While essential, excessive O₂ (hyperoxia) increases ROS production (superoxide, H₂O₂, hydroxyl radicals), causing oxidative stress and damaging DNA, proteins, lipids.
-   **Cellular Differentiation/Function:** O₂ tension influences stem cell differentiation, angiogenesis, tissue development, etc.
-   **Hypoxia (Low O₂):** Occurs in high-density cultures or poorly oxygenated systems. Leads to reduced growth, metabolic shifts (increased lactate), cell stress, apoptosis.
-   **Hyperoxia (High O₂):** Can induce oxidative stress, cell dysfunction, reduced viability.

## O₂ Tension: In Vivo vs. In Vitro

-   **Physiological O₂ *In Vivo*:** Varies by tissue, often significantly lower than atmospheric levels (hypoxic).
    -   *Arterial Blood:* ~13% O₂ (90-100 mmHg)
    -   *Venous Blood:* ~5% O₂ (30-40 mmHg)
    -   *Tissues (average):* ~1-7% O₂ (5-50 mmHg)
    -   *Bone Marrow:* ~1-5% O₂
    -   *Brain:* ~2-5% O₂
-   **Atmospheric O₂ *In Vitro*:** Standard incubators (~21% O₂) are often **supraphysiological** (hyperoxic) for many cell types.

## Controlling Oxygen Tension

Specialized incubators or bioreactors can control O₂ tension:

-   **Hypoxic Incubators (Low Oxygen):** Maintain O₂ below atmospheric levels.
    -   *Mechanism:* Nitrogen (N₂) displacement or gas mixers (N₂/O₂/CO₂).
    -   *Applications:* Culturing cells from hypoxic *in vivo* environments (stem cells, tumor cells), studying hypoxia responses, mimicking physiological conditions.
-   **Hyperoxic Conditions:** Less common, used for specialized applications, risk of oxidative stress.

## Oxygen Supply and Demand

Balancing supply and demand is crucial.

-   **Oxygen Supply Methods:**
    *   *Atmospheric Diffusion (Static Culture):* O₂ diffuses from air into media (flasks, dishes). Can be limiting in deep or dense cultures.
    *   *Agitation/Surface Aeration (Shake Flasks, Stirred Tanks):* Enhances O₂ transfer from air-liquid interface.
    *   *Sparging/Diffused Aeration (Bioreactors):* Air/O₂ bubbled directly into media. More efficient for high density.
    *   *Oxygen-Permeable Vessels:* Silicone flasks, gas-permeable bags improve O₂ supply in static cultures.
-   **Oxygen Demand Factors:**
    *   *Cell Density:* Higher density = higher demand.
    *   *Cell Type/Metabolic Activity:* Varies by cell type and growth rate.
    *   *Media Composition:* Influences metabolism and O₂ demand.

Optimizing O₂ tension and delivery based on cell type, culture system, and scale is essential for successful cell culture.
