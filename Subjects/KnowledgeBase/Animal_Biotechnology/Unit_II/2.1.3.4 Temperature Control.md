# 2.1.3.4 Temperature Control

Temperature is a fundamental [[2.1.3 Environmental Factors|environmental factor]] profoundly affecting biological processes in [[Animal Cell Culture]]. Precise temperature control within a narrow physiological range is critical for cell survival, growth, proliferation, differentiation, and function.

## Importance of Temperature Control

-   **Enzyme Activity:** Highly temperature-dependent. Optimal ranges exist; deviations reduce activity, slow metabolism.
-   **Metabolic Rate/Cellular Processes:** Temperature influences metabolic rates, protein synthesis, DNA replication, cell cycle progression. Rates increase with temperature up to an optimum, then decline.
-   **Membrane Fluidity/Function:** Affects membrane fluidity, permeability, transport, receptor function, signaling.
-   **Protein Stability/Folding:** Temperature-sensitive. High temperatures cause denaturation, aggregation, loss of function.
-   **Cell Survival/Viability:** Extreme temperatures (hyperthermia/hypothermia) can be lethal, inducing stress, apoptosis, or necrosis.

## Physiological Temperature Range (Mammalian Cells)

-   **Optimal Temperature:** **37°C** (normal human body temperature) is the standard for most mammalian cells.
-   **Physiological Range:** Cells typically tolerate a narrow range around 37°C (e.g., **36-38°C**).
-   **Sensitivity:** Animal cells are highly sensitive to fluctuations; even small deviations impact growth and results.

## Temperature Control Equipment

Precise control is maintained using:

1.  **[[1.2.2 CO2 Incubator|Cell Culture Incubators]]:**
    *   Maintain stable, uniform temperature (typically 37°C ± 0.5°C).
    *   *Control Systems:* Accurate sensors (thermocouples, thermistors) and feedback loops regulate heating elements.
    *   *Uniformity:* Achieved via air circulation fans, heated shelves, or water jackets to minimize gradients.
    *   *Monitoring/Alarms:* Temperature displays and alarms alert users to deviations.

2.  **Bioreactors:**
    *   Sophisticated temperature control systems for large-scale culture.
    *   *Probes/Controllers:* Temperature probes (RTDs) monitor media temperature; controllers regulate heating/cooling.
    *   *Heating/Cooling Systems:* Jacketed vessels or internal coils circulate temperature-controlled water/coolant. External heat exchangers used for large volumes.
    *   *Cascade Control Loops (Advanced):* Multiple sensors/controllers for precise control.

## Factors Affecting Temperature Control

-   **Heat Generation (Metabolism):** Cellular metabolism is exothermic, generating heat, especially in high-density cultures/bioreactors. Cooling systems are needed to remove metabolic heat.
-   **Heat Transfer Efficiency:** Vessel material, design, surface area-to-volume ratio affect heat transfer.
-   **Environmental Fluctuations:** Ambient lab temperature changes require compensation by control systems.
-   **Evaporative Cooling:** Water evaporation can cool media, especially in systems with insufficient humidity control. Humidified incubators minimize this.

Precise and reliable temperature control is non-negotiable for successful animal cell culture, ensuring viability, growth, function, and experimental reproducibility.
