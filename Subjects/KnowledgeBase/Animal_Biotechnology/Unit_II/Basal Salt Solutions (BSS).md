# Basal Salt Solutions (BSS)

Basal Salt Solutions (BSS) are the simplest form of [[Animal Cell Culture]] media, representing a type of [[2.1.1 Natural Media]]. They are aqueous solutions of inorganic salts designed primarily to maintain **osmotic balance** and **pH** mimicking animal body fluids. BSS alone **do not support cell proliferation** but are vital for short-term cell survival, washing cells, and as a base for more complex media.

## Types of BSS

Common examples differ slightly in ionic composition and buffering capacity:

-   **Balanced Salt Solutions (BSS):** General term.
-   **Dulbecco’s Phosphate-Buffered Saline (DPBS or D-PBS):** Widely used for washing cells, diluting reagents, base for complex media. Isotonic, buffered to physiological pH (~7.2-7.4) with phosphate.
-   **Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution (HBSS):** Similar to DPBS but includes glucose, phenol red, and often calcium/magnesium. Used for short-term tissue/organ maintenance and procedures requiring Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺.
-   **Earle’s Balanced Salt Solution (EBSS):** Differs from HBSS in bicarbonate concentration. Designed for use with controlled [[CO2 Control|CO₂ atmosphere]] (e.g., in a [[1.2.2 CO2 Incubator]]) for bicarbonate buffering.

## Composition (General Components)

BSS typically contain inorganic salts dissolved in high-purity water:

-   **Sodium Chloride (NaCl):** Maintains osmolarity, ionic balance.
-   **Potassium Chloride (KCl):** Intracellular ionic environment, membrane potential.
-   **Sodium Phosphate (Na₂HPO₄, NaH₂PO₄):** Phosphate buffer for pH maintenance.
-   **Potassium Phosphate (KH₂PO₄):** Contributes to buffering, provides K⁺.
-   **Calcium Chloride (CaCl₂):** Cell adhesion, membrane function, signaling (often omitted in suspension culture media).
-   **Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄):** Enzyme cofactor, ribosome stabilization.
-   **Sodium Bicarbonate (NaHCO₃):** Major physiological buffer (used with CO₂ control, e.g., in EBSS). See [[2.1.3.1 pH and Buffering Systems]].
-   **Glucose (Dextrose):** Minimal energy source (added to some BSS like HBSS).
-   **Phenol Red:** pH indicator (added to some BSS like HBSS). Red at physiological pH, yellow (acidic), purple (alkaline).

**Note:** BSS are typically **serum-free** and **nutrient-poor**. They require supplementation (e.g., with serum, amino acids, vitamins) to create complete growth media.
