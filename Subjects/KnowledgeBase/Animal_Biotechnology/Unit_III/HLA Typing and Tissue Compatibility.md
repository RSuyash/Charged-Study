# HLA Typing and Tissue Compatibility

**HLA Typing** (Human Leukocyte Antigen Typing), also known as tissue typing or histocompatibility testing, identifies HLA genes/molecules on an individual's cells. These genes are located on chromosome 6 within the Major Histocompatibility Complex (MHC) and are highly polymorphic (many alleles exist).

## HLA Molecules and Function

HLA genes encode cell surface glycoproteins crucial for the immune system:

-   **Antigen Presentation:** Present processed antigens to T cells, initiating immune responses.
    -   *Class I HLA (HLA-A, -B, -C):* Present to cytotoxic T cells (CD8+). Expressed on nearly all nucleated cells.
    -   *Class II HLA (HLA-DR, -DQ, -DP):* Present to helper T cells (CD4+). Expressed primarily on antigen-presenting cells (APCs: macrophages, dendritic cells, B cells).
-   **Self vs. Non-self Discrimination:** Help the immune system distinguish self from foreign entities (pathogens, transplants).

## Importance in Tissue Compatibility (Histocompatibility)

-   **Transplantation Immunology:** Essential for assessing compatibility between organ/tissue donors and recipients to minimize transplant rejection.
-   **HLA Matching & Rejection:** Differences (mismatches) in HLA alleles cause the recipient's immune system (T cells) to recognize the graft as foreign (alloantigens), leading to immune attack and rejection. Closer matches (especially for HLA-DR, -A, -B) reduce rejection risk and improve graft survival.
-   **Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT):** HLA matching is extremely critical. Mismatches can lead to severe Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD), where donor immune cells attack recipient tissues.
-   **Disease Association:** Certain HLA alleles are linked to susceptibility or resistance to specific diseases (autoimmune, infectious). HLA typing is used in genetic association studies.

## Methods of HLA Typing

-   **Serological HLA Typing (Traditional):** Uses antibodies against specific HLA antigens. Less precise than molecular methods.
-   **Molecular HLA Typing (DNA-based - Modern):** Analyzes HLA genes directly. More precise and widely used.
    -   *PCR-based Methods:* Amplify HLA genes followed by:
        -   *Sequence-Specific Oligonucleotide Probing (SSOP):* Uses labeled probes for known alleles.
        -   *Sequence-Based Typing (SBT):* Determines exact DNA sequence. Highest resolution/accuracy.
        -   *Sequence-Specific PCR (SSP):* Uses allele-specific primers.
    -   *Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) HLA Typing:* High-throughput sequencing for rapid, comprehensive, high-resolution typing. Becoming increasingly common.
