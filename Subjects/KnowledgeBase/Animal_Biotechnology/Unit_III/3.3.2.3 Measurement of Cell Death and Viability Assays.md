# 3.3.2.3 Measurement of Cell Death and Viability Assays

Measuring cell death and viability is crucial for assessing [[Animal Cell Culture]] health, evaluating treatment effects, and optimizing bioprocesses. Viability assays distinguish live/dead cells, while cell death assays detect specific mechanisms like apoptosis or necrosis. (See also [[2.2.1.2 Viable Cell Count Methods]]).

## 1. Cell Viability Assays (Membrane Integrity-Based)

Assess the integrity of the cell membrane. Live cells exclude dyes; dead/damaged cells allow entry.

-   **[[Trypan Blue]] Exclusion Assay:**
    *   **Principle:** Live cells exclude blue dye; dead cells stain blue.
    *   **Procedure:** Mix cells with Trypan Blue, count stained/unstained cells using [[Hemocytometer]] and [[1.2.3 Microscopes|microscope]]. Calculate `Viability (%) = (Live Cells / Total Cells) * 100`.
    *   **Pros:** Simple, rapid, inexpensive.
    *   **Cons:** Manual, subjective, snapshot, may underestimate if permeabilized but not lysed.
-   **[[Propidium Iodide]] (PI) Exclusion Assay ([[Flow Cytometry]] or Microscopy):**
    *   **Principle:** PI (red fluorescent DNA dye) excluded by live cells, enters dead cells with compromised membranes, intercalates DNA, fluoresces red.
    *   **Procedure:** Stain cells with PI, analyze via flow cytometry (quantifies PI-negative live vs. PI-positive dead) or fluorescence microscopy (count fluorescent dead cells).
    *   **Pros:** Quantitative (flow), can combine with other markers, objective, high throughput (flow).
    *   **Cons:** PI is potential mutagen, requires specific equipment, membrane integrity-based.

## 2. Metabolic Activity-Based Viability Assays

Measure metabolic functions indicative of live, active cells. Often rely on substrate reduction producing a detectable signal.

-   **[[MTT Assay]] (Colorimetric):**
    *   **Principle:** Measures mitochondrial reductase activity. Yellow MTT tetrazolium salt reduced to purple formazan crystals by live cells. Formazan amount proportional to viable cell number.
    *   **Procedure:** Incubate cells with MTT → Solubilize formazan crystals (DMSO/isopropanol) → Measure absorbance (570/540 nm). Calculate relative viability vs. control.
    *   **Pros:** Widely used, simple, inexpensive, quantitative (colorimetric), high-throughput possible, measures metabolic function.
    *   **Cons:** Indirect (metabolic activity), cell type/condition dependent, requires solubilization, light-sensitive reagents, relative viability.
-   **[[WST-1 Assay]] (Colorimetric):**
    *   **Principle:** Similar to MTT, but uses water-soluble tetrazolium salt (WST-1) producing a water-soluble formazan dye.
    *   **Pros over MTT:** No solubilization step needed, more convenient/faster.
-   **[[Resazurin Assay]] (Alamar Blue - Fluorescent/Colorimetric):**
    *   **Principle:** Blue, non-fluorescent resazurin reduced to pink, fluorescent resorufin by metabolically active cells. Signal proportional to viable cells.
    *   **Pros:** Highly sensitive, non-toxic, allows continuous/kinetic measurements, measurable fluorometrically (more sensitive) or colorimetrically.

## 3. Cell Death Assays (Apoptosis/Necrosis Detection)

Detect specific types of cell death.

-   **[[TUNEL Assay]] (Apoptosis Detection):**
    *   **Principle:** Detects DNA fragmentation (apoptosis hallmark) by labeling DNA strand breaks using TdT enzyme and labeled dUTP (e.g., fluorescein-dUTP).
    *   **Procedure:** Fix/permeabilize cells → Incubate with TdT/labeled dUTP → Detect labeled DNA via fluorescence microscopy or flow cytometry.
    *   **Pros:** Direct detection of DNA fragmentation, specific apoptosis marker, usable for microscopy/flow.
    *   **Cons:** Detects later-stage apoptosis, potential false positives (necrosis), requires specific reagents/equipment.
-   **[[Caspase Assays]] (Apoptosis Detection):**
    *   **Principle:** Detect activation of caspases (proteases central to apoptosis execution phase), especially caspase-3.
    *   **Types:** Colorimetric, Fluorometric, Luminescent assays using specific peptide substrates cleaved by active caspases, releasing a detectable signal.
    *   **Procedure:** Prepare cell lysates → Incubate with caspase substrate → Measure signal (absorbance, fluorescence, luminescence). Calculate activity vs. control.
    *   **Pros:** Direct measure of caspase activation, quantitative, high-throughput possible.
    *   **Cons:** Measures activity in lysates (*in vitro*), activation can occur in some non-apoptotic death.
-   **[[LDH Assay]] (Cytotoxicity/Necrosis Detection):**
    *   **Principle:** Measures release of cytosolic enzyme Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) from cells with damaged membranes (necrosis, late apoptosis).
    *   **Procedure:** Collect culture supernatant → Measure LDH activity in supernatant using colorimetric enzymatic assay. Calculate % LDH release vs. total LDH (from lysed control cells).
    *   **Pros:** Widely used cytotoxicity assay, simple, inexpensive, quantitative (colorimetric), high-throughput possible, measures membrane damage.
    *   **Cons:** Indirect measure, LDH release in necrosis & late apoptosis (doesn't distinguish), relative cytotoxicity.

**Choice of Assay:** Depends on research question, type of death investigated, sensitivity/throughput needs, equipment, cost. Often, combining viability and specific death assays (e.g., MTT + Caspase) provides a more complete picture.
