# 3.2.1 Principles of Cryopreservation

The fundamental principle of [[3.2 Cryopreservation]] is to minimize or eliminate cell damage during freezing and thawing, preserving cell viability and function. Damage primarily arises from ice crystal formation and thermal shock.

![image.png](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-III/Unit III 1a53a995df878024b76bc10d633533df/image 6.png>)

## Sources of Cryodamage

1.  **Ice Crystal Formation:**
    *   **Intracellular Ice Formation (IIF):** Ice forming *inside* cells during slow/uncontrolled freezing. Generally lethal, physically damages organelles/membranes.
        ![Fig. Ice Crystal Formation in Cryopreservation](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-III/Unit III 1a53a995df878024b76bc10d633533df/image 7.png>)
        *Fig. Ice Crystal Formation in Cryopreservation*
    *   **Extracellular Ice Formation:** Ice forming *outside* cells. Indirectly damaging via:
        *   *Solution Effects:* Solutes concentrate in the remaining unfrozen liquid, causing osmotic stress, dehydration, pH/ionic strength changes.
        *   *Membrane Dehydration/Destabilization:* Water drawn out by osmosis shrinks cells, destabilizing membranes.

2.  **Thermal Shock:**
    *   **Rapid Temperature Changes:** Occur during freezing and thawing, inducing thermal stress, affecting membrane fluidity and protein conformation.
    *   **Differential Thermal Expansion:** Different cellular components expand/contract differently, generating mechanical stress.
        ![Fig. Cryopreservation](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-III/Unit III 1a53a995df878024b76bc10d633533df/image 8.png>)
        *Fig. Cryopreservation*

## Strategies to Minimize Cryodamage

1.  **Use of [[3.2.2 Cryoprotective Agents and Methods|Cryoprotective Agents (CPAs)]]:** Substances reducing ice formation and solution effects.
    *   **Mechanism:** Lower freezing point, reduce amount of ice formed, alter ice crystal morphology (smaller, less damaging), reduce solute concentration effects, stabilize membranes/proteins.
    *   **Permeating CPAs (Intracellular):** Enter cells (e.g., [[DMSO]], [[Glycerol]], Ethylene glycol, Propylene glycol). Reduce intracellular ice, reduce solution effects inside/outside, stabilize structures.
    *   **Non-Permeating CPAs (Extracellular):** Do not readily enter cells (e.g., [[Sucrose]], [[Trehalose]], PVP, HES). Reduce extracellular solution effects, limit cell dehydration. Sugars can stabilize membranes via water replacement.
    *   **Combinations:** Often used (e.g., DMSO + Sucrose) for optimal protection.
        ![Fig. Cryoprotective Agents in Cryopreservation](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-III/Unit III 1a53a995df878024b76bc10d633533df/image 9.png>)
        *Fig. Cryoprotective Agents in Cryopreservation*

2.  **Controlled Freezing Rate:**
    *   **Principle:** Slow cooling (typically -1°C to -10°C/min) promotes extracellular ice formation, dehydrating cells and reducing lethal IIF. Allows water efflux before intracellular ice nucleation.
    *   **Equipment:** Programmable controlled-rate freezers achieve precise rates.

3.  **Rapid Thawing Rate:**
    *   **Principle:** Minimizes ice crystal recrystallization (growth of small crystals into larger damaging ones) and osmotic shock during warming. Quickly passes through critical temperature zone.
    *   **Method:** Rapid warming, typically by immersing cryovials in a 37°C water bath.

4.  **Optimal Storage Temperature:**
    *   **Ultra-low Temperatures:** Essential for long-term stability.
        *   *[[LN2]] Vapor Phase (-150°C to -196°C):* Recommended. Reduces cross-contamination/explosion risk.
        *   *[[LN2]] Liquid Phase (-196°C):* Most stable temperature, but higher contamination/explosion risk.
        *   *-80°C Freezers:* Suitable for intermediate-term storage, less stable long-term than LN2. See [[1.2.5 Refrigerators and Freezers]].

5.  **Cell Type-Specific Protocols:** Optimize CPA type/concentration, freezing/thawing rates for different cell types (stem cells, primary cells, cell lines).
