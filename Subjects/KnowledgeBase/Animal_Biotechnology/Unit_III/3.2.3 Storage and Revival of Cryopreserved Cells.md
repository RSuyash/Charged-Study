# 3.2.3 Storage and Revival of Cryopreserved Cells

Proper storage and revival techniques are crucial for maintaining the viability and function of cells after [[3.2 Cryopreservation]].

![Fig. Physical events and cryoinjury of cells during freezing and thawing...](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-III/Unit III 1a53a995df878024b76bc10d633533df/image 14.png>)
*Fig. Physical events and cryoinjury during freezing and thawing.*

## Storage of Cryopreserved Cells

-   **Ultra-low Temperature Storage:** Essential for long-term preservation.
    *   **[[LN2]] Vapor Phase (-150°C to -196°C):** Recommended. Vials stored above liquid nitrogen level. Reduces cross-contamination/explosion risk.
    *   **[[LN2]] Liquid Phase (-196°C):** Most stable temperature. Higher risk of cross-contamination/vial explosion if improperly sealed.
    *   **-80°C Deep Freezers:** Suitable for intermediate-term storage (months to years). Less stable long-term than LN2; temperature fluctuations can reduce viability over time. See [[1.2.5 Refrigerators and Freezers]].
-   **Cryovial Labeling/Record Keeping:** Crucial for sample ID/tracking. Label vials clearly (cell line, passage #, date, location). Maintain detailed records ([[2.2.1.4 Maintenance of Stock Culture and Cell Lines]]).
-   **Inventory Management System:** Robust system (database, spreadsheet) to track location/status of cryopreserved stocks.

## Revival (Thawing and Culture) of Cryopreserved Cells

1.  **Rapid Thawing:** Essential to minimize ice recrystallization and osmotic damage.
    *   **Method:** Immerse [[Cryovials]] rapidly in a 37°C water bath with gentle agitation until just thawed (typically 1-2 min).

2.  **Removal of Cryoprotective Agent (CPA):** [[3.2.2 Cryoprotective Agents and Methods|CPAs]] (especially [[DMSO]], [[Glycerol]]) must be removed to prevent toxicity. Done gradually to minimize osmotic shock.
    *   **Dilution Method (Most Common):** Slowly add pre-warmed culture medium dropwise or stepwise to the thawed cell suspension. Centrifuge cells, remove supernatant, resuspend in fresh medium.
    *   **Medium Change without Washing:** For some CPAs/cell types, simple medium change after a short recovery period might suffice.

3.  **Cell Culturing and Recovery:** Transfer thawed cells to flasks/dishes with pre-warmed fresh medium. Incubate under optimal conditions ([[2.1.3 Environmental Factors]]) for recovery and growth.

4.  **Monitoring Viability and Growth:** Assess viability immediately after thawing (e.g., [[2.2.1.2 Viable Cell Count Methods|Trypan Blue]]) and monitor growth over the next few days/passages. Initial viability may be lower but should recover.

## Factors Affecting Revival Success

-   **Cryopreservation Protocol Optimization:** Use of optimized protocols (CPA type/concentration, freezing/thawing rates) specific to the cell type.
-   **Cell Health Before Freezing:** Freezing healthy, exponentially growing cells yields better revival rates.
-   **Storage Duration/Temperature:** Long-term storage, especially at -80°C, can reduce viability. LN2 is preferred.
-   **Thawing Technique/CPA Removal:** Rapid thawing and proper CPA removal are critical.
-   **Post-Thaw Culture Conditions:** Optimal media and incubation conditions promote recovery.

Proper storage and revival are vital steps in the overall cryopreservation process to ensure the successful recovery of viable and functional cells.
