# 3.3.2.2 Measuring Parameters of Growth

Measuring cell growth parameters is essential for monitoring [[Animal Cell Culture]] performance, optimizing conditions, and characterizing cell lines during [[3.3.2 Scale-up of Cell Culture]]. Key parameters include growth curves, doubling time, viability, and density.

## 1. Growth Curves

A graphical representation of cell population growth over time (cell density/number vs. time). Provides insights into growth kinetics.

-   **Phases of Batch Growth Curve:**
    1.  **Lag Phase:** Initial adaptation, little/no growth.
    2.  **Exponential (Log) Phase:** Rapid, constant growth rate (maximum). Cells actively dividing. Target phase for production in batch cultures.
    3.  **Deceleration Phase:** Growth rate slows (nutrient depletion, waste accumulation).
    4.  **Stationary Phase:** Growth rate = 0 (division rate = death rate). Max cell density reached.
    5.  **Death Phase:** Viability declines, density decreases.
-   **Construction:** Inoculate culture → Sample at regular intervals → Measure cell density → Plot density (log or linear scale) vs. time → Analyze phases, rates.

## 2. Doubling Time (Td) or Generation Time (g)

The time required for a cell population to double during the exponential growth phase. Characterizes growth rate under specific conditions.

-   **Calculation:**
    -   From Growth Curve:
        `Td = (t₂ - t₁) * log(2) / (log(N₂) - log(N₁))`
        (Where N₁, N₂ are densities at times t₁, t₂ in exponential phase)
    -   From Specific Growth Rate (μ):
        `Td = ln(2) / μ ≈ 0.693 / μ`
        (Where μ = slope of ln(Density) vs. time during exponential phase)
-   **Factors Affecting Td:** Cell type, media, temperature, pH, oxygen, etc.

## 3. Methods for Measuring Cell Density (Cell Number/Concentration)

-   **[[Hemocytometer]] Counting (Manual):**
    *   **Principle:** Count cells manually in a grid of known volume under a [[1.2.3 Microscopes|microscope]].
    *   **Procedure:** Load diluted cell suspension into hemocytometer, count cells in defined squares, calculate density based on count, volume, dilution.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple, inexpensive, direct count, allows morphology assessment.
    *   **Limitations:** Manual, low throughput, subjective errors, less accurate for dense/clumped cultures, doesn't distinguish viability (unless using [[Trypan Blue]]).
-   **[[Automated Cell Counters]] (Electronic):**
    *   **Principle:** Electronically count cells passing an aperture (Impedance-based, e.g., Coulter Counter) or use image analysis (Automated Microscopy, e.g., Vi-CELL, Countess).
    *   **Procedure:** Load sample (potentially diluted/stained) into instrument, run automated count.
    *   **Advantages:** Rapid, automated, high throughput, objective, reproducible, provides size info (some models), viability assessment (some models).
    *   **Limitations:** Higher equipment cost, requires consumables, accuracy affected by clumps/debris, potential limitations for certain cells/media.
-   **Spectrophotometry (Turbidity Measurement):**
    *   **Principle:** Measures turbidity (cloudiness) of cell suspension via absorbance/Optical Density (OD) at a specific wavelength (e.g., OD600). Higher density = higher turbidity/OD.
    *   **Procedure:** Measure OD of diluted cell suspension against a media blank using a spectrophotometer. Requires calibration curve (OD vs. cell count) for quantitative density.
    *   **Advantages:** Rapid, simple, inexpensive estimation, non-destructive, real-time monitoring possible.
    *   **Limitations:** Indirect estimate (turbidity), affected by morphology/size/clumps/debris/media components, requires calibration for quantitative results, doesn't distinguish viability.

Choice depends on required accuracy, throughput, scale, equipment, and application. See also [[2.2.1.2 Viable Cell Count Methods]].
