# 3.3.1 Tissue Typing and Cell-Cell Interaction

Understanding tissue typing and cell-cell interactions is crucial in various fields, including transplantation immunology, [[Animal Cell Culture]] characterization, and developmental biology.

## [[HLA Typing and Tissue Compatibility]]

-   **HLA Typing (Human Leukocyte Antigen Typing):** Identifies HLA genes/molecules on cells, crucial for immune system's self/non-self recognition. HLA genes are highly polymorphic.
-   **Tissue Compatibility (Histocompatibility):** HLA matching between donor and recipient is essential in organ/tissue transplantation to minimize immune rejection. Mismatches trigger T-cell responses against the graft. Critical in Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT) to prevent Graft-versus-Host Disease (GVHD).
-   **Methods:** Serological (historical) and Molecular (DNA-based - PCR-SSOP, SBT, SSP, NGS) methods.

## [[Cell Adhesion Molecules (CAMs) and Cell-Cell Interaction]]

-   **CAMs:** Cell surface proteins mediating cell-cell and cell-ECM adhesion. Crucial for tissue organization, migration, signaling.
-   **Major Families:**
    -   *Cadherins:* Ca²⁺-dependent, homophilic adhesion (E-cadherin, N-cadherin). Form adherens junctions, desmosomes.
    -   *Immunoglobulin Superfamily (IgSF) CAMs:* Ig-like domains, homophilic or heterophilic (NCAM, ICAMs, VCAM-1). Involved in neural adhesion, leukocyte trafficking.
    -   *Integrins:* Heterodimers (α/β subunits), mediate cell-ECM adhesion (fibronectin, laminin, collagen) and some cell-cell adhesion. Crucial for migration, signaling.
    -   *Selectins:* Ca²⁺-dependent, heterophilic (bind carbohydrates). Mediate leukocyte rolling/adhesion during inflammation (E-, P-, L-selectin).
-   **Mechanisms:** Homophilic vs. Heterophilic adhesion.
-   **Cell Junctions:** CAMs form adherens junctions, desmosomes, tight junctions, gap junctions.
-   **Signaling:** CAMs mediate outside-in and inside-out signaling, influencing cell behavior.
-   **Characterization:** Immunofluorescence, Western blotting, flow cytometry, adhesion assays, inhibition studies, gene expression analysis, CRISPR gene editing.

Understanding these interactions is vital for characterizing cultured cells, ensuring they retain relevant properties, and for applications like tissue engineering.
