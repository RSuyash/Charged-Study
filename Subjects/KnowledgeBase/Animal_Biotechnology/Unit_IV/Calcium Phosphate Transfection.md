# Calcium Phosphate Transfection

Calcium phosphate transfection is a widely used [[4.1.2 Chemical Methods of Cell Transformation|chemical method]] for introducing DNA into mammalian cells, particularly for transient [[Transfection]]. It relies on forming a calcium phosphate-DNA co-precipitate that facilitates DNA uptake.

*Fig. Flowchart of the Ca2+-phosphate transfection protocol*

## Mechanism

1.  **Co-Precipitate Formation:**
    *   DNA is mixed with Calcium Chloride (CaCl₂). Ca²⁺ binds to DNA's phosphate backbone.
    *   A phosphate buffer (e.g., HEPES-buffered saline - HeBS) is added. Ca²⁺ reacts with PO₄³⁻ to form a fine calcium phosphate precipitate, trapping/adsorbing DNA. Precipitate size/morphology depends on pH, concentrations, mixing.
2.  **DNA Binding to Cell Surface:** The positively charged precipitate adheres to the negatively charged cell membrane via electrostatic interactions, increasing local DNA concentration.
3.  **DNA Uptake via Endocytosis:** Cells internalize the precipitate through endocytosis.
4.  **Lysosomal Escape & Nuclear Entry (Inefficient):**
    *   Precipitate enters endosomes, which may fuse with lysosomes (degradative). DNA must escape the endosome (mechanism unclear, possibly membrane disruption by precipitate or osmotic effects).
    *   DNA must then enter the nucleus for expression/integration. Mechanism unclear, possibly during cell division (nuclear envelope breakdown) or via active transport. Nuclear entry is a major efficiency barrier.
5.  **Transient or Stable Transformation:**
    *   *Transient:* DNA remains episomal (most common outcome).
    *   *Stable:* DNA integrates into host genome (low frequency with this method).

## Procedure

1.  **Cell Prep:** Culture healthy, actively growing mammalian cells.
2.  **Precipitate Prep:** Dilute DNA → Mix with CaCl₂ solution → Add dropwise to phosphate buffer (e.g., 2x HeBS) with gentle mixing → Incubate (20-30 min) to form fine precipitate.
3.  **Add Precipitate to Cells:** Carefully add precipitate suspension to cells in culture dishes.
4.  **Incubation:** Incubate cells with precipitate (overnight or 16-24h) in a [[1.2.2 CO2 Incubator|CO₂ incubator]] for DNA uptake.
5.  **Media Change/Recovery:** Remove precipitate-containing medium, wash cells, add fresh medium, incubate for recovery/expression.
6.  **Selection (Stable):** If stable transfection desired, apply selection (e.g., antibiotic) to isolate integrated transformants.
7.  **Screening:** Screen/assay cells for gene expression or phenotype.

## Applications

-   Transient transfection of mammalian cells (common for research).
-   Stable transfection (lower efficiency).
-   Gene function studies, protein production, reporter gene assays.

## Advantages

-   Simple and relatively inexpensive.
-   Easy to perform in standard labs.
-   Effective for transient transfection of many cell lines.
-   Can co-transfect multiple plasmids.

## Limitations

-   Relatively low efficiency (especially stable) compared to [[Viral Vectors for Gene Delivery|viral methods]] or [[Electroporation]].
-   Efficiency is cell-type dependent, requires optimization.
-   Precipitate can be cytotoxic.
-   Sensitive to pH, buffer composition, DNA quality; reproducibility can vary.
-   *In vitro* use only.
