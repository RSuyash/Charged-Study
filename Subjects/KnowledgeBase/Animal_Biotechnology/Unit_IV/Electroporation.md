# Electroporation

Electroporation is a [[4.1.1 Physical Methods of Cell Transformation|physical method]] for [[4.1 Cell Transformation|cell transformation]] that uses brief, high-voltage electrical pulses to create temporary pores in the cell membrane, making it permeable to exogenous molecules like DNA, RNA, or proteins.

![Fig. Electroporation](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-IV/Unit IV 1a53a995df87806c8040f81a42fcedc5/image 1.png>)
*Fig. Electroporation*

## Mechanism

1.  **Electric Field Application:** Cells suspended in conductive buffer with DNA are placed between electrodes in an electroporation cuvette. A short, intense electrical pulse (ms to µs) is applied using an electroporator.
2.  **Membrane Polarization & Pore Formation:** The electric field induces a voltage difference across the membrane, causing polarization and formation of transient aqueous pores in the lipid bilayer.
3.  **DNA Entry:** DNA molecules enter the cell through these pores via diffusion and electrophoretic forces.
4.  **Membrane Resealing:** Pores reseal spontaneously after the pulse.
5.  **Integration or Transient Expression:** Internalized DNA can integrate into the host genome (stable transformation, low frequency) or remain episomal (transient expression).

## Procedure

1.  **Cell Preparation:** Harvest cells, wash, resuspend in low ionic strength electroporation buffer (chilled). Adjust density.
2.  **DNA Preparation:** Purified plasmid or linear DNA.
3.  **Mixing:** Mix DNA with cell suspension in an electroporation cuvette.
4.  **Pulse Delivery:** Place cuvette in electroporator, deliver optimized pulse (voltage, length, number, shape vary by cell type/instrument).
5.  **Recovery:** Immediately transfer cells to nutrient-rich recovery medium, incubate.
6.  **Selection/Screening:** Apply selection (e.g., antibiotics) for stable transformants or screen for transient expression.

## Applications

-   Transformation of diverse cell types (bacteria, yeast, fungi, plant, mammalian), including difficult-to-transform cells.
-   Stable transformation (plasmid DNA).
-   Transient gene expression or gene editing (delivery of mRNA, siRNA, CRISPR components).
-   Drug delivery and gene therapy (*in vitro* / *in vivo*).

## Advantages

-   Broad host range.
-   Relatively high efficiency for some cell types.
-   Simple, rapid procedure.
-   Versatile (DNA, RNA, proteins).
-   Applicable for stable and transient transformation.

## Limitations

-   Requires specialized equipment (electroporator, cuvettes).
-   Can affect cell viability (optimization crucial).
-   Efficiency varies by cell type, DNA quality, parameters.
-   May be less efficient for very large DNA.
-   Pore formation is transient.
