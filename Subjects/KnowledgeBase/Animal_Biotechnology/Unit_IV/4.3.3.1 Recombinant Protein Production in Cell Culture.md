# 4.3.3.1 Recombinant Protein Production in Cell Culture

Producing recombinant proteins (proteins encoded by recombinant DNA introduced into host cells) is a major application of [[4.3.3 Engineered Cell Culture as a Source of Valuable Products|engineered cell culture]]. It's used for [[4.3.3.2 Biopharmaceuticals and Therapeutic Proteins from Cell Culture|therapeutic proteins]], industrial enzymes, and research reagents.

## Host Cell Systems

Various systems are used, each with pros and cons:

1.  **Mammalian Cell Culture ([[CHO Cells]], [[HEK293 Cells]], NS0, etc.):**
    *   **Dominant for Therapeutics:** Especially [[CHO Cells]] for [[Monoclonal Antibodies (mAbs)]], enzymes, growth factors.
    *   **Advantages:** Proper protein folding & post-translational modifications (PTMs) like glycosylation (often human-like, reducing immunogenicity), efficient secretion simplifies purification, established regulatory pathway.
    *   **Limitations:** Slower growth/lower yields vs. microbes, higher costs (complex media, long fermentation), complex genetic manipulation, susceptible to viral/mycoplasma contamination.

2.  **Insect Cell Culture (Sf9, Sf21):**
    *   **Baculovirus Expression Vector System (BEVS):** Widely used for complex proteins, [[Virus-Like Particle (VLP) Vaccines|VLPs]].
    *   **Advantages:** High expression levels (BEVS), proper folding & some PTMs (glycosylation differs from mammalian but can be engineered), faster production than mammalian, cost-effective vs. mammalian, scalable.
    *   **Limitations:** Primarily transient expression (baculovirus is lytic), different glycosylation patterns (potential immunogenicity concern for some therapeutics), less suitable for stable lines.

3.  **Yeast Cell Culture (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, *Pichia pastoris*):**
    *   **Microbial Eukaryotic System:** Combines microbial speed/scalability with some eukaryotic PTM capabilities.
    *   ***Pichia pastoris*:** Popular due to strong inducible promoter (AOX1), high expression, secretion, some PTMs.
    *   ***Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (Baker's Yeast):** Robust, well-characterized, GRAS status.
    *   **Advantages:** Faster growth/higher yields vs. mammalian, cost-effective vs. mammalian, scalable, some eukaryotic PTMs, GRAS status (*S. cerevisiae*).
    *   **Limitations:** Different glycosylation patterns (may require glyco-engineering for human therapeutics), folding/secretion less efficient for some complex proteins vs. mammalian, potential proteolytic degradation.

4.  **Bacterial Cell Culture (*E. coli*, *Bacillus subtilis*):**
    *   **Prokaryotic System:** *E. coli* most widely used due to fast growth, high density, well-known genetics, easy manipulation. *Bacillus subtilis* used for secretion.
    *   **Advantages:** Fastest growth/highest yields, simplest/cheapest media & fermentation, well-established tools, very scalable.
    *   **Limitations:** **No eukaryotic PTMs (no glycosylation)** - major limitation for many therapeutics, protein folding challenges (often forms insoluble inclusion bodies), intracellular production common (*E. coli* - requires cell disruption), endotoxin (LPS) contamination (*E. coli* - requires removal), *Bacillus* lacks endotoxin.

## General Production Process

1.  **Gene Cloning/Vector Construction:** Clone gene into suitable expression vector (promoter, signal sequence, marker).
2.  **[[4.1 Cell Transformation|Cell Transformation/Transfection]]:** Introduce vector into host cells.
3.  **Cell Line Selection/Screening (Stable Lines):** Select using markers (e.g., antibiotic resistance), screen clones for high/stable expression.
4.  **Cell Culture/[[Fermentation Process|Fermentation]]:** Culture selected cells in [[Bioreactors]] under optimized conditions (fed-batch/continuous often used).
5.  **Harvesting/[[Cell Disruption]] (if intracellular):** Harvest cells; disrupt if product is inside.
6.  **[[Purification of Products|Protein Purification (Downstream Processing)]]:** Isolate/purify protein from lysate or supernatant using [[Chromatography]], [[Membrane Processes]], etc.
7.  **Formulation/[[Quality Control and Testing|Quality Control]]:** Formulate purified protein (liquid/lyophilized), perform rigorous QC tests (purity, activity, stability, safety).

The choice of host system is critical and depends on the specific protein, required PTMs, scale, cost, and regulatory considerations.
