# 4.1.2 Chemical Methods of Cell Transformation

Chemical methods for [[4.1 Cell Transformation|cell transformation]] (often called **transfection** for mammalian cells) use chemical agents to alter cell membrane permeability and facilitate the entry of exogenous genetic material (DNA). These methods are widely used due to relative simplicity and cost-effectiveness.

Key chemical methods include:

-   [[Calcium Phosphate Transfection]]
-   [[DEAE-Dextran Transfection]]

Other chemical methods not detailed in the source but relevant include:
-   **Lipofection (Lipid-Mediated Transfection):** Uses cationic lipid formulations (e.g., Lipofectamine) that form complexes with negatively charged DNA. These lipid-DNA complexes (lipoplexes) fuse with the cell membrane or are taken up via endocytosis, releasing DNA into the cytoplasm. Highly efficient for many mammalian cell types.
-   **Cationic Polymers:** Uses positively charged polymers (e.g., Polyethylenimine - PEI) to condense DNA into positively charged nanoparticles (polyplexes). These polyplexes bind to the cell surface and are internalized via endocytosis.

Chemical methods primarily result in transient expression, although stable transformation can occur at lower frequencies. Efficiency varies significantly depending on the cell type, chemical agent, DNA quality, and protocol optimization.
