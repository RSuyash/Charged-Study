# Microinjection

Microinjection is a [[4.1.1 Physical Methods of Cell Transformation|physical method]] involving the direct injection of DNA (or other molecules) into the cytoplasm or nucleus of a single cell using a fine glass micropipette. It is a precise, direct delivery method.

![Fig. Microinjection](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-IV/Unit IV 1a53a995df87806c8040f81a42fcedc5/image 2.png>)
*Fig. Microinjection*

## Mechanism

1.  **Cell Immobilization/Visualization:** Individual cells immobilized (slide/dish), visualized under high-magnification [[1.2.3 Microscopes|microscope]]. Micromanipulators position cell/pipette.
2.  **Micropipette Prep/Loading:** Fine glass micropipette (sub-µm tip) prepared (pipette puller), loaded with DNA solution.
3.  **Cell Penetration/Injection:** Micropipette precisely inserted through cell membrane (and nuclear membrane for nuclear injection) using micromanipulators. Picoliter-femtoliter volume of DNA solution injected using pressure system.
4.  **Cell Recovery/Culture:** Microinjected cells transferred to culture dish with medium for recovery/growth.
5.  **Selection/Screening:** Apply selection for stable transformants or screen for desired phenotype/expression.

## Variations

-   **Cytoplasmic Microinjection:** DNA injected into cytoplasm. Simpler, often used for transient expression.
-   **Nuclear Microinjection:** DNA injected directly into nucleus. More challenging, higher efficiency for stable transformation/integration. Preferred for transgenesis, gene editing.

## Applications

-   **Production of Transgenic Animals (Pronuclear Microinjection):** Historically used; DNA injected into zygote pronucleus.
-   **Transformation of Large Cells:** Well-suited for oocytes, eggs, embryos where other methods are less efficient/toxic.
-   **Delivery of Macromolecules:** Delivers proteins, RNA, CRISPR components poorly taken up by other methods.
-   **Single-Cell Transformation/Gene Editing:** Precise manipulation of individual cells.
-   **Fundamental Research:** Study gene function, regulation, signaling, development by manipulating individual cells.

## Advantages

-   Direct, precise delivery, high control.
-   High efficiency for certain cells (especially nuclear injection for stable transformation).
-   Applicable to large/difficult-to-transform cells.
-   Versatile (DNA, RNA, proteins).
-   Single-cell capability.

## Limitations

-   Technically challenging, labor-intensive, requires skilled operators/expertise.
-   Low throughput (individual cells). Not suitable for large scale.
-   Requires specialized, expensive equipment (micromanipulators, injectors, microscopes).
-   Can cause cell damage/death.
-   Random DNA integration (low frequency for stable transformation unless combined with targeted editing).
