# 4.1 Cell Transformation

**Cell transformation** (in the context of genetic modification, distinct from [[3.1.3 Transformed Cell Lines|cancerous transformation]]) is the process of introducing exogenous genetic material (DNA or RNA) into a cell, resulting in a stable or transient genetic alteration. This is fundamental to [[Animal Cell Culture]] and biotechnology for studying gene function, producing recombinant proteins, creating engineered cell lines, and developing gene therapies.

![Fig. Bacterial transformation techniques...](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-IV/Unit IV 1a53a995df87806c8040f81a42fcedc5/image.png>)
*Fig. Bacterial transformation techniques (illustrative of general concepts)*

Transformation can be achieved through various methods:

-   [[4.1.1 Physical Methods of Cell Transformation]] (e.g., [[Electroporation]], [[Microinjection]])
-   [[4.1.2 Chemical Methods of Cell Transformation]] (e.g., [[Calcium Phosphate Transfection]], [[DEAE-Dextran Transfection]])
-   [[4.1.3 Biological Methods of Cell Transformation]] (e.g., [[Viral Vectors for Gene Delivery]])

The introduced genetic material can either integrate into the host genome (stable transformation) or remain episomal (transient transformation/expression).
