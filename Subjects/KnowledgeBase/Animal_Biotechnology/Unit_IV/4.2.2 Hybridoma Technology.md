# 4.2.2 Hybridoma Technology

Hybridoma technology, developed by Köhler and Milstein (1975), is a technique for producing [[Monoclonal Antibodies (mAbs)]] - antibodies derived from a single B-cell clone, all recognizing the same epitope. It involves fusing antibody-producing B cells with immortal myeloma cells.

![Fig. A general representation of the hybridoma method used to produce monoclonal antibodies.](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-IV/Unit IV 1a53a995df87806c8040f81a42fcedc5/image 8.png>)
*Fig. General representation of hybridoma method*

## Principle

Combines properties of two cell types:
1.  **Antibody-Producing B Lymphocytes (B Cells):** From an immunized animal (e.g., mouse). Produce specific antibodies but have a finite lifespan in culture.
2.  **Immortal Myeloma Cells:** Cancerous plasma cells that grow indefinitely *in vitro*. Crucially, they are genetically modified to lack enzymes like **HGPRT** (Hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase) or **TK** (Thymidine kinase), making them unable to use nucleotide salvage pathways.

## [[4.2.2.1 Production of Monoclonal Antibodies (mAbs) using Hybridoma Technology|Production Process]]

1.  **Immunization:** Animal (mouse) immunized with the target antigen to stimulate specific B cell production.
2.  **Isolation of Spleen Cells:** Spleen harvested, rich in B cells.
3.  **Myeloma Cell Culture:** HGPRT⁻ or TK⁻ myeloma cells cultured.
4.  **[[4.2.1 Cell Fusion Methods (Chemical and Viral)|Cell Fusion]]:** Spleen cells fused with myeloma cells using [[PEG]] or [[Sendai Virus]]. Creates a mix of fused (hybridomas, B-B, Myeloma-Myeloma) and unfused cells.
5.  **Selection in HAT Medium:** Fused cells cultured in **HAT medium** (Hypoxanthine, Aminopterin, Thymidine).
    *   *Aminopterin:* Blocks *de novo* nucleotide synthesis.
    *   *Hybridoma Cells:* Survive. Inherit immortality (myeloma) + functional HGPRT/TK (B cell) allowing use of salvage pathway.
    *   *Unfused Myeloma Cells:* Die. Lack HGPRT/TK, cannot use salvage pathway when *de novo* is blocked.
    *   *Unfused B Cells:* Die. Have finite lifespan.
    *   *Other Hybrids:* Eventually die out or are outcompeted.
6.  **Limiting Dilution & Cloning:** Surviving hybridomas diluted to isolate single cells/clones in microtiter wells. Ensures monoclonality.
7.  **Screening:** Clones screened for production of the desired antibody using assays like [[ELISA]], Immunofluorescence, Western Blotting.
8.  **Scale-up & Production:** Positive clones expanded for large-scale mAb production:
    *   *In Vitro Culture:* [[Bioreactors]] ([[Suspension Cultures]] or [[Monolayer Cultures]]). Preferred method.
    *   *In Vivo Ascites Production:* (Historical/less common due to ethics) Injecting hybridomas into mouse peritoneal cavity, collecting antibody-rich ascites fluid.
9.  **Purification:** mAbs purified from culture supernatant or ascites using [[Chromatography]] (Protein A/G affinity, IEX, SEC).

## [[4.2.2.2 Applications of Hybridoma Technology|Applications]]

Hybridoma technology revolutionized diagnostics, therapeutics, and research through the production of mAbs.
