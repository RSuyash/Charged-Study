# 4.1.1 Physical Methods of Cell Transformation

Physical methods for [[4.1 Cell Transformation|cell transformation]] use physical forces to create transient pores or openings in the cell membrane, allowing exogenous genetic material (DNA, RNA, etc.) to enter the cell. These methods are generally less cell-type specific than chemical or biological methods.

Key physical methods include:

-   [[Electroporation]]
-   [[Microinjection]]

Other physical methods not detailed in the source but relevant include:
-   **Gene Gun (Biolistics):** Uses high pressure to shoot DNA-coated microparticles (gold or tungsten) directly into cells. Often used for plant cell transformation.
-   **Sonoporation:** Uses ultrasound waves to create transient pores in cell membranes.
-   **Laserporation:** Uses focused laser beams to create transient pores.

These methods bypass the need for chemical reagents or viral vectors, offering alternatives for delivering genetic material into various cell types.
