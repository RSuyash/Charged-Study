# 4.2.1 Cell Fusion Methods (Chemical and Viral)

[[4.2 Cell Fusion and Hybridoma Technology|Cell fusion]] can be induced by various chemical and biological (viral) methods to create hybrid cells (heterokaryons or synkaryons).

## 1. Chemical Methods

-   **[[PEG]]-Mediated Cell Fusion:**
    *   **Principle:** Polyethylene glycol (PEG, e.g., PEG 1500 or 4000) is the most common chemical fusogen. It promotes cell agglutination (aggregation) by dehydration/reducing repulsion and destabilizes membranes, facilitating fusion.
        ![Fig. Polyethylene Glycol (PEG)-Mediated Cell Fusion](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-IV/Unit IV 1a53a995df87806c8040f81a42fcedc5/image 6.png>)
        *Fig. PEG-Mediated Cell Fusion*
    *   **Technique:** Mix/pellet cells → Add concentrated PEG solution (30-50%) slowly → Incubate briefly (1-2 min) → Gradually dilute out PEG with medium → Wash and culture cells.
    *   **Applications:** [[4.2.2 Hybridoma Technology]], generating hybrid lines, research.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple, widely applicable, efficient for many cell types, inexpensive.
    *   **Limitations:** Variable efficiency, cytotoxic at high conc./long exposure, random/non-specific fusion, hybrids often unstable (chromosome loss), requires selection.
-   **Other Chemical Fusogens (Less Common):**
    *   *Calcium Ions (Ca²⁺) and Alkaline pH:* Promote fusion, especially in plant protoplasts.
    *   *Fatty Acids (Oleic Acid):* Can induce fusion in certain cells by altering membrane fluidity.

## 2. Viral Methods

Utilize fusogenic viruses (typically inactivated) to induce fusion. Often more efficient than chemical methods for certain cells.

-   **Inactivated [[Sendai Virus]]-Mediated Cell Fusion:**
    *   **Principle:** Sendai virus (paramyxovirus) is naturally fusogenic. Inactivated virus (UV or β-propiolactone treated) retains fusion activity via its F (fusion) protein.
    *   **Mechanism:** Virus adsorbs to cells, agglutinates them → Viral F protein promotes membrane fusion between adjacent cells → Heterokaryon formation.
        ![Fig. Exogenous viruses and HERVs can hijack physiological fusogens...](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-IV/Unit IV 1a53a995df87806c8040f81a42fcedc5/image 7.png>)
        *Fig. Viral Fusion Mechanism Concept*
    *   **Technique:** Prepare inactivated Sendai virus → Mix cells and incubate with virus at low temp (4°C) for adsorption/agglutination → Warm to 37°C to activate fusion → Culture and select cells.
    *   **Applications:** [[4.2.2 Hybridoma Technology]] (common), creating syncytia, cell engineering.
    *   **Advantages:** Efficient, good for cells resistant to chemical fusion, can create syncytia.
    *   **Limitations:** Requires virus prep/handling (biosafety), virus preps can be expensive, potential cytotoxicity, random/non-specific fusion, hybrids often unstable, requires selection.
-   **Other Fusogenic Viruses (Less Common):** Newcastle Disease Virus (NDV), Reovirus.

## Selection of Method

Depends on:
-   **Cell Type:** Responsiveness to PEG vs. virus.
-   **Efficiency Needed:** Viral methods often more efficient.
-   **Application:** Hybridoma (PEG or Sendai), Syncytia (Viral).
-   **Cost/Availability:** PEG is cheaper/simpler.
-   **Biosafety:** Viral methods require handling viruses.
