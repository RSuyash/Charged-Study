# 4.3.1 Vaccine Production Using Cell Culture

[[4.3 Applications of Engineered Cell Culture|Cell culture-based vaccine production]] has revolutionized manufacturing, offering advantages over traditional methods (live animals, eggs). It provides a controlled, scalable, and safer platform, especially for viral and subunit vaccines.

![Fig. Schematic flowchart of the upstream and downstream production process of the cell-based vaccine](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-IV/Unit IV 1a53a995df87806c8040f81a42fcedc5/image 11.png>)
*Fig. Schematic flowchart of cell-based vaccine production*

## Advantages

-   **Improved Safety:**
    *   Reduced risk of animal-derived contaminants (pathogens, proteins).
    *   No risk of egg allergies (relevant for influenza vaccines).
    *   Reduced risk of zoonotic disease transmission (closed systems).
-   **Enhanced Scalability/Capacity:**
    *   Readily scaled up in [[Bioreactors]] for large quantities.
    *   Faster production time possible (esp. vs. egg-based flu vaccines), enabling rapid pandemic response.
-   **Improved Quality/Consistency:**
    *   Controlled environment ensures consistent quality, potency, purity.
    *   Reduced batch-to-batch variability compared to animal-based systems.
-   **Flexibility/Adaptability:**
    *   Adaptable for various vaccine types (viral, subunit, VLP, nucleic acid).
    *   Rapid adaptation for emerging pathogens.

## Cell Lines Used

Selected based on ability to support virus replication/protein expression, scalability:

-   **Mammalian Cell Lines:**
    *   *[[Vero Cells]] (African Green Monkey Kidney):* Widely used (IPV, measles, rubella, rotavirus). Continuous, well-characterized.
    *   *[[CHO Cells]] (Chinese Hamster Ovary):* Primarily for recombinant proteins/mAbs, but also explored for viral vaccines. Robust, high-density culture.
    *   *Human Cell Lines:*
        *   *PER.C6 (Human Embryonic Retinoblast):* Used for adenoviral vector vaccines. May be preferred for human vaccines.
        *   *[[HEK293 Cells]] (Human Embryonic Kidney):* Used for adenoviral/lentiviral vector production (gene therapy, vaccines).
-   **Insect Cell Lines:**
    *   *Sf9, Sf21 (*Spodoptera frugiperda*):* Used with Baculovirus Expression Vector System (BEVS) for recombinant protein/VLP vaccines. Cost-effective, high expression.
-   **Avian Cell Lines:**
    *   *CEF (Chicken Embryo Fibroblast):* Historically used (measles, mumps).
    *   *Duck Embryo-Derived (e.g., AGE1.CR.pCELL®):* Used for influenza vaccines (alternative to eggs).

## Types of Vaccines Produced

-   **Viral Vaccines:**
    *   *Inactivated:* Virus grown in cells, then killed (IPV, inactivated flu, rabies).
    *   *Live Attenuated:* Weakened virus grown in cells (MMR, rotavirus).
    *   *[[Viral Vectors for Gene Delivery|Viral Vector]]:* Recombinant vectors (Adeno, AAV) produced in cells deliver antigen genes (e.g., some COVID-19 vaccines).
    *   *Virus-Like Particle (VLP):* Non-infectious particles resembling viruses, produced via recombinant expression (e.g., HPV, Hep B - often in yeast/insect cells).
-   **Subunit Vaccines (Recombinant Protein):**
    *   Genes encoding antigens expressed in cell culture (mammalian, insect, yeast, bacteria). Purified protein used as vaccine (e.g., Hep B - yeast, acellular pertussis - bacteria).

## Examples

-   IPV (Vero)
-   MMR (Vero, CEF)
-   Rotavirus (Vero)
-   Cell-based Influenza (MDCK, Vero, PER.C6)
-   HPV (VLP in Yeast)
-   Hepatitis B (Recombinant HBsAg in Yeast)
-   Adenoviral COVID-19 Vaccines (HEK293, PER.C6)

Cell culture has significantly improved vaccine manufacturing safety, scalability, and consistency.
