# 4.1.3 Biological Methods of Cell Transformation

Biological methods for [[4.1 Cell Transformation|cell transformation]] utilize biological agents, primarily viruses, to deliver genetic material into cells. These methods, often referred to as **transduction** when using viruses, leverage the natural efficiency of viruses for cell entry and gene delivery.

The primary biological method involves using [[Viral Vectors for Gene Delivery]].

## Viral Vectors

-   **Principle:** Engineered viruses (replication-defective for safety) are used as vehicles to carry therapeutic or target genes into host cells. They exploit natural viral infection mechanisms.
-   **Process:** The gene of interest replaces essential viral genes. The vector genome is packaged into viral particles using helper plasmids or packaging cell lines that provide the missing viral proteins *in trans*. These particles can infect target cells and deliver the genetic payload, but cannot replicate further within the target cell.
-   **Types:** Different viral vectors have distinct characteristics regarding integration, tropism, packaging capacity, and immunogenicity. Common types include:
    -   [[Retroviral Vectors]] (including [[Lentiviral Vectors]])
    -   [[Adenoviral Vectors]]
    -   [[Adeno-Associated Viral (AAV) Vectors]]

Biological methods, especially using lentiviral and AAV vectors, are highly efficient for gene delivery *in vitro* and *in vivo*, forming the basis for many [[Gene Therapy Applications]].
