# Viral Vectors for Gene Delivery

Viral vectors are engineered viruses used as vehicles in [[4.1.3 Biological Methods of Cell Transformation]] to safely and efficiently deliver genes into target cells. They exploit natural viral mechanisms for cell entry and gene delivery but are typically made **replication-defective** for safety.

## Types of Viral Vectors

1.  **[[Retroviral Vectors]] (e.g., MLV-based, [[Lentiviral Vectors]]):**
    *   **Mechanism:** RNA viruses; genome reverse transcribed to DNA and **integrates stably** into host genome (provirus). Replication-defective vectors packaged in packaging cells.
    *   **Types:**
        *   *MLV-based:* Infect **dividing cells** preferentially. Efficient stable gene transfer. Risk of insertional mutagenesis.
        *   *Lentiviral (HIV-1-based):* Infect **dividing and non-dividing cells**. Widely used in gene therapy (esp. stem cells). Engineered with safety features (e.g., SIN vectors) to reduce risks.
    *   **Advantages:** Efficient stable integration, long-term expression. Lentiviruses infect non-dividing cells. Broad tropism.
    *   **Limitations:** Insertional mutagenesis risk (reduced with SIN), potential immunogenicity, limited packaging capacity (~8-10 kb), complex/costly production, biosafety concerns.

2.  **[[Adenoviral Vectors]]:**
    *   **Mechanism:** DNA viruses; genome enters nucleus but **does not integrate** (remains episomal). Replication-defective vectors packaged in packaging cells.
    *   **Characteristics:** High transduction efficiency (dividing & non-dividing cells), high-level **transient** gene expression (days to weeks), broad tropism.
    *   **Applications:** Gene therapy (transient expression), vaccine development (widely used), cancer therapy (oncolytic adenoviruses), transient protein production.
    *   **Advantages:** High efficiency/expression, non-integrating (no insertional mutagenesis risk), large packaging capacity (~8 kb), relatively easy high-titer production.
    *   **Limitations:** Transient expression, pre-existing immunity in humans (common cold virus) reduces efficiency and causes immune responses *in vivo*, potential immunogenicity/inflammation, risk of replication-competent adenovirus (RCA) contamination during production.

3.  **[[Adeno-Associated Viral (AAV) Vectors]]:**
    *   **Mechanism:** Small, non-pathogenic DNA parvoviruses. Replication-defective (require helper virus like adenovirus for replication). Vector genome can persist **episomally** or integrate at low frequency (often randomly for recombinant vectors, though WT AAV2 integrates site-specifically). Packaged using helper virus functions. Self-complementary AAV (scAAV) vectors have dsDNA genome for faster expression.
    *   **Characteristics:** High safety profile (non-pathogenic, low immunogenicity), broad tropism (different serotypes target different tissues), long-term gene expression potential (episomal persistence in non-dividing cells or integration).
    *   **Applications:** Leading vector for *in vivo* [[Gene Therapy Applications|gene therapy]] (hemophilia, SMA, LCA), vaccine development, CNS gene delivery.
    *   **Advantages:** High safety, broad/tunable tropism, long-term expression, infects non-dividing cells, relatively low immunogenicity.
    *   **Limitations:** Small packaging capacity (~4.7 kb), complex/costly high-titer production, pre-existing immunity can be an issue *in vivo* (serotype selection helps), primarily episomal persistence in dividing cells.

## Selection Criteria

Choice depends on:
-   **Stable vs. Transient Expression:** Integrating (Retro/Lenti) vs. Non-integrating/Episomal (Adeno, AAV).
-   **Target Cell Type:** Dividing vs. Non-dividing (Lenti, Adeno, AAV preferred for non-dividing).
-   **Packaging Capacity:** AAV < Adeno ≈ Retro/Lenti.
-   **Safety/Immunogenicity:** AAV generally considered safest/least immunogenic for *in vivo*. Adeno can be immunogenic. Retro/Lenti have insertional mutagenesis risk (mitigated by design).
-   **Production/Cost:** Adeno/MLV often easier/cheaper than Lenti/AAV.

Viral vectors are powerful tools for gene delivery in research, [[Vaccine Production Using Cell Culture|vaccine development]], and gene therapy.
