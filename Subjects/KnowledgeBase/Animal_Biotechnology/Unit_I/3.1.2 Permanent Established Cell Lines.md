# 3.1.2 Permanent/Established Cell Lines

## Definition

Permanent cell lines, also known as established or continuous cell lines, are [[Animal Cell Culture|cell cultures]] that have acquired **immortality**, meaning they can proliferate indefinitely *in vitro* and do not undergo senescence. They are often derived from tumors or transformed *in vitro*, overcoming the [[3.1.1 Primary Cell Lines|Hayflick limit]].

## Characteristics

-   **Immortalized (Infinite Lifespan):** Can be passaged indefinitely, providing a continuous, renewable cell source. Escaped senescence.
-   **Aneuploid Karyotype (Typically):** Often have abnormal chromosome number/structure (aneuploidy). Genetically less stable than primary lines, can accumulate changes over time.
-   **Cellular Homogeneity (Relatively):** Generally more uniform population than primary lines, though phenotypic drift can occur over extended passages.
-   **Anchorage Dependence or Suspension Growth:** Some are anchorage-dependent ([[Monolayer Cultures]]), others are adapted for suspension culture ([[Suspension Cultures]]), useful for large-scale biomanufacturing.
-   **Altered *In Vivo* Characteristics:** Often deviate significantly from normal cells *in vivo* (morphology, gene expression, growth rate, differentiation capacity). Less physiologically representative than primary cells.
-   **Easier to Culture/Maintain:** Generally simpler growth requirements, faster growth rates, higher plating efficiency, more robust than primary cells.
-   **Widely Available/Characterized:** Many available from cell banks (e.g., ATCC, ECACC) with well-documented properties.

## Advantages

-   **Infinite Lifespan:** Continuous, renewable cell source, avoids repeated tissue isolation.
-   **Cellular Homogeneity:** More consistent cell populations lead to more reproducible results.
-   **Easier to Culture:** Robust, fast-growing, simpler requirements, easier for routine/large-scale use.
-   **Widely Available/Characterized:** Reduces need for *de novo* establishment/characterization.
-   **Lower Cost (Long-term):** More cost-effective for long-term research/large-scale use compared to repeated primary cell isolation.

## Limitations

-   **Altered *In Vivo* Characteristics:** Less physiologically relevant, may not accurately model normal cell biology or complex *in vivo* processes.
-   **Aneuploidy/Genetic Instability:** Can lead to phenotypic drift and variability, affecting reproducibility.
-   **Potential Transformation Artifacts:** May exhibit characteristics of transformed cells (altered growth regulation, tumorigenicity) not representative of normal cells. See [[3.1.3 Transformed Cell Lines]].
-   **Contamination/Misidentification Risks:** Susceptible to microbial contamination, cross-contamination with other cell lines, and misidentification. Requires rigorous cell line authentication and quality control.

## Examples

-   **HeLa Cells:** Oldest, widely used human cell line (cervical cancer). Used in cancer research, cell biology, virology.
-   **CHO Cells (Chinese Hamster Ovary):** Workhorse for industrial production of recombinant therapeutic proteins/antibodies.
-   **Vero Cells:** African green monkey kidney cells. Used for vaccine production, virus propagation, toxicology.
-   **NIH-3T3 Cells:** Mouse fibroblast line. Used in cell biology, transformation assays, cancer research.
-   **Hybridoma Cell Lines:** Fused B lymphocytes and myeloma cells. Used for [[Monoclonal Antibody Production]]. See [[4.2.2 Hybridoma Technology]].
