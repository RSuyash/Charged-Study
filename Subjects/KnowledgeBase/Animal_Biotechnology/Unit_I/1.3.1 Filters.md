# 1.3.1 Filters

Filters are crucial materials in [[Animal Cell Culture]], vital for maintaining sterility, purifying media/reagents, and separating cellular components. They are typically membrane filters (defined pore sizes) or depth filters (fibrous materials). Sterility and non-pyrogenicity are paramount for filters used in sterile applications.

## Types of Filters Used in Animal Cell Culture

1.  **Sterile Filters (Sterilizing Filters):**
    *   **Purpose:** Remove bacteria, fungi, microorganisms from media, reagents, buffers to ensure [[Aseptic Technique]]. Essential for preparing sterile media.
    *   **Pore Size:** Standard is **0.22 µm** (removes bacteria/most fungi). 0.1 µm or smaller may be used for mycoplasma removal.
    *   **Types:**
        *   *Syringe Filters:* Small, disposable, for small volumes (mLs). Point-of-use sterilization.
        *   *Bottle-Top Filters:* Screw onto culture bottles/flasks for sterilizing larger volumes (up to 1-2 L) directly into the vessel.
        *   *Vacuum Filter Units:* Funnel top, receiving flask, vacuum source. For larger volumes (hundreds of mL to L). Faster than gravity.
        *   *In-line Filters (Capsule, Cartridge):* For integration into tubing/fluid handling systems (large-scale bioprocessing). Capsule filters are self-contained disposable units. Cartridge filters fit into housings.
    *   **Materials:**
        *   *Membrane Materials:*
            *   Cellulose Acetate (CA): Low protein binding, general aqueous use.
            *   Mixed Cellulose Esters (MCE): Similar to CA.
            *   Polyethersulfone (PES): Low protein binding, high flow rates, good chemical resistance. Often preferred for cell culture media.
            *   Polyvinylidene Fluoride (PVDF): Low protein binding, good chemical resistance, for proteins/organic solvents.
        *   *Housing Materials:* Polystyrene, polycarbonate, etc. (biocompatible, sterile, non-pyrogenic).
    *   **Sterilization/Quality:** Pre-sterilized (gamma irradiation or EtO), single-use, certified sterile, non-pyrogenic, often low protein binding.

2.  **Non-Sterile Filters (Pre-filters, Clarification Filters):**
    *   **Purpose:** Clarification, pre-filtration, removal of particulates, cell debris, precipitates *before* sterilization or further purification. Protect sterilizing filters from clogging.
    *   **Pore Size:** Larger than sterilizing filters (0.45 µm to several µm, e.g., 1 µm, 5 µm, 10 µm). Depends on particle size to be removed.
    *   **Types:**
        *   *Depth Filters:* Fibrous/granular (cellulose, glass fibers, diatomaceous earth). For turbid solutions, high particulate load. Trap particles within the matrix.
        *   *Syringe Filters (Non-Sterile):* For clarifying small volumes or analytical samples.
        *   *Cartridge Filters (Non-Sterile):* Larger cartridges for pre-filtering larger volumes.
    *   **Materials:** Cellulose, glass fibers, polypropylene, nylon, etc.

3.  **Air Filters (Vent Filters, Gas Filters):**
    *   **Purpose:** Filter air/gases entering/exiting culture vessels, bioreactors, incubators to prevent microbial contamination while allowing gas exchange/pressure equalization.
    *   **Types:**
        *   *[[HEPA Filtration|HEPA Filters]]:* Sterilize air, remove airborne particles/microorganisms for sterile areas, [[1.2.1 Laminar Flow Hoods]], [[1.2.2 CO2 Incubator]]. Remove ≥ 99.97% of 0.3 µm particles.
        *   *Hydrophobic Membrane Filters:* Vent filters for flasks/bottles/bioreactors. Allow gas exchange, prevent liquid/microbial passage. Materials like PTFE, PVDF prevent wetting.
        *   *Activated Carbon Filters (Optional):* Used with HEPA filters to remove odors/volatile chemicals.
    *   **Materials:**
        *   *HEPA Media:* Glass or synthetic fibers.
        *   *Hydrophobic Membrane Media:* PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene), PVDF.
        *   *Housing Materials:* Polystyrene, polycarbonate, polypropylene (autoclavable plastics).

## Key Features and Considerations When Selecting Filters

-   **Sterility (for Sterile Filtration):** Ensure certified sterile, non-pyrogenic, suitable for cell culture.
-   **Pore Size:** Select based on application (0.22 µm for sterilization, larger for clarification).
-   **Filter Material and Compatibility:** Choose materials compatible with solutions (aqueous, organic, protein, media). Consider chemical resistance, protein binding, extractables.
-   **Flow Rate and Throughput:** Select adequate flow rate/capacity for the volume. Filter area and membrane type influence flow.
-   **Filter Capacity and Fouling Resistance:** Consider capacity and clogging resistance, especially for turbid solutions or media with serum/proteins. Pre-filters can protect sterilizing filters.
-   **Filter Format and Housing:** Choose format (syringe, bottle-top, cartridge, in-line) based on application, volume, equipment.
-   **Certifications and Quality Standards:** Ensure filters meet standards (e.g., USP Class VI plastics).
