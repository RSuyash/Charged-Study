# 1.2.6 Pipetting Aids

Pipetting aids are laboratory devices designed to assist in the accurate and efficient aspiration and dispensing of liquids using pipettes, particularly serological and Pasteur pipettes. They enhance precision, reduce operator fatigue, improve ergonomics, and minimize contamination risk, especially in aseptic [[Animal Cell Culture]] work. They replace less accurate and potentially hazardous manual pipetting.

## Principle

Pipetting aids provide controlled suction (for aspiration) and pressure (for dispensing) to move liquids within pipettes, allowing for precise volume control and handling.

## Types of Pipetting Aids

1.  **Pipette Pumps (Pipette Controllers, Pipette Aids):**
    *   **Design:** Handheld, pistol-grip devices attached to serological pipettes. Contain motorized or manual mechanisms to control liquid movement. Buttons/triggers control aspiration/dispensing speed and mode (gravity, blow-out).
    *   **Types:**
        *   *Motorized Pipette Pumps (Electronic):* Battery-powered, push-button control. Precise, effortless, adjustable speed, ergonomic. Rechargeable/cordless.
        *   *Manual Pipette Pumps (Bulb Pipettes, Fillers):* Use squeeze bulb or plunger. Simpler, less expensive, but require manual dexterity and can be less precise/more fatiguing.
    *   **Applications:** Routine pipetting of media, reagents, solutions in cell culture hoods and general lab work. Essential for serological pipettes.
    *   **Advantages:** Enhanced precision/control, reduced fatigue, improved ergonomics, safer liquid handling (especially large volumes), minimizes contamination risk (vs. mouth pipetting).
    *   **Limitations:** Require charging (electronic), more expensive than simple pipettes, require proper technique.

2.  **Pipette-Aid Pipettors (Pipette Guns, Macro Pipette Controllers):**
    *   **Design:** Larger benchtop or handheld devices for larger volume serological pipettes (25 mL, 50 mL, 100 mL) and bottles. Use motorized pump or vacuum. Ergonomic grip, digital display, programmable modes.
    *   **Applications:** Dispensing large volumes of media/reagents, large-scale cell culture, filling large flasks/bottles/bioreactors.
    *   **Advantages:** Efficient large-volume dispensing, ergonomic for large pipettes, precise volume control, reduces fatigue.
    *   **Limitations:** More expensive, require bench space/handheld operation, not suitable for very small volumes.

3.  **Pipette Tips and Pipettes (Used with Aids):**
    *   **Serological Pipettes (Volumetric, Graduated):** Glass or disposable plastic pipettes (1 mL to 100 mL+) with volume graduations. Used with pipette pumps/pipettors.
        *   *Types:* Sterile, individually wrapped essential for aseptic work. Various volumes and tip styles (standard, wide-bore for viscous liquids/cells).
        *   *Materials:* Disposable plastic (polystyrene/polypropylene) most common for cell culture. Reusable glass less convenient.
    *   **Pasteur Pipettes (Transfer, Dropping):** Glass or disposable plastic, tapered tip, no graduations. Used for non-volumetric transfer, dropwise addition. Used with pipette bulbs.
        *   *Types:* Reusable glass or disposable plastic.
        *   *Materials:* Glass or plastic (polystyrene/polyethylene).
    *   **Pipette Tips (for Micropipettes/Electronic Pipettes):** Disposable plastic tips for accurate microliter to milliliter dispensing. Essential for microscale work, reagent prep, assays.
        *   *Types:* Sterile, DNase/RNase-free, pyrogen-free essential for cell culture/molecular biology. Various sizes. Filter tips prevent aerosol contamination.
        *   *Materials:* Typically polypropylene (inert, autoclavable).

## Key Features and Considerations When Selecting Pipetting Aids and Pipettes

-   **Volume Range:** Select appropriate ranges for applications (serological for mL, micropipettes for µL-mL).
-   **Accuracy and Precision:** Use calibrated pipettes (micropipettes, electronic) for high accuracy needs. Serological pipettes/pumps suitable for general transfer.
-   **Sterility and Aseptic Handling:** Always use sterile, certified non-pyrogenic disposables for cell culture. Work aseptically in [[1.2.1 Laminar Flow Hoods]].
-   **Ergonomics and User Comfort:** Choose comfortable designs to reduce fatigue/RSI risk. Electronic aids generally more ergonomic.
-   **Compatibility with Liquids:** Ensure compatibility with liquids (aqueous, organic, viscous). Use appropriate tips (e.g., low-retention).
-   **Disposable vs. Reusable:** Disposables most common for cell culture (convenience, no cross-contamination). Reusable glass less common.
-   **Cost and Consumables:** Consider costs of aids, pipettes, and disposable tips.

Pipetting aids are essential for accurate, efficient, and aseptic liquid handling in animal cell culture labs.
