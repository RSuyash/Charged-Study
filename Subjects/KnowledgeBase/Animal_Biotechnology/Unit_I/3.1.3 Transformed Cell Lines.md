# 3.1.3 Transformed Cell Lines

## Definition

Transformed cell lines are a subset of [[3.1.2 Permanent Established Cell Lines]] that have undergone **transformation**, acquiring cancer-like properties. These properties include immortality, loss of contact inhibition, anchorage-independent growth, and often tumorigenicity (ability to form tumors in animals). Transformation can occur spontaneously, or be induced by viruses, chemical carcinogens, or radiation.

## Characteristics

Transformed cell lines share characteristics with established lines (immortality, aneuploidy) but exhibit additional features:

-   **Immortalized (Infinite Lifespan):** Proliferate indefinitely *in vitro*.
-   **Aneuploid Karyotype (Typically):** Genetically unstable, often with significant chromosomal abnormalities and mutations.
-   **Loss of Contact Inhibition:** Continue to grow and pile up after reaching confluence, forming foci (dense, multilayered colonies). Normal cells stop growing upon contact.
-   **Anchorage-Independent Growth:** Can grow in suspension or semi-solid media (e.g., soft agar) without attachment to a substrate. Normal anchorage-dependent cells require attachment. This is a hallmark of transformation and tumorigenicity.
-   **Tumorigenicity (Often):** Many can form tumors when injected into immunocompromised animals (e.g., nude mice). Not all transformed lines are tumorigenic *in vivo*.
-   **Altered Morphology:** Often exhibit changes in cell shape, size, nuclear morphology, and cytoskeletal organization compared to normal cells (e.g., rounded, spindle-shaped, pleomorphic).
-   **Reduced Growth Factor Requirements:** Often have reduced need for exogenous growth factors due to autocrine production or constitutively active signaling pathways.
-   **Increased Glucose Uptake/Aerobic Glycolysis (Warburg Effect):** Exhibit increased glucose uptake and shift towards aerobic glycolysis, characteristic of cancer cells.
-   **Genetic and Epigenetic Alterations:** Harbor changes contributing to the transformed phenotype:
    *   *Oncogene Activation:* Activation of genes promoting cell growth/cancer (e.g., *ras*, *myc*, *src*, *HER2*) via mutations, amplification, rearrangements.
    *   *Tumor Suppressor Gene Inactivation:* Loss of function of genes suppressing growth/tumor formation (e.g., *p53*, *Rb*, *PTEN*, *BRCA1*) via mutations, deletions, epigenetic silencing.
    *   *Cell Cycle Dysregulation:* Altered expression/function of cyclins, CDKs, CDK inhibitors.
    *   *Telomerase Activation:* Reactivation of telomerase maintains telomere length, contributing to immortality.
    *   *Angiogenesis Factor Production:* Production of factors like VEGF promoting blood vessel formation.
    *   *Metastasis-Associated Gene Expression:* Expression of genes involved in cancer spread (e.g., MMPs, cell adhesion molecules).

## Induction of Cellular Transformation

Transformation can occur via:

-   **Spontaneous Transformation:** Can occur *in vitro* over extended passages (especially rodent lines) due to accumulated genetic/epigenetic changes.
-   **Viral Transformation:** Infection with certain viruses (retroviruses, DNA tumor viruses like SV40, adenovirus, papillomavirus). Viral oncogenes disrupt cell cycle control or inactivate tumor suppressors (e.g., SV40 Large T antigen).
-   **Chemical Transformation:** Exposure to chemical carcinogens induces mutations/epigenetic alterations. Multistep process.
-   **Radiation Transformation:** Exposure to ionizing radiation (X-rays, gamma rays) or UV radiation induces DNA damage/mutations. Multistep process.

## Applications

-   **Cancer Research:** Widely used *in vitro* models for studying cancer biology, oncogenes, tumor suppressors, metabolism, drug discovery, screening anticancer agents.
-   **Tumorigenicity Assays:** Assess carcinogenic potential of chemicals or study tumorigenic properties of genes.
-   **Drug Discovery (Cancer Therapeutics):** Used in screening assays to identify compounds inhibiting cancer cell growth or inducing death.
-   **Toxicity Testing (Cytotoxicity Assays):** Assess toxic effects of chemicals/drugs on cell viability.
-   **Cellular/Molecular Biology Research:** Used as models for fundamental processes (gene regulation, signaling, cell cycle), though relevance to normal biology is limited.

## Advantages

-   **Immortalized and Easy to Culture:** Continuous, readily available cell source.
-   **Cancer-Like Properties:** Relevant *in vitro* models for cancer research.
-   **Relatively Homogeneous:** More homogeneous than primary tumors, providing reproducible systems.

## Limitations

-   **Altered *In Vivo* Characteristics:** Significantly altered from normal cells, may not accurately reflect normal tissue biology.
-   **Genetic/Phenotypic Instability:** Can undergo further drift over time, affecting reproducibility.
-   **Not Representative of *In Vivo* Tumor Complexity:** Simplified models lacking tumor microenvironment, stromal interactions, immune responses.
-   **Ethical Considerations:** Related to the use of cancer-derived cell lines.

Despite limitations, transformed cell lines are invaluable tools in cancer research, drug discovery, and toxicology. Results must be interpreted cautiously considering their transformed nature.
