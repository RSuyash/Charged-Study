# 2.1 Basic Aseptic Techniques

**Aseptic technique** is a set of procedures and practices used in [[Animal Cell Culture]] and microbiology to prevent contamination of cultures, media, reagents, and sterile materials with unwanted microorganisms (bacteria, fungi, viruses, mycoplasma). Maintaining aseptic conditions is critical for successful cell culture, preventing inaccurate results, loss of cell lines, and compromised product quality.

## 2.1.1 Fundamental Principles of Aseptic Technique

1.  **Sterilization:** Complete elimination of all viable microorganisms. This is the foundation.
    *   **Methods:**
        *   *Autoclaving (Steam):* Most common for heat-stable materials (glassware, media, instruments). Uses steam under pressure (121°C, 15 psi, 15-30 min). Kills spores.
        *   *Dry Heat Sterilization:* For glassware, dry powders. Hot air oven (160-180°C, 1-2 hours).
        *   *[[Filtration]] Sterilization:* For heat-labile liquids (media, serum, growth factors). Uses 0.22 µm or smaller membrane filters. See [[1.3.1 Filters]].
        *   *Ethylene Oxide (EtO) Sterilization (Gas):* For heat/moisture-sensitive materials (some plastics). Requires specialized equipment/ventilation (toxic/flammable).
        *   *Gamma Irradiation Sterilization:* For pre-packaged disposables (pipettes, flasks, tips). Uses ionizing radiation.

2.  **Working in a Sterile Environment:**
    *   **[[1.2.1 Laminar Flow Hoods]] (BSCs):** Work should be performed within a laminar flow hood (vertical BSC preferred for animal cells) to provide a localized sterile workspace via filtered airflow.

3.  **Aseptic Handling Techniques:**
    *   **Sterile Gloves:** Always wear sterile gloves when handling sterile items. Change frequently if contaminated/torn.
    *   **Sterile Instruments/Materials:** Use sterile instruments (forceps, pipettes) and materials (flasks, media). Autoclave reusables; use sterile disposables.
    *   **Flaming Glassware (Optional):** Flaming glass Pasteur pipette tips can help sterilize (not for plastic).
    *   **Disinfection:** Regularly disinfect work surfaces (hoods, incubators) and equipment with 70% ethanol or other disinfectants before/after use and during procedures. Wipe down items before placing them in the hood.
    *   **Aseptic Transfer:** Minimize exposure of sterile materials to non-sterile air/surfaces. Work quickly and efficiently.
    *   **Minimize Air Exposure:** Keep sterile containers open briefly. Work within the sterile zone of the hood, away from drafts/traffic.
    *   **Good Personal Hygiene:** Wash hands thoroughly. Avoid talking/coughing/sneezing over sterile items. Tie back hair, avoid loose clothing.

4.  **Regular Monitoring for Contamination:**
    *   **Visual Inspection:** Regularly inspect cultures under a [[1.2.3 Microscopes|microscope]] for turbidity, unusual morphology, bacteria, fungi.
    *   **Media Controls:** Incubate samples of media/reagents as negative controls to check sterility.
    *   **Cleaning/Maintenance:** Regularly clean/maintain equipment (hoods, incubators, centrifuges, water baths). Perform routine maintenance (e.g., HEPA filter replacement).

## 2.1.2 Practical Procedures for Maintaining Sterility

-   **Before Starting Work:**
    *   *Plan and Prepare:* Gather all materials/reagents/equipment.
    *   *Clean Work Area:* Wipe down hood surface with 70% ethanol, let dry.
    *   *Sterilize Materials:* Ensure all items contacting cultures are sterile.
    *   *UV Sterilization (Optional):* Use hood UV lamp for 15-30 min before work (avoid exposure).
    *   *Organize Materials:* Arrange sterile items within reach inside the hood, keeping wrappers on until needed.
-   **During Work:**
    *   *Work within Sterile Zone:* Central area of the hood with strongest airflow.
    *   *Maintain Airflow:* Do not block airflow with hands/materials.
    *   *Aseptic Transfers:* Use proper technique for all transfers.
    *   *Flame Glassware (Optional):* If using glass Pasteur pipettes.
    *   *Keep Containers Open Briefly:* Minimize exposure time.
    *   *Work Upstream to Downstream:* Process sterile items before potentially contaminated ones.
    *   *Use Separate Pipettes:* Fresh sterile pipette for each container/reagent.
    *   *Prevent Spills/Splashes:* Clean spills immediately with disinfectant.
-   **After Work:**
    *   *Close/Secure Cultures:* Seal flasks/dishes properly.
    *   *Disinfect Work Area:* Wipe down hood and equipment.
    *   *Turn Off UV Lamp (If Used).*
    *   *Dispose of Waste Properly:* Biohazard waste (autoclave before disposal), sharps waste.
    *   *Wash Hands:* Thoroughly before leaving the lab.

## 2.1.3 Importance of Aseptic Technique

Meticulous aseptic technique is paramount for:
-   **Maintaining Culture Sterility:** Preventing microbial contamination.
-   **Ensuring Experimental Reproducibility:** Consistent, contamination-free conditions.
-   **Preventing False Results:** Contamination interferes with experiments.
-   **Maintaining Cell Line Integrity:** Contamination alters cell characteristics.
-   **Protecting Workers:** Minimizing exposure to biohazards.

Mastering and consistently applying aseptic technique is a fundamental skill requiring attention to detail, training, and commitment.
