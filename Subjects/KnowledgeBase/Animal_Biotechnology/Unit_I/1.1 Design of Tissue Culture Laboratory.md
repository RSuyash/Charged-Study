# 1.1 Design of Tissue Culture Laboratory

The design of a tissue culture laboratory is crucial for maintaining a sterile and controlled environment, essential for successful and reliable [[Animal Cell Culture]]. A well-designed lab minimizes the risk of contamination, ensures efficient workflow, and promotes safety.

![image.png](<../../../V2/Animal Biotechnology/AB-Unit-I/Unit I 1a53a995df8780f69720e68a76b33240/image.png>)

Key considerations include:

## 1.1.1 Zoning and Layout

-   **Separate Zones:** The laboratory should be divided into distinct zones based on cleanliness and activity to minimize contamination risks.
    -   **Preparation Area (Non-sterile):** For washing/sterilizing glassware, media prep components, general lab work. May contain autoclaves, washers, storage.
    -   **Sterile Area (Clean Room):** Core area for aseptic cell culture work. Houses laminar flow hoods, CO2 incubators, microscopes. Restricted access, requires gowning.
    -   **Equipment Area:** For non-sterile equipment like centrifuges, refrigerators, freezers, water baths.
    -   **Storage Area:** Separate storage for chemicals, media, plasticware, cryopreserved cells.
    -   **Office/Data Analysis Area:** Designated non-lab area for data analysis, writing, etc.
-   **Workflow:** Layout should facilitate a unidirectional workflow (non-sterile → sterile) to minimize contamination. Materials enter via prep, move to sterile, then equipment/storage.
-   **Traffic Control:** Minimize traffic in the sterile area. Implement strict entry/exit procedures (gowning, hand washing).

## 1.1.2 Environmental Control

-   **Air Handling System (HVAC):** Dedicated system for air quality and environmental control.
    -   **Positive Air Pressure:** Maintain positive pressure in the sterile area relative to surroundings.
    -   **HEPA Filtration:** High-Efficiency Particulate Air filters remove airborne particles (microorganisms, dust).
    -   **Air Changes:** Adequate air changes per hour (e.g., 15-20) to maintain cleanliness.
    -   **Temperature and Humidity Control:** Stable temperature (e.g., 37°C ± 1°C) and humidity (e.g., 50-70%) for optimal cell culture and minimal evaporation.
-   **Surface Finishes and Materials:**
    -   **Non-porous and Smooth Surfaces:** Use non-porous, smooth, chemically resistant materials (epoxy, stainless steel, laminates) for easy cleaning/disinfection.
    -   **Coving:** Cove corners between walls/floors to eliminate dust traps and facilitate cleaning.
    -   **Minimal Shelving and Furniture:** Reduce surfaces in the sterile area; furniture should be easy to clean.
-   **Lighting:** Adequate, uniform lighting. Indirect or recessed lighting preferred to minimize dust on fixtures.

## 1.1.3 Essential Features

-   **Passage Window or Hatch:** Between preparation and sterile areas for material transfer without compromising sterility. UV sterilization lamps optional.
-   **Sink with Hands-Free Operation:** Foot pedal or sensor-activated faucet in the sterile area.
-   **Viewing Window:** Allows observation from outside, minimizing traffic.
-   **Power Supply and Backup:** Stable power with UPS or generator for critical equipment (incubators, freezers).
-   **Emergency Exits and Safety Features:** Clear exits, fire extinguishers, eyewash stations, spill kits.

## 1.1.4 Optional but Recommended Features

-   **Anteroom or Gowning Area:** Area before sterile zone for donning sterile garments (gowns, gloves, masks, caps).
-   **Air Shower:** At sterile area entrance to remove particles from garments.
-   **Sticky Mats:** At entrances to trap particles from shoes.
-   **Intercom System:** Communication without opening doors.

A well-designed tissue culture laboratory is essential for experiment integrity, contamination prevention, and a safe, efficient work environment. Design should be tailored to work type, scale, and budget.
