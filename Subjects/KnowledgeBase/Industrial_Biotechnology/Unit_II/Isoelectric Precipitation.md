# Isoelectric Precipitation

Isoelectric precipitation is a [[2.1.1 Precipitation Methods|precipitation method]] used in [[2.1 Removal and Recovery of Cell Mass|cell mass removal]] and protein purification. It exploits the fact that proteins and other amphoteric biomolecules have minimum solubility at their isoelectric point (pI).

## Principle

At the isoelectric point (pI), a protein has a net zero charge. This minimizes electrostatic repulsion between protein molecules, promoting hydrophobic interactions and aggregation, leading to precipitation.

## Technique

1.  **pH Adjustment:** The pH of the fermentation broth or cell lysate is adjusted to the pI of the target cellular components (often bulk cell proteins). This is typically achieved by adding acids (HCl, H₂SO₄) or bases (NaOH).
2.  **Precipitation:** The mixture is stirred and allowed to settle, or centrifuged to separate the precipitated cell mass.

## Applications

-   Bulk protein precipitation from microbial broths.
-   Can be used to precipitate whole cell mass or specific protein fractions.

## Advantages

-   Relatively inexpensive.
-   Simple to implement.
-   Can be used for large volumes.

## Limitations

-   Non-selective: Precipitates a mixture of cellular components.
-   Can denature/damage sensitive biomolecules if pH is too extreme.
-   Requires further purification steps.

Isoelectric precipitation is often used as an initial bulk separation step before more selective purification techniques.
