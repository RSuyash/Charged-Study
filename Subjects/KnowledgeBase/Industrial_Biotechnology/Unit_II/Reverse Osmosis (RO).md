# Reverse Osmosis (RO)

Reverse osmosis (RO) is a [[2.3.3 Membrane Processes|membrane process]] used in [[Downstream Processing]] to separate water from dissolved salts and other small molecules. It uses semi-permeable membranes with very small pore sizes (< 0.001 µm).

## Principle

High pressure is applied to overcome osmotic pressure, forcing water through the membrane while retaining dissolved solutes.

## Separation Mechanism

Primarily size exclusion (for very small molecules/ions) and solution-diffusion (water preferentially permeates through the dense membrane matrix). Membranes are essentially non-porous.

## Membrane Types

Thin-film composite membranes (polyamide or cellulose acetate) are commonly used.

## Membrane Configurations

Spiral-wound and hollow fiber configurations are typical.

## Operating Pressure

High pressure is required (10-100 bar or 150-1500 psi), depending on feed water salinity and desired permeate quality.

## Applications in Downstream Processing

-   Water Purification and [[Water for Injection (WFI)]] Production: Producing high-purity water for pharmaceutical, biopharmaceutical, and other industries.
-   Desalting and Deionization: Removing salts and minerals from process streams or product solutions.
-   Concentration of Low Molecular Weight Solutes: Concentrating amino acids, organic acids, or antibiotics by removing water.
-   Wastewater Treatment and Water Reuse: Treating industrial/municipal wastewater for reuse.

## Advantages

-   Highest resolution separation among membrane processes.
-   Removes virtually all dissolved solids.
-   Produces high-purity water.
-   Continuous operation possible (TFF).

## Limitations

-   Highest energy consumption (high pressure).
-   Membranes are prone to fouling (scaling, organic fouling, biofouling).
-   Membrane fouling reduces flux and separation efficiency.
-   Membranes can be expensive.
-   Limited to separation of water from small solutes.
-   Not typically used for protein purification or separation of macromolecules.

RO is a powerful technique for water purification and desalination, but less commonly used for direct purification of macromolecules in bioprocessing.
