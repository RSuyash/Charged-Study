# Surface Filtration (Membrane Filtration)

Surface filters, particularly membrane filters, are a type of [[2.1.2 Filtration Techniques|filtration]] used in [[2.1 Removal and Recovery of Cell Mass|cell mass removal]] and purification. They have a thin, porous membrane with a defined pore size on the surface, separating particles based on size exclusion.

## Principle

Particles larger than the membrane pore size are retained on the surface, while smaller molecules pass through as filtrate (permeate).

## Types of Membrane Filters

-   **[[Microfiltration (UF)]]:** Pore size 0.1-10 µm. Removes bacteria, yeast, fungal cells, particulate matter, cell debris. Effective for cell harvesting and clarification.
-   **[[Ultrafiltration (UF)]]:** Pore size 0.01-0.1 µm. Removes viruses, colloids, proteins, larger biomolecules, plus MF removals. Used for protein concentration, endotoxin removal, clarification.
-   **Nanofiltration (NF):** Pore size 0.001-0.01 µm. Removes multivalent ions (hardness, nitrates, sulfates), some dissolved organics, viruses. Used for softening, color removal.
-   **Reverse Osmosis (RO):** Pore size < 0.001 µm (essentially non-porous). Removes virtually all dissolved salts, minerals, organics. Used for water purification, desalination.

## Membrane Configurations

-   Plate-and-Frame
-   Spiral-Wound
-   Hollow Fiber
    Hollow fiber and cassette formats are widely used for bioprocessing due to high surface area and ease of scale-up.

## Filtration Modes

-   **Dead-End Filtration:** All feed passes through the filter. Clogs as particles accumulate. Suitable for low solids, small volumes.
-   **Crossflow Filtration (Tangential Flow Filtration - TFF):** Feed flows tangential to the membrane. Permeate passes through, retentate (concentrated particles) is recirculated. Minimizes fouling, suitable for high solids, large volumes.

Surface filtration provides sharp separation based on size exclusion and can be used for sterile filtration.
