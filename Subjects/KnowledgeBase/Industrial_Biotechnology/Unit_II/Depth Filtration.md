# Depth Filtration

Depth filters are a type of [[2.1.2 Filtration Techniques|filtration]] used in [[2.1 Removal and Recovery of Cell Mass|cell mass removal]] and clarification. They are thick, porous matrices that trap particles within the filter medium.

## Principle

Separation occurs through a combination of mechanical entrapment, adsorption, and inertial impaction within the depth of the filter medium.

## Types of Depth Filters

-   **Packed Bed Filters:** Simple columns packed with granular materials.
-   **Cartridge Filters:** Filter media pleated/wrapped around a core (increased surface area), housed in cartridges.
-   **Filter Sheets:** Flat sheets of fibrous media, used in plate-and-frame filter presses.

## Applications

-   Pre-filtration: Removing coarse particles/cell debris from fermentation broths *before* more selective purification.
-   Clarification: Removing suspended solids.
-   Effective for handling broths with high solids content.

## Advantages

-   High solids loading capacity.
-   Relatively inexpensive.
-   Simple to operate.
-   Can handle viscous fluids.

## Limitations

-   Lower resolution separation compared to [[Surface Filtration|membrane filtration]].
-   Filtrate may still contain fine particles/debris.
-   Not suitable for sterilization.
-   Filter media can be prone to clogging.

Depth filtration is often used as a pre-treatment step to protect more expensive membrane filters or chromatography columns from fouling.
