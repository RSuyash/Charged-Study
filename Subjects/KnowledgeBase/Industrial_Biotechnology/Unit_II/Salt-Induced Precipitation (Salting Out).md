# Salt-Induced Precipitation (Salting Out)

Salt-induced precipitation, also known as "salting out", is a [[2.1.1 Precipitation Methods|precipitation method]] used in [[2.1 Removal and Recovery of Cell Mass|cell mass removal]] and protein purification. High salt concentrations reduce protein solubility, causing aggregation and precipitation.

## Principle

High salt concentrations (e.g., ammonium sulfate, sodium sulfate) compete with proteins for water molecules, reducing protein hydration and increasing hydrophobic interactions. This leads to protein aggregation and precipitation.

## Technique

1.  **Salt Addition:** Concentrated salt solution added to fermentation broth/cell lysate to reach a specific saturation level (e.g., 20-80% ammonium sulfate).
2.  **Mixing and Separation:** Mixture stirred, allowed to settle, or centrifuged to separate precipitated cell mass/proteins.

## Fractional Precipitation

Gradually increasing salt concentration allows selective precipitation of different protein fractions based on their solubility at different salt levels. Used for partial purification.

## Applications

-   Protein precipitation and concentration.
-   Cell mass removal.
-   Ammonium sulfate precipitation is a common technique in protein purification.

## Advantages

-   Effective for precipitating a broad range of biomolecules.
-   Relatively inexpensive.
-   Ammonium sulfate can stabilize some proteins.

## Limitations

-   Non-selective: Precipitates a mixture of cellular components.
-   High salt concentrations can be corrosive.
-   Requires salt removal (desalting) in subsequent purification steps.
-   May denature some proteins at high salt concentrations.

Salt-induced precipitation is a widely used technique for initial protein concentration and cell mass removal, but requires careful optimization and subsequent purification steps.
