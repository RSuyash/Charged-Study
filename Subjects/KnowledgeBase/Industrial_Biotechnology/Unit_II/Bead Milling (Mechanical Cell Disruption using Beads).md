# Bead Milling (Mechanical Cell Disruption using Beads)

Bead milling (or bead beating) is a [[2.2.1 Physical Methods of Cell Disruption|physical method]] used for [[2.2 Cell Disruption|cell disruption]]. It utilizes mechanical agitation with small, hard beads to break open cells.

## Principle

Beads collide with cells, causing breakage through shear forces, impaction, and attrition.

## Technique

Cell suspension is mixed with beads in a vessel/chamber and agitated vigorously (shaker, mixer, rotating impeller).

## Equipment

-   **Batch Bead Mill (Vortex, Shaker):** Sample/beads in a tube agitated. For small volumes, batch processing.
-   **Continuous Bead Mill (Agitated, Dyno-Mill, Media Mill):** Continuous processing of large volumes. Cell suspension/beads continuously fed into a chamber with an agitator. Cooling jackets control temperature.

## Operational Parameters and Control

-   **Bead Size/Type:** Selected based on cell type/size, wall strength, desired disruption. Smaller beads for smaller cells. Materials: glass, ceramic, zirconia (harder, more efficient).
-   **Bead Loading (Bead:Sample Ratio):** Higher loading increases disruption but also viscosity.
-   **Agitation Speed/Intensity:** Higher speeds increase disruption but also heat.
-   **Processing Time/Cycles:** Longer times increase disruption but also heat. Batch mills may use cycles of agitation/cooling.
-   **Temperature Control:** Cooling systems are essential to prevent protein denaturation.

## Applications

-   Effective for disrupting bacteria (including tough *Mycobacteria*), yeast, fungi, algae, plant cells at lab/industrial scales.
-   Releasing intracellular enzymes, proteins, cell wall components.

## Advantages

-   Highly effective cell disruption.
-   Scalable for large-scale continuous processing (continuous bead mills).
-   Versatile for different cell types.
-   Can achieve high levels of cell lysis.

## Limitations

-   Generates significant heat (requires cooling).
-   Can be noisy.
-   Bead separation from lysate can be challenging (especially small beads).
-   Potential for product degradation due to heat/mechanical forces.
-   Equipment can be expensive.

Bead milling is a powerful method for cell disruption, particularly for tough cells, but requires careful optimization and temperature control.
