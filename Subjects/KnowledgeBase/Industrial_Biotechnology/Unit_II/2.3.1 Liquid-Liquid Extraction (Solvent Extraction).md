# 2.3.1 Liquid-Liquid Extraction (Solvent Extraction)

Liquid-liquid extraction (LLE), also known as solvent extraction, is a separation technique used to selectively transfer a desired compound (solute) from one liquid phase (feed phase) to another immiscible liquid phase (solvent phase) based on differences in solubility. It is a widely used unit operation in [[Downstream Processing]], particularly for the initial recovery and partial purification of hydrophobic products from fermentation broths or cell lysates.

## 2.3.1.1 Principles and Techniques of Liquid-Liquid Extraction

-   **Immiscible Solvents:** LLE relies on two immiscible (or partially miscible) liquid phases, typically an aqueous phase (fermentation broth/lysate) and an organic solvent phase.
-   **Solute Partitioning (Distribution Coefficient - Kd):** Solute distributes between phases until equilibrium is reached. The distribution coefficient (Kd) quantifies this: `Kd = [Solute]organic / [Solute]aqueous`. High Kd is desirable for extraction. Kd is influenced by solute/solvent properties, temperature, pH.
-   **Selectivity:** LLE can be selective if the target solute has a significantly higher Kd for the organic solvent compared to impurities. Selectivity is crucial for purification.
-   **Mass Transfer:** Rate of solute transfer depends on interfacial area, diffusion coefficient, viscosity, temperature.

## Techniques

-   **Batch Extraction:** Broth/lysate mixed with solvent in a vessel (separatory funnel, flask), agitated, then allowed to settle. Organic phase (with product) separated. Simple, lab-scale.
-   **Continuous Countercurrent Extraction:** More efficient for large-scale. Continuous flow of aqueous and solvent phases in opposite directions through an extraction column. Stages promote mixing/separation.

-   **Supercritical Fluid Extraction (SFE):** Uses supercritical fluids (SFs) as solvents (typically supercritical CO₂ - SC-CO₂). Tunable solvent properties (pressure/temperature). Environmentally friendly.

## 2.3.1.2 Solvent Selection and Recovery

-   **Solvent Selection Criteria:**
    -   High Selectivity for Target Solute
    -   High Distribution Coefficient (Kd)
    -   Low Miscibility with Feed Phase
    -   Low Viscosity and Density
    -   Chemical Inertness and Stability
    -   Low Toxicity and Flammability
    -   Low Cost and Availability
    -   Ease of Recovery and Recycling
    -   Low Emulsion Formation Tendency
-   **Types of Solvents:** Non-polar (hydrocarbons, ethers), polar aprotic (ethyl acetate, ketones), alcohols (butanol), supercritical CO₂.
-   **Solvent Recovery Methods:**
    -   Distillation (for volatile solvents)
    -   Evaporation (rotary/thin-film)
    -   Stripping (gas/steam)
    -   Adsorption (activated carbon)
    -   Membrane Separation (pervaporation, vapor permeation)
    -   Back Extraction (transfer solvent back to another phase)

Solvent selection and recovery are critical for efficient, economical, and sustainable LLE.
