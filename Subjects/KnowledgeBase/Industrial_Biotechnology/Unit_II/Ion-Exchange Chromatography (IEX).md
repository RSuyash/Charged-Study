# Ion-Exchange Chromatography (IEX)

Ion-exchange chromatography (IEX) is a type of [[2.3.2 Chromatography|chromatography]] that separates molecules based on differences in their net surface charge. It is a widely used technique for protein purification, nucleic acid purification, and water treatment.

## Principle

IEX utilizes a stationary phase with charged functional groups that interact electrostatically with oppositely charged sample components (ions or charged biomolecules).

## Stationary Phase

Ion-exchange resins are insoluble matrices (typically polymeric beads) with covalently attached charged functional groups.

-   **Cation Exchangers:** Negatively charged stationary phase. Bind to positively charged molecules (cations).
    -   *Strong Cation Exchangers:* Sulfonic acid groups (-SO₃⁻), e.g., Source S, Mono S, SP-Sepharose. Remain negatively charged over a wide pH range.
    -   *Weak Cation Exchangers:* Carboxylic acid groups (-COO⁻), e.g., CM-Cellulose, CM-Sepharose. Charge is pH-dependent, lose charge at low pH.
-   **Anion Exchangers:** Positively charged stationary phase. Bind to negatively charged molecules (anions).
    -   *Strong Anion Exchangers:* Quaternary ammonium groups (-N⁺(CH₃)₃), e.g., Source Q, Mono Q, Q-Sepharose. Remain positively charged over a wide pH range.
    -   *Weak Anion Exchangers:* Amino groups (-NH₃⁺), e.g., DEAE-Cellulose, DEAE-Sepharose. Charge is pH-dependent, lose charge at high pH.

## Mobile Phase

Buffer solution. pH and ionic strength are critical parameters for controlling binding and elution.

## Process

1.  **Binding:** Sample loaded onto the column at low ionic strength, promoting binding of oppositely charged molecules to the resin.
2.  **Elution:** Bound molecules are eluted by:
    *   *Increasing Ionic Strength (Salt Gradient):* Gradually increasing the salt concentration in the buffer to weaken electrostatic interactions.
    *   *Changing pH (pH Gradient):* Altering the pH to change the charge state of the molecules or the resin.

## Applications

-   Protein purification (separate proteins with different isoelectric points).
-   Nucleic acid purification.
-   Separation of charged metabolites.
-   Water purification (ion exchange resins for water softening/deionization).

## Advantages

-   High selectivity for charged molecules.
-   High binding capacity.
-   Versatile for different biomolecules.
-   Scalable for large-scale purification.

## Limitations

-   Requires sample to be charged.
-   Separation is sensitive to pH and ionic strength.
-   Salt gradient elution increases salt concentration in product (desalting may be needed).
-   Resins can be expensive.

IEX is a powerful technique for separating and purifying charged biomolecules, widely used in downstream processing.
