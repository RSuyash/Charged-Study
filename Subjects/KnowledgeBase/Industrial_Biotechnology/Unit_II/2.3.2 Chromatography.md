# 2.3.2 Chromatography

[[Chromatography]] is a powerful separation technique used in [[Downstream Processing]] for the purification of bioproducts. It separates molecules based on differences in their physicochemical properties as they interact with a stationary phase while being carried by a mobile phase.

## General Principles

-   **Mobile Phase:** Fluid (liquid or gas) carrying the sample through the system.
-   **Stationary Phase:** Solid or liquid material fixed within a column, selectively interacting with sample components.
-   **Column:** Tube packed with stationary phase.
-   **Separation Mechanism:** Differential migration based on affinity for stationary vs. mobile phase.
-   **Elution:** Components washed out of the column at different times/conditions.
-   **Detection:** Detector measures a property (UV absorbance, fluorescence, mass) and generates a chromatogram.
-   **Fractions Collection:** Eluted fractions containing purified components are collected.

## Types of Chromatography (Based on Separation Mechanism)

-   **Adsorption Chromatography (Liquid-Solid):** Separation based on differential adsorption onto a solid stationary phase (silica gel, alumina, activated carbon).
-   **[[Ion-Exchange Chromatography (IEX)]]:** Separation based on charge. Stationary phase has charged groups (cation or anion exchangers).
-   **[[High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC)]]:** High-resolution liquid chromatography using small particle stationary phases and high pressure. Can be used with various separation modes (adsorption, IEX, size-exclusion, affinity).
-   **Other Techniques:**
    -   [[Affinity Chromatography]]: Separation based on specific binding to a ligand.
    -   Size-Exclusion Chromatography (SEC) / Gel Filtration: Separation based on molecular size.
    -   Hydrophobic Interaction Chromatography (HIC): Separation based on hydrophobicity.

The choice of technique depends on the properties of the target product and impurities.
