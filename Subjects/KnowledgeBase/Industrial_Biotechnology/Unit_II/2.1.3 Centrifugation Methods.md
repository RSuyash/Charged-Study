# 2.1.3 Centrifugation Methods

[[Centrifugation]] is a widely used technique for separating cell mass from fermentation broth based on density differences. Centrifugal force accelerates sedimentation, enabling rapid and efficient separation.

## Types of Centrifugation Methods for Cell Mass Removal

1.  **Batch Centrifugation:**
    *   **Principle:** Sample spun in tubes/bottles. Solids sediment to form a pellet; supernatant is separated.
    *   **Equipment:** Lab centrifuges (benchtop, high-speed, ultracentrifuges) with various rotors.
    *   **Operation:** Tubes/bottles are filled, balanced, placed in rotor, spun at specific g-force/time. Supernatant decanted, pellet recovered.
    *   **Applications:** Small-scale cell harvesting in research, pilot studies, small volumes.
    *   **Advantages:** Simple, readily available equipment, versatile.
    *   **Limitations:** Batch process, limited throughput, labor-intensive for large volumes, not suitable for industrial bioprocessing.

2.  **Continuous Centrifugation:**
    *   **Principle:** Continuous separation of solids from liquids in large-scale bioprocessing. Broth continuously fed, separated solids/liquid continuously discharged.
    *   **Types:**
        *   *Disc-Stack Centrifuges:* Most common for cell harvesting. Rotating rotor with conical discs. Solids move outwards, liquid inwards. Solids discharged intermittently/continuously.
        *   *Tubular Bowl Centrifuges:* High-speed, long cylindrical rotor. Efficient separation of fine particles due to high g-forces. Solids removed periodically.
        *   *Decanter Centrifuges:* Horizontal bowl with scroll conveyor. For high solids concentration slurries, sludge dewatering.
    *   **Operational Parameters:**
        *   *Feed Flow Rate:* Controlled to optimize separation/throughput.
        *   *Rotor Speed (G-force):* Selected based on particle size/density.
        *   *Temperature:* Can affect viscosity/separation.
        *   *Back Pressure (Disc-Stack):* Controls solids concentration in discharge.
        *   *Solids Discharge Rate:* Controls solids buildup.
    *   **Applications:** Large-scale cell harvesting (bacteria, yeast, fungi, debris), clarification.
    *   **Advantages:** High throughput, efficient separation, continuous operation.
    *   **Limitations:** High capital cost, significant energy consumption, potential cell lysis, separation efficiency affected by particle size/broth viscosity.

The choice depends on scale, cell type, product location, downstream requirements, and cost.
