# High-Pressure Homogenization

High-pressure homogenization is a [[2.2.1 Physical Methods of Cell Disruption|physical method]] used for [[2.2 Cell Disruption|cell disruption]] at both laboratory and industrial scales. It forces cell suspensions through a narrow gap under high pressure, causing cell lysis due to shear forces and rapid decompression.

## Principle

Cells experience intense shear forces, cavitation, and impingement as they pass through a narrow gap under high pressure, leading to cell breakage.

## Technique

Cell suspension is pumped through a high-pressure homogenizer, consisting of a high-pressure pump and a homogenizing valve/gap assembly.

## Equipment

-   **High-Pressure Homogenizers (Piston Gap):** Use a piston to force suspension through a narrow valve. Pressure generated mechanically. Single-piston or multi-piston designs.
-   **Microfluidizers (Microchannel):** Force suspension through microchannels at high velocities/pressures. Generate very high shear rates.

## Operational Parameters and Control

-   **Operating Pressure:** Higher pressure increases disruption, but also energy use/degradation. Optimize empirically. Typical range: 500-1500 bar+.
-   **Number of Passes/Cycles:** Multiple passes increase disruption, but also processing time/heat.
-   **Temperature Control:** Cooling systems (heat exchangers, pre-cooling) are essential to prevent protein denaturation.
-   **Flow Rate:** Influences throughput and disruption efficiency.

## Applications

-   Large-scale industrial cell disruption of bacteria, yeast, fungi, algae, mammalian cells.
-   Releasing intracellular enzymes, proteins, inclusion bodies.

## Advantages

-   Highly effective cell disruption.
-   Scalable for large-scale continuous processing.
-   Can achieve high levels of cell lysis.
-   Relatively fast processing times.

## Limitations

-   High capital cost.
-   High energy consumption.
-   Generates significant heat (requires cooling).
-   Cell lysate can be heated/pressurized, affecting sensitive products.
-   Valve clogging can be an issue with high solids broths.

High-pressure homogenization is a powerful technique for large-scale cell disruption, but requires careful optimization and temperature control.
