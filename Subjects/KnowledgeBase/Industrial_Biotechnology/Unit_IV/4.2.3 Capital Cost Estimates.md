# 4.2.3 Capital Cost Estimates

Capital cost estimates assess the one-time investments needed to establish a fermentation production facility. They are crucial for evaluating project economic viability.

**Categories of Capital Cost:**

1.  **Fixed Capital Investment (FCI):** Total cost of physical assets/infrastructure.
    *   **Direct Costs:** Equipment (fermenters, DSP, utilities, ancillary), installation, instrumentation, buildings, land/site development.
    *   **Indirect Costs:** Engineering/design, construction fees, contingency, start-up/validation, legal/regulatory.
2.  **Working Capital (WC):** Funds for initial operation before revenue generation.
    *   Covers raw materials inventory, products inventory, accounts receivable, cash for operating expenses (salaries, utilities, etc. for typically 1-3 months).
3.  **Total Capital Investment (TCI):** Sum of FCI and WC. Represents total funds needed to start operation.
    `TCI = FCI + WC`

**Methods for Capital Cost Estimation (Varying Accuracy):**

1.  **Order-of-Magnitude Estimate ("Ballpark"):**
    *   **Accuracy:** ±30% to ±50%+. Lowest accuracy.
    *   **Method:** Based on limited info, scaling from similar plants. Uses factorial methods:
        -   *Lang Factor Method:* `FCI ≈ Lang Factor × Total Purchased Equipment Cost` (Lang factor typically 3-5).
        -   *Six-Tenths Rule:* `Cost₂ = Cost₁ × (Capacity₂ / Capacity₁)⁰.⁶` (Scales cost based on capacity change).
    *   **Use:** Early feasibility studies, initial screening.
    *   **Pros/Cons:** Quick, requires minimal info; Low accuracy, relies on assumptions.

2.  **Study Estimate (Preliminary):**
    *   **Accuracy:** ±15% to ±30%. Moderate accuracy.
    *   **Method:** Based on preliminary flow diagrams, equipment lists, some vendor quotes. Factorial methods used with more specific data.
    *   **Use:** Preliminary planning, process selection, initial budget.
    *   **Pros/Cons:** Better accuracy; Still relies on estimations.

3.  **Definitive Estimate (Detailed, Budget):**
    *   **Accuracy:** ±5% to ±15%. High accuracy.
    *   **Method:** Based on detailed design, complete equipment lists with vendor quotes, detailed layout/P&IDs, firm contractor quotes. Detailed material/labor estimates.
    *   **Use:** Detailed planning, budgeting, financing, final investment decisions, cost control.
    *   **Pros/Cons:** High accuracy; Time-consuming, expensive, requires significant engineering effort.

4.  **Detailed/Contractor’s Estimate:**
    *   **Accuracy:** ±5% or less. Highest accuracy.
    *   **Method:** Prepared by contractors based on complete engineering drawings/specs, site surveys, detailed take-offs. Essentially fixed-price bids.
    *   **Use:** Final cost confirmation, contractor bidding, construction cost control.
    *   **Pros/Cons:** Most accurate; Most expensive/time-consuming, requires finalized design, usually for large projects pre-construction.

**Factors Affecting Capital Cost Estimates:**
-   Plant Capacity & Scale (Economies of scale)
-   Process Complexity (More steps/specialized equipment = higher cost)
-   Technology Choice (Novel tech may increase initial cost)
-   Materials of Construction (Stainless steel vs. disposable)
-   Automation Level
-   Plant Location (Land, labor, construction costs vary)
-   Regulatory Requirements (GMP, containment increase costs)
-   Project Timeline & Inflation

Accurate capital cost estimation is critical for investment decisions and requires careful planning and detailed design.
