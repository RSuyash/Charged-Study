# 1.2.2 Screening and Selection Strategies for Feedback Deregulated Mutants

To isolate mutants lacking feedback inhibition or repression, screening and selection strategies are designed to target the regulatory mechanisms. Analog resistance selection is a powerful and commonly used method.

## Analog Resistance Selection

Analog resistance selection leverages the principle that feedback-regulated enzymes are often sensitive to **analogs** of their end products. Analogs are compounds that are structurally similar to the natural metabolite and can mimic its regulatory effects (feedback inhibition or repression) but cannot fully substitute for the metabolite in cellular functions.

**Principle of Analog Resistance Selection:**

1.  **Toxic Analogs:** Use toxic analogs of the target metabolite that exert feedback inhibition or repression, but are not metabolized or utilized by the cell, leading to growth inhibition.
2.  **Selection Pressure:** Apply selection pressure by growing mutagenized cells in a medium containing a toxic analog of the desired metabolite.
3.  **Resistant Mutants:** Wild-type cells with normal feedback regulation will be inhibited or killed by the analog. However, mutants that have lost or reduced feedback sensitivity will be resistant to the analog and will be able to grow in the presence of the analog.
4.  **Deregulated Phenotype:** Analog resistance often correlates with deregulation of feedback control mechanisms, leading to overproduction of the desired metabolite.

**Screening and Selection Strategies using Analog Resistance:**

1.  **Mutagenesis:** Mutagenize a microbial population using chemical, physical, or biological mutagens to generate genetic diversity.
2.  **Plate on Analog-Containing Medium:** Plate mutagenized cells on an agar medium containing a toxic analog of the desired primary metabolite at a concentration that inhibits the growth of the wild-type strain.
3.  **Selection of Resistant Colonies:** Incubate plates and select colonies that are able to grow in the presence of the analog. These colonies are likely to be analog-resistant mutants.
4.  **Confirmation of Analog Resistance:** Re-streak selected colonies on fresh analog-containing medium to confirm their resistance phenotype and eliminate false positives.
5.  **Screening for Overproduction:** Screen analog-resistant mutants for overproduction of the desired primary metabolite. Not all analog-resistant mutants will be overproducers, but analog resistance is a strong indicator of potential deregulation of feedback control. Screening assays (plate assays, quantitative HTS assays) are used to identify overproducing strains among the analog-resistant mutants.
6.  **Characterization of Mutants:** Characterize overproducing, analog-resistant mutants to confirm deregulation of feedback inhibition or repression at the enzyme or gene expression level. Enzyme assays, gene expression analysis (RT-qPCR, transcriptomics), and genetic analysis can be used.

**Examples of Analogs Used for Selection:**

-   **Amino Acid Analogs:**
    -   **Lysine Analogs:** Diaminoethylcysteine (DAEC), S-(2-aminoethyl)-L-cysteine. Used to select for lysine-overproducing mutants of *Corynebacterium glutamicum* and other lysine producers. DAEC inhibits aspartokinase, a key regulatory enzyme in lysine biosynthesis.
    -   **Threonine Analogs:** α-Methylthreonine. Used to select for threonine-overproducing mutants.
    -   **Tryptophan Analogs:** 5-Methyltryptophan. Used to select for tryptophan-overproducing mutants.
    -   **Phenylalanine and Tyrosine Analogs:** *p*Fluorophenylalanine. Used to select for phenylalanine and tyrosine-overproducing mutants.
-   **Nucleotide Analogs:**
    -   **Purine Analogs:** 8-Azaguanine, 6-Mercaptopurine. Used to select for purine nucleotide-overproducing mutants.
    -   **Pyrimidine Analogs:** 5-Fluorouracil, 6-Azauracil. Used to select for pyrimidine nucleotide-overproducing mutants.

**Advantages of Analog Resistance Selection:**

-   Efficient selection method, directly enriching for desired deregulated mutants.
-   Relatively simple and cost-effective screening procedure.
-   Often yields mutants with significant overproduction of the target metabolite.

**Limitations of Analog Resistance Selection:**

-   Not universally applicable; suitable analogs are not available for all metabolic pathways or metabolites.
-   Analog resistance may arise through mechanisms other than feedback deregulation (e.g., altered analog transport, analog detoxification), leading to false positives.
-   May require optimization of analog concentration for effective selection pressure.
-   Detailed biochemical and genetic characterization is needed to confirm feedback deregulation in analog-resistant mutants.
