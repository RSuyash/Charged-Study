# 1.3.1 Mechanisms of Inhibitor and Repressor Action

## 1. Inhibitor Action

Inhibitors are molecules that decrease the rate of enzyme-catalyzed reactions. In metabolic pathways, inhibitors can be metabolic intermediates, end products, or exogenous compounds (e.g., drugs, antibiotics, toxins). Inhibitors can affect enzyme activity through different mechanisms:

![Fig. Types of Inhibitors and their Actions](<../../../V2/Industrial Biotechnology/Unit I 1a83a995df878020b6daf598c47a21ae/image%207.png>)
*Fig. Types of Inhibitors and their Actions*

-   **Competitive Inhibition:**
    -   **Mechanism:** The inhibitor structurally resembles the substrate and competes with the substrate for binding to the enzyme’s active site.
    -   **Effect on Enzyme Kinetics:** Increases the apparent Michaelis constant (Km) but does not affect the maximum velocity (Vmax).
    -   **Overcoming Inhibition:** Can be overcome by increasing the substrate concentration.
    -   **Example:** Malonate inhibiting succinate dehydrogenase.
-   **Non-Competitive Inhibition:**
    -   **Mechanism:** The inhibitor binds to the enzyme at an allosteric site, inducing a conformational change that reduces catalytic activity.
    -   **Effect on Enzyme Kinetics:** Decreases Vmax but does not affect Km.
    -   **Overcoming Inhibition:** Cannot be overcome by increasing substrate concentration.
    -   **Example:** Heavy metal ions binding to essential sulfhydryl groups.
-   **Uncompetitive Inhibition:**
    -   **Mechanism:** The inhibitor binds only to the enzyme-substrate complex, forming an inactive complex.
    -   **Effect on Enzyme Kinetics:** Decreases both Km and Vmax.
    -   **Example:** Less common, can occur in multi-substrate reactions.
-   **Feedback Inhibition (Allosteric):** (See [[1.2.1 Feedback Inhibition Mechanisms]]) The end product acts as an allosteric inhibitor of an early pathway enzyme.

## 2. Repressor Action

Repressors are regulatory proteins that decrease the expression of genes, typically by blocking transcription (negative gene regulation).

-   **Mechanism of Repressor Action:**
    -   **Repressor Protein:** A DNA-binding protein encoded by a regulatory gene.
    -   **Operator Sequence:** A specific DNA sequence near the promoter where the repressor binds.
    -   **Repressor Binding to Operator:** Specific and reversible binding.
    -   **Blocking Transcription:** Bound repressor physically blocks RNA polymerase binding or transcription initiation.
    -   **Regulation by Inducers or Co-repressors:**
        -   **Repression by Co-repressor (Feedback Repression):** Repressor is active only when bound to a co-repressor (often the end product). High end product levels activate the repressor, leading to gene repression. (See [[1.2.1 Feedback Inhibition Mechanisms]])
        -   **Induction by Inducer (Inducible Systems):** Repressor is active alone. An inducer (often a substrate) binds to the repressor, inactivating it and allowing transcription.

        ![Fig. Lac Operon Repressor Function](<../../../V2/Industrial Biotechnology/Unit I 1a83a995df878020b6daf598c47a21ae/3c8d94ba-4cec-4f12-bdf7-19efb5237298.png>)
        *Fig. Lac Operon Repressor Function*

-   **Examples of Repressor Systems:**
    -   ***lac* Operon Repressor (LacI) in *E. coli*:** Regulates lactose metabolism genes. Repressor binds in absence of lactose (inducer). Lactose inactivates repressor, inducing transcription (Inducible system, catabolic repression).
    -   ***trp* Operon Repressor (TrpR) in *E. coli*:** Regulates tryptophan biosynthesis genes. Tryptophan (co-repressor) activates repressor. High tryptophan levels lead to repression (Feedback repression).
