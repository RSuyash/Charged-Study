# 1.3.3 Examples of Resistant Strains Isolated

Selecting for mutants insensitive to inhibitors or repressors has yielded strains valuable for industrial applications.

## 1. Inhibitor-Insensitive Enzyme Mutants

-   **Fluoracetate-Resistant *Aspergillus niger* Mutants (Citric Acid Overproduction):**
    -   **Mechanism:** Resistance due to mutations in the aconitase gene reducing sensitivity to fluoracetate inhibition. Reduced aconitase activity leads to citrate accumulation.
    -   **Application:** Used for industrial citric acid production.
-   **Methotrexate-Resistant *E. coli* Mutants (DHFR Overproduction):**
    -   **Mechanism:** Resistance via DHFR gene amplification (overcoming inhibition by increased enzyme levels) or mutations in the *folA* gene reducing methotrexate binding affinity.
    -   **Application:** Enhanced production of tetrahydrofolate derivatives; relevant to understanding cancer drug resistance.

## 2. Repressor-Insensitive Mutants

-   ***E. coli* Mutants Resistant to Catabolite Repression (Enhanced Lactose Utilization):**
    -   **Mechanism:** Resistance via mutations in genes like *crp* (encoding CRP), *cya* (encoding adenylate cyclase), or the *lac* promoter, making transcription less sensitive to glucose repression.
    -   **Application:** Valuable for bioprocesses using mixed sugar substrates, allowing efficient utilization of sugars normally subject to catabolite repression.
-   ***Bacillus subtilis* Mutants with Deregulated Tryptophan Operon (Tryptophan Overproduction):**
    -   **Mechanism:** Repressor insensitivity due to mutations in the *trpR* gene (encoding TrpR repressor) or the *trp* operator region (reducing repressor binding).
    -   **Application:** Used for industrial L-tryptophan production due to reduced feedback repression.

These examples illustrate how selecting for inhibitor or repressor resistance can lead to strains with enhanced production capabilities or altered metabolic phenotypes beneficial for bioprocessing.
