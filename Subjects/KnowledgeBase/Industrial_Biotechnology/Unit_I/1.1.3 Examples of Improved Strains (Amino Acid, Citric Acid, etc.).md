# 1.1.3 Examples of Improved Strains (Amino Acid, Citric Acid, etc.)

Strain improvement through mutation and selection has been instrumental in enhancing the industrial production of various primary metabolites. Here are some classic examples:

## 1. Amino Acid Overproduction

-   **L-Glutamate Production by *Corynebacterium glutamicum*:** *Corynebacterium glutamicum* is a Gram-positive bacterium widely used for the industrial production of L-glutamate, a flavor enhancer (monosodium glutamate - MSG) and a precursor for other amino acids. Strain improvement efforts have focused on:
    -   **Auxotrophic Mutants:** Early strain improvement strategies involved isolating auxotrophic mutants, particularly **homoserine auxotrophs**. Blocking homoserine synthesis can redirect metabolic flux towards glutamate biosynthesis.
    -   **Feedback-Resistant Mutants:** Mutants resistant to feedback inhibition by glutamate or feedback repression of glutamate biosynthetic enzymes were selected.
    -   **Permeability Mutants:** Mutants with altered membrane permeability that facilitates glutamate excretion into the culture medium were selected.
    -   **Examples of Improved *C. glutamicum* Strains:**
        -   *C. glutamicum* **ATCC 13032** (wild-type, low glutamate production)
        -   *C. glutamicum* **ATCC 14067** (homoserine auxotroph, improved glutamate production)
        -   *C. glutamicum* **KY9102** (feedback-resistant to glutamate analogs, further enhanced glutamate production)

-   **L-Lysine Production by *Corynebacterium glutamicum*:** *C. glutamicum* is also a major industrial producer of L-lysine, an essential amino acid used in animal feed and food fortification. Strain improvement strategies for lysine overproduction include:
    -   **Diaminoethylcysteine (DAEC)-Resistant Mutants:** Mutants resistant to DAEC (a lysine analog) were selected, often carrying mutations in the aspartokinase enzyme, deregulating feedback inhibition.
    -   **Threonine Auxotrophs (in combination with DAEC resistance):** Combining threonine auxotrophy with DAEC resistance further enhances lysine overproduction.
    -   **Examples of Improved *C. glutamicum* Strains:**
        -   *C. glutamicum* **WT** (wild-type, low lysine production)
        -   *C. glutamicum* **AJ3456** (DAEC-resistant, improved lysine production)
        -   *C. glutamicum* **RB41** (DAEC-resistant, threonine auxotroph, high lysine production)

## 2. Citric Acid Production by *Aspergillus niger*

*Aspergillus niger* is a filamentous fungus widely used for the industrial production of citric acid. Strain improvement efforts have focused on enhancing citric acid yield and productivity:

-   **Methanol-Resistant Mutants:** Methanol resistance in some *A. niger* mutants was correlated with increased citric acid production.
-   **Fluoracetate-Resistant Mutants:** Fluoracetate resistance in some *A. niger* strains was associated with reduced aconitase activity, leading to citrate accumulation and citric acid overproduction.
-   **Radiation Mutagenesis (UV and Gamma Irradiation):** UV and gamma irradiation have been used to induce random mutations, followed by screening for strains with increased citric acid production.
-   **Examples of Improved *A. niger* Strains:**
    -   *A. niger* **Wild-type** (moderate citric acid production)
    -   *A. niger* **Methanol-resistant mutants** (improved citric acid production)
    -   *A. niger* **Fluoracetate-resistant mutants** (further enhanced citric acid production)

## General Strategies Illustrated by These Examples

-   **Targeting Regulatory Mechanisms:** Disrupting feedback inhibition and repression.
-   **Metabolic Pathway Engineering through Mutagenesis:** Random mutagenesis and selection.
-   **Auxotrophy as a Tool:** Redirecting metabolic flux.
-   **Resistance to Analogs and Inhibitors:** Selecting for deregulated pathways.
-   **Iterative Strain Improvement:** Multiple rounds of mutagenesis and selection.

These examples demonstrate the power of strain improvement techniques in developing microbial strains with enhanced production capabilities for commercially important primary metabolites.
