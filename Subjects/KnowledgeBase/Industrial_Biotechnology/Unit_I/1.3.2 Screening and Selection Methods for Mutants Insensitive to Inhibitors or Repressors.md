# 1.3.2 Screening and Selection Methods for Mutants Insensitive to Inhibitors or Repressors

To isolate mutants insensitive to inhibitors or repressors, selection strategies are designed to directly select for cells that can grow or function in the presence of inhibitory or repressing compounds.

![Fig. Selection of Mutants Based on Plating Methods](<../../../V2/Industrial Biotechnology/Unit I 1a83a995df878020b6daf598c47a21ae/image%208.png>)
*Fig. Selection of Mutants Based on Plating Methods*

## Screening and Selection Methods for Inhibitor-Insensitive Mutants

1.  **Growth Selection in Inhibitor-Containing Medium:** The most direct selection method.
    *   **Principle:** Plate mutagenized cells on medium containing an inhibitor at a concentration that inhibits wild-type growth. Only inhibitor-insensitive mutants will grow.
    *   **Procedure:**
        1.  Mutagenesis.
        2.  Plate on inhibitor-containing medium.
        3.  Select resistant colonies.
        4.  Confirm resistance.
        5.  Characterize the mechanism of resistance (e.g., altered target enzyme, efflux pumps).
2.  **Enzyme Activity Assay-Based Screening:** Used when inhibitors target specific enzymes.
    *   **Principle:** Screen mutant extracts for enzyme activity maintained in the presence of the inhibitor.
    *   **Procedure:**
        1.  Mutagenesis and cultivation (e.g., microtiter plates).
        2.  Prepare cell-free extracts.
        3.  Perform enzyme assays with the inhibitor.
        4.  Identify mutants with retained activity.
        5.  Characterize the molecular basis (e.g., enzyme gene mutations).

## Screening and Selection Methods for Repressor-Insensitive Mutants

1.  **Growth Selection in the Presence of Repressing Conditions:** Select for mutants that grow under conditions that normally repress gene expression in the wild-type.
    *   **Example: Catabolite Repression Resistance:** Grow mutagenized cells on medium with both a repressing sugar (e.g., glucose) and an inducible sugar (e.g., lactose). Select mutants that utilize the inducible sugar despite the presence of the repressing sugar.
    *   **Procedure:**
        1.  Mutagenesis.
        2.  Plate on repressing condition medium.
        3.  Select colonies with altered growth phenotype.
        4.  Confirm repressor insensitivity.
        5.  Characterize the mechanism (e.g., mutations in repressor gene, operator, regulatory pathway).
2.  **Reporter Gene Assay-Based Screening:** Useful for high-throughput screening.
    *   **Principle:** Fuse a reporter gene (e.g., *lacZ*, GFP) to the promoter regulated by the repressor. Screen for mutants showing increased reporter expression under repressing conditions.
    *   **Procedure:**
        1.  Construct reporter strain.
        2.  Mutagenesis and cultivation.
        3.  Perform reporter assays under repressing conditions.
        4.  Identify mutants with high reporter expression.
        5.  Characterize the molecular basis.
