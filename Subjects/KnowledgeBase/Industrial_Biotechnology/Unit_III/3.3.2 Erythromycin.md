# 3.3.2 Erythromycin

Erythromycin is a macrolide antibiotic produced by *Saccharopolyspora erythraea*. It inhibits bacterial protein synthesis (binding 50S ribosome) and is effective against many Gram-positive bacteria and some Gram-negative/atypical pathogens.

## 3.3.2.1 Microorganisms Involved in Erythromycin Production

-   ***Saccharopolyspora erythraea* (Actinomycete Bacterium):** Primary industrial producer (formerly *Streptomyces erythraeus*). Filamentous, Gram-positive soil bacterium. High-yielding strains developed.

## 3.3.2.2 Fermentation Process for Erythromycin Production

Typically uses submerged fermentation (SmF) in stirred tanks. Erythromycin is a secondary metabolite.

**Submerged Fermentation (SmF):**

-   **Raw Materials:**
    -   **Carbon Source:** Glucose, Sucrose, Glycerol, Soybean/Vegetable Oils (can supplement).
    -   **Nitrogen Source:** Organic (Soybean Flour/Meal, Peptone). Inorganic (Ammonium salts) can supplement.
    -   **Minerals:** Phosphate, Magnesium, Trace Metals (Fe, Zn, Mn, Cu, Co).
-   **Fermentation Conditions:**
    -   **Type:** Fed-batch (common). Allows controlled feeding.
    -   **Aeration:** Strictly Aerobic (requires high DO).
    -   **Temperature:** 28-32°C.
    -   **pH:** Controlled around 6.5-7.5.
    -   **Agitation:** For mixing, O₂ transfer. Optimized for shear sensitivity of filamentous growth.
    -   **Precursor Feeding:** Generally not required, though some amino acids might enhance production.
-   **Process Control:** DO, temperature, pH, nutrient feeding (fed-batch), foam control, SCADA systems.

## 3.3.2.3 Product Recovery and Applications of Erythromycin

**Product Recovery (Downstream Processing):**

Erythromycin is produced both intracellularly and extracellularly.

1.  **Cell Mass Removal:** Centrifugation or filtration to separate *S. erythraea* mycelia.
2.  **Solvent Extraction:** Extract erythromycin from both clarified broth (extracellular) and cell mass (intracellular) using organic solvent (butyl acetate, ethyl acetate, methylene chloride) at alkaline pH (9-10). Cell disruption (homogenization) may be used for intracellular extraction.
3.  **Back Extraction:** Back-extract erythromycin into aqueous phase at acidic pH (2-3) as a water-soluble salt.
4.  **Precipitation/Crystallization:** Precipitate/crystallize erythromycin salt (hydrochloride, lactobionate) from concentrated aqueous solution by adjusting pH, adding counterions, or changing solvent.
5.  **Filtration & Washing:** Separate crystals by filtration, wash impurities.
6.  **Drying & Formulation:** Dry crystals (vacuum, air). Formulate into tablets, capsules, suspensions, injections. Often uses water-soluble salts/derivatives (lactobionate, ethylsuccinate) due to base's poor solubility.

**Applications:**

Macrolide antibiotic used for various bacterial infections, especially in penicillin-allergic patients or for penicillin-resistant strains:
-   Respiratory Tract Infections (pneumonia, atypical pneumonia, whooping cough, diphtheria)
-   Skin & Soft Tissue Infections (acne)
-   Sexually Transmitted Infections (Chlamydia, Gonorrhea alternative)
-   Gastrointestinal Infections (*Campylobacter*)
-   Ocular Infections (conjunctivitis)
-   Prophylaxis (bacterial endocarditis prevention in dental procedures for penicillin-allergic patients)

Erythromycin and derivatives (azithromycin, clarithromycin) are important, but macrolide resistance is increasing.
