# 3.3.1 Penicillin

Penicillin, discovered by Alexander Fleming, is a crucial β-lactam antibiotic inhibiting bacterial cell wall synthesis. Large-scale production was developed by Florey, Chain, and Heatley.

## 3.3.1.1 Microorganisms Involved in Penicillin Production

-   ***Penicillium chrysogenum* (Filamentous Fungus):** Primary industrial producer. High-yielding strains developed through strain improvement. (*P. notatum* was the original discovery source but is less productive).

## 3.3.1.2 Fermentation Process for Penicillin Production

Typically uses submerged fermentation (SmF) in stirred tanks. Penicillin is a secondary metabolite, produced mainly during the stationary phase (idiophase).

**Submerged Fermentation (SmF):**

-   **Raw Materials:**
    -   **Carbon Source:** Lactose (preferred, slowly metabolized, avoids catabolite repression). Glucose (less common due to repression). Corn Steep Liquor (also provides N, growth factors).
    -   **Nitrogen Source:** Corn Steep Liquor (major source).
    -   **Precursors:** Phenylacetic Acid (PAA) for Penicillin G, or Phenoxyacetic Acid (POA) for Penicillin V. Added to direct side chain synthesis. Fed during fermentation.
    -   **Minerals:** Phosphate (limited during production phase to promote synthesis), Sulfate.
-   **Fermentation Conditions:**
    -   **Type:** Fed-batch (predominant mode). Allows controlled feeding of C source and precursors.
    -   **Aeration:** Strictly Aerobic (requires high DO).
    -   **Temperature:** 25-27°C.
    -   **pH:** Controlled around 6.0-6.5 during production.
    -   **Agitation:** For mixing, O₂ transfer. Optimized for shear sensitivity.
    -   **Morphology Control:** Dispersed mycelial growth generally preferred over pellets.
-   **Process Control:** DO, temperature, pH, substrate/precursor feeding rates, phosphate limitation, foam control, SCADA systems.

## 3.3.1.3 Product Recovery and Applications of Penicillin

**Product Recovery (Downstream Processing):**

Penicillin is extracellular.

1.  **Cell Mass Removal:** Filtration (rotary vacuum, pressure) to remove *P. chrysogenum* mycelia.
2.  **Solvent Extraction:** Extract penicillin from clarified broth using organic solvent (butyl acetate, MIBK) at acidic pH (2-3) where penicillin is in free acid form. Countercurrent extraction often used.
3.  **Back Extraction:** Back-extract penicillin into aqueous phase at higher pH (7-8) as a water-soluble salt (K⁺ or Na⁺ salt). Concentrates the product.
4.  **Precipitation/Crystallization:** Precipitate/crystallize penicillin salt (e.g., Potassium Penicillin G) by adding salts (e.g., potassium acetate) at low temperature.
5.  **Filtration & Washing:** Separate crystals by filtration, wash impurities.
6.  **Drying & Formulation:** Dry crystals (vacuum, air). Formulate into powders for injection, tablets, capsules, etc.

**Applications:**

Broad-spectrum antibiotic against Gram-positive and some Gram-negative bacteria. Used for:
-   Respiratory Tract Infections (pneumonia, bronchitis)
-   Skin & Soft Tissue Infections
-   Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs)
-   Sexually Transmitted Infections (Syphilis, Gonorrhea - resistance issues)
-   Meningitis
-   Surgical Prophylaxis
-   Veterinary Medicine

Penicillin and its derivatives remain vital, though resistance is a growing concern.
