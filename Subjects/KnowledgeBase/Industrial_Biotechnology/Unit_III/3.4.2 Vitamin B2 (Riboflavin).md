# 3.4.2 Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)

Vitamin B2 (riboflavin) is a water-soluble vitamin, precursor for coenzymes FAD and FMN involved in redox reactions. Produced industrially by fermentation (preferred over chemical synthesis).

## 3.4.2.1 Microorganisms Involved in Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin) Production

-   **Bacteria (Predominant Modern Producers):**
    -   ***Bacillus subtilis*:** Predominant industrial producer. Genetically engineered strains achieve high yields. Advantages: genetic manipulability, fast growth, established fermentation.
    -   ***Corynebacterium ammoniagenes*:** Also used, less common than *B. subtilis*.
-   **Fungi (Historically Important, Less Used Now):**
    -   ***Ashbya gossypii*:** Filamentous fungus, high producer, but morphological issues (viscosity).
    -   ***Eremothecium ashbyii*:** Filamentous fungus, high producer, still used in some regions.

*B. subtilis* is currently favored due to high yields and genetic tractability.

## 3.4.2.2 Fermentation Process for Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin) Production

Typically uses submerged fermentation (SmF), primarily with *Bacillus subtilis*.

**Submerged Fermentation (SmF):**

-   **Raw Materials:**
    -   **Carbon Source:** Glucose, Molasses, Vegetable Oils (can supplement or be primary for *E. ashbyii*).
    -   **Nitrogen Source:** Organic (Yeast Extract, Corn Steep Liquor). Inorganic (Ammonium salts) can supplement.
    -   **Minerals:** Phosphate, Magnesium, Trace Metals (Fe, Zn, Mn, Mo). Iron concentration is critical.
    -   **Precursors (Optional):** Ribose, guanosine (less common for *B. subtilis*).
-   **Fermentation Conditions:**
    -   **Type:** Fed-batch (common). Allows controlled feeding.
    -   **Aeration:** Aerobic (requires sufficient DO).
    -   **Temperature:** 30-35°C for *B. subtilis*, 25-30°C for *E. ashbyii*.
    -   **pH:** Controlled around 6.0-7.0.
    -   **Agitation:** For mixing, O₂ transfer.
    -   **Iron Limitation (for *B. subtilis*):** Key factor promoting riboflavin overproduction. Low Fe channels precursors towards riboflavin. Iron chelators may be used.
    -   **Purine Nucleotide Limitation (for *B. subtilis*):** Can also enhance overproduction by making precursors available.
-   **Process Control:** DO, temperature, pH, nutrient feeding (fed-batch), foam control, SCADA systems.

## 3.4.2.3 Product Recovery and Applications of Vitamin B2 (Riboflavin)

**Product Recovery (Downstream Processing):**

Depends on microorganism (extracellular for *B. subtilis*, intracellular for *E. ashbyii*).

1.  **Cell Mass Removal/Clarification:** Centrifugation/filtration. Necessary for intracellular recovery (*E. ashbyii*), may be needed for clarification (*B. subtilis*).
2.  **Riboflavin Extraction:**
    -   **Extracellular (*B. subtilis*):** Recover from clarified broth via adsorption chromatography (activated carbon) or ion-exchange chromatography.
    -   **Intracellular (*E. ashbyii*):** Cell disruption (physical/chemical lysis) followed by solvent extraction or aqueous extraction from lysate.
3.  **Purification (Chromatography):** Adsorption chromatography, Ion-Exchange (IEX).
4.  **Crystallization:** Final purification step. Adjust solvent, temperature, pH to induce crystallization from purified solution. Riboflavin has limited water solubility.
5.  **Drying:** Separate crystals (filtration/centrifugation) and dry.

**Applications:**

-   **Nutritional Supplement & Food Fortification (Primary Use):** Treat/prevent deficiency (ariboflavinosis). Fortification of cereals, flour, milk, infant formulas.
-   **Pharmaceutical:** Treatment of deficiency, component of multivitamins/B-complex, adjunct phototherapy for neonatal jaundice.
-   **Animal Feed Additive:** Ensures adequate intake for growth/health (poultry, swine, aquaculture).
-   **Food Colorant:** Natural yellow colorant (E101), limited use.
-   **Research & Diagnostics:** Biochemical studies, fluorescence microscopy (due to fluorescence). FAD/FMN are key coenzymes.
