## Nystatin

Nystatin (brand names: Mycostatin, Nilstat, Nyamyc, Nystop, Nystex) is a polyene macrolide antifungal antibiotic produced by *Streptomyces noursei*. It is primarily used topically to treat localized *Candida* infections.

**Structure**

-   Polyene Macrolide: Large lactone ring with conjugated double bonds (tetraene in nystatin).
-   Mycosamine Sugar: Deoxysugar attached to the macrolide ring.
    ![Fig. Nystatin Structure](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/PB-Unit-III/image 6.png>)

## Mechanism of Action

1.  **Ergosterol Binding:** High affinity for ergosterol (fungal membrane sterol).
2.  **Pore Formation:** Nystatin molecules aggregate, forming transmembrane channels/pores in the fungal membrane (barrel-stave mechanism).
3.  **Increased Membrane Permeability:** Pores disrupt membrane integrity, leading to leakage of cellular contents and osmotic imbalance.
4.  **Fungicidal/Fungistatic Activity:** Concentration-dependent. High concentrations are fungicidal (cell killing); lower concentrations are fungistatic (growth inhibition).
5.  **Selectivity:** Higher affinity for ergosterol (fungal) than cholesterol (mammalian), limiting toxicity to human cells.

## Applications

Primarily for localized *Candida* infections due to poor systemic absorption.

-   **Topical/Mucocutaneous Candidiasis:**
    -   *Cutaneous:* Diaper rash, skin folds.
    -   *Oral:* Thrush (oral candidiasis).
    -   *Esophageal:* Mild cases.
    -   *Vulvovaginal:* Vaginal yeast infections.
    -   *Anal:* Perianal candidiasis.
-   **Prophylaxis of Candidiasis:** (Limited role)
    -   Immunocompromised patients (AIDS, transplant, chemotherapy).
    -   During antibiotic therapy.
    -   Prevention in neonates (off-label).
-   **Combination Products:** With antibacterials/anti-inflammatories for mixed infections.

## Pharmacokinetics

-   Minimal Systemic Absorption: Negligible absorption after oral, topical, or vaginal use.
-   Local Action: Confined to site of application (GI tract, skin, mucous membranes).
-   Not Systemically Metabolized.
-   Fecal Excretion (Oral): Excreted in feces unchanged.

## Side Effects

Generally very safe due to poor absorption.

-   Local Reactions (Topical): Skin irritation, redness, itching (rare).
-   Gastrointestinal Disturbances (Oral): Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea (usually mild). Poor taste.
-   Systemic Toxicity: Minimal due to poor absorption.
