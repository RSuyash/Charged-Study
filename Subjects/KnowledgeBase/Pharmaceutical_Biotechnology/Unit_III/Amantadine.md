# Amantadine

Amantadine (brand names: Gocovri, Symmetrel) is a synthetic adamantane derivative with antiviral and antiparkinsonian properties.

**Structure**

-   Adamantane core (C₁₀H₁₆): Cage-like hydrocarbon.
-   Amino Substitution: 1-adamantanamine hydrochloride.

## Mechanism of Action

-   **Antiviral (Influenza A):** Targets M2 protein (proton channel) of influenza A virus. Blocks M2, preventing virion acidification and viral RNA release. Resistance develops rapidly via M2 mutations.
-   **Antiparkinsonian:** Enhances dopamine release, inhibits dopamine reuptake, may stimulate dopamine receptors, and has weak NMDA receptor antagonism and sigma σ₁ receptor agonism.

## Applications

-   **Parkinson's Disease:** Symptom relief (bradykinesia, rigidity, tremor), treatment of levodopa-induced dyskinesia.
-   **Influenza A (Historically):** Limited current use due to widespread resistance.

## Pharmacokinetics

-   **Absorption:** Well-absorbed orally (high bioavailability).
-   **Distribution:** Widely distributed, crosses blood-brain barrier.
-   **Metabolism:** Minimal hepatic metabolism.
-   **Excretion:** Primarily renal. Dosage adjustment needed in renal impairment.

## Side Effects

-   Neurological: Drowsiness, insomnia, dizziness, anxiety, confusion, hallucinations, seizures (rare).
-   Anticholinergic: Dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision.
-   Cardiovascular: Orthostatic hypotension, peripheral edema.
-   Other: Livedo reticularis (skin discoloration).

Due to resistance, amantadine's use is now limited primarily to Parkinson's disease.
