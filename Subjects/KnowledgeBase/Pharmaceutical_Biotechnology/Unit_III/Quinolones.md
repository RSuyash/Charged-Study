# Quinolones/Fluoroquinolones

Quinolones are a class of synthetic antibacterial agents valued for their broad-spectrum activity. Fluoroquinolones, distinguished by a fluorine atom, are a clinically important subgroup.

**Structure**

Characterized by a bicyclic quinolone ring system. Substitutions at various positions (especially C-6, N-1, C-7, C-8) affect activity and pharmacokinetics.

-   **Fluoroquinolones:** Have a fluorine atom at position 6 (e.g., Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin).

**Mechanism of Action**

Inhibit bacterial DNA replication/repair by targeting DNA gyrase (Gram-negative) and topoisomerase IV (Gram-positive).

-   **Dual Target Enzymes:**
    -   *DNA Gyrase:* Introduces negative supercoils (essential for DNA compaction/replication).
    -   *Topoisomerase IV:* Decatenates daughter DNA molecules (essential for chromosome segregation).
-   **Stabilization of Cleavage Complexes:** Quinolones form a ternary complex with topoisomerase and DNA, preventing DNA religation and causing double-strand breaks, leading to cell death.

**Applications**

Fluoroquinolones are used to treat diverse bacterial infections:

-   Urinary Tract Infections (UTIs)
-   Respiratory Tract Infections (Pneumonia, Bronchitis, Sinusitis)
-   Prostatitis
-   Skin and Soft Tissue Infections
-   Intra-abdominal Infections (with other antibiotics)
-   Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)
-   Bone and Joint Infections (Osteomyelitis)
-   Anthrax
-   Traveler's Diarrhea

**Resistance Mechanisms**

-   **Mutations in Target Genes:** *gyrA/gyrB* (DNA gyrase), *parC/parE* (topoisomerase IV).
-   **Efflux Pumps:** Overexpression of pumps (e.g., AcrAB-TolC in *E. coli*, NorA in *S. aureus*) actively exports quinolones.
-   **Plasmid-Mediated Resistance:** *qnr* genes protect DNA gyrase; *aac(6')-Ib-cr* acetylates ciprofloxacin/norfloxacin.
-   **Target Protection:** *MfpA* protein in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* protects gyrase.

**Pharmacokinetics**

-   **Absorption:** Generally well-absorbed orally (70-95% bioavailability), but absorption is reduced by divalent/trivalent cations (antacids, supplements, dairy).
-   **Distribution:** Excellent tissue penetration (prostate, bone, lung, kidney), intracellular accumulation.
-   **Metabolism:** Variable hepatic metabolism (CYP450s).
-   **Excretion:** Primarily renal. Dosage adjustment needed in renal impairment.

**Adverse Effects**

-   Gastrointestinal Disturbances
-   Central Nervous System (CNS) Effects: Headache, dizziness, seizures (rare).
-   QT Prolongation: Risk of arrhythmias (torsades de pointes).
-   Tendon Rupture/Tendinitis: Rare but serious.
-   Peripheral Neuropathy: Can be irreversible.
-   Photosensitivity: Increased sun sensitivity.
-   Dysglycemia: Hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia (risk in diabetics).
-   *Clostridioides difficile* Infection (CDI): Risk due to gut microbiota disruption.

Judicious use, monitoring, and patient counseling are essential for quinolone therapy.
