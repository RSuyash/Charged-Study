# Azidothymidine (AZT)

Azidothymidine (AZT), also known as zidovudine (ZDV), is a synthetic thymidine analog and a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI). It was the first antiretroviral drug approved for HIV/AIDS.

**Structure**

AZT is a thymidine analog with a key structural modification:

![Fig. Azidothymidine along with Thymidine](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/PB-Unit-III/image 5.png>)

-   **Thymidine Analog:** Resembles deoxythymidine (dT), a natural DNA building block.
-   **Azido Group Substitution:** The 3'-hydroxyl group (-OH) on deoxyribose is replaced with an azido group (-N₃). This prevents phosphodiester bond formation, causing chain termination.
-   **Chemical Formula:** C₁₀H₁₃N₅O₄

## Mechanism of Action

AZT inhibits HIV replication by targeting HIV reverse transcriptase (RT).

1.  **Intracellular Phosphorylation:** AZT is a prodrug, requiring phosphorylation by cellular kinases to its active form, ZDV-triphosphate (ZDV-TP).
2.  **Inhibition of HIV Reverse Transcriptase:** ZDV-TP competes with dTTP for binding to HIV reverse transcriptase.
3.  **Chain Termination:** ZDV-TP is incorporated into the growing viral DNA chain, but lacks a 3'-OH group, causing chain termination and halting viral DNA synthesis.
4.  **Selectivity:** AZT has higher affinity for HIV reverse transcriptase than human DNA polymerases, and human cells have better DNA repair mechanisms, contributing to its selectivity. However, mitochondrial toxicity can occur with long-term use.

## Applications

-   **HIV Treatment:** A cornerstone of combination antiretroviral therapy (ART), suppressing viral load and improving immune function.
-   **Prevention of Mother-to-Child Transmission (PMTCT):** Reduces vertical HIV transmission rates.

## Pharmacokinetics

-   **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed orally (60-70% bioavailability).
-   **Distribution:** Widely distributed, crosses blood-brain barrier.
-   **Metabolism:** Hepatic glucuronidation (UGT enzymes).
-   **Excretion:** Primarily renal (unchanged drug and glucuronide metabolite). Dosage adjustment needed in renal impairment.

## Side Effects

-   Hematological Toxicity: Bone marrow suppression (anemia, neutropenia).
-   Gastrointestinal Disturbances: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea.
-   Mitochondrial Toxicity: Myopathy, lactic acidosis (rare but serious).
-   Genotoxic Effects/Carcinogenicity Concerns: Potential long-term risks.

While newer antiretrovirals have improved efficacy/tolerability, AZT remains important, especially in resource-limited settings and PMTCT.
