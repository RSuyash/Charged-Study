# Sulfonamides

Sulfonamides (sulfa drugs) are a class of synthetic antibacterial agents that inhibit bacterial folate synthesis. They have historically been critical in treating bacterial infections.

**Structure**

Characterized by a sulfonamide group (-SO₂NH₂) attached to an aromatic amine.

![Fig. General structure of sulphonamides; R=R1=H for sulphanilamide.](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/PB-Unit-III/image.png>)
*Fig. General structure of sulphonamides*

-   **Chemical Formula:** C₆H₈N₂O₂S (for sulfanilamide)
-   **Core Structure:** Benzene ring with amino (-NH₂) and sulfonamide (-SO₂NH₂) groups.
-   **Sulfanilamide:** Prototype for many derivatives.

**Mechanism of Action**

Sulfonamides are competitive inhibitors of **dihydropteroate synthase (DHPS)**, an enzyme crucial for bacterial folate synthesis.

![Fig. Mechanism of Action of Sulfonamides](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/PB-Unit-III/image 2.png>)
*Fig. Mechanism of Action of Sulfonamides*

1.  **Folate Synthesis Pathway:** Bacteria synthesize folate from para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA).
2.  **Inhibition of DHPS:** Sulfonamides mimic PABA, competitively binding to DHPS and preventing the formation of dihydropteroate.
3.  **Inhibition of Nucleic Acid/Protein Synthesis:** Without folate, bacteria cannot synthesize nucleic acids/proteins, leading to growth inhibition (bacteriostatic).
4.  **Selective Toxicity:** Humans obtain folate from diet, so sulfonamides selectively target bacteria.

**Applications**

-   **Treatment of Infections:** Effective against UTIs, respiratory infections, some GI infections, *Nocardia*, *Actinomyces*, some protozoa.
-   **Combination Therapy:** Often combined with diaminopyrimidines (e.g., trimethoprim) for synergistic effect (sequential blockade of folate synthesis).
-   **Veterinary Medicine:** Widely used in livestock/pets.

**Resistance Mechanisms**

1.  **Mutations in DHPS:** Reduce sulfonamide binding affinity.
2.  **Increased PABA Production:** Outcompetes sulfonamides.
3.  **Acquisition of Resistance Genes:** Encode altered DHPS or other folate enzymes.
4.  **Alternative Pathways:** Bypass the need for folate synthesis.

**Pharmacokinetics**

-   **Absorption:** Well-absorbed orally.
-   **Distribution:** Widely distributed.
-   **Metabolism:** Hepatic metabolism.
-   **Excretion:** Primarily renal.

**Adverse Effects**

-   Hypersensitivity Reactions: Rash, Stevens-Johnson syndrome.
-   Hematological Effects: Agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia.
-   Renal Toxicity: Crystalluria (requires hydration).
-   Gastrointestinal Disturbances.
