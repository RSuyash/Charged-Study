# 1.4.4 Elicitation Strategies

**Elicitation** is a technique used, particularly in plant cell/tissue/organ cultures, to enhance the production of secondary metabolites by applying external stimuli (elicitors) that trigger plant defense or stress responses. These responses often involve the upregulation of biosynthetic pathways leading to the accumulation of specific secondary metabolites.

**Types of Elicitors:**

1.  **Biotic Elicitors:** Derived from biological sources, often mimicking pathogen attack or herbivory.
    -   *Microbial Elicitors:* Components derived from microorganisms (fungi, bacteria).
        -   **Polysaccharides:** Chitin, chitosan (from fungal cell walls), glucans, oligosaccharides.
        -   **Proteins/Peptides:** Flagellin fragments, elicitins, glycoproteins from microbial cell walls or secretions.
        -   **Whole Microbial Cells/Extracts:** Preparations from yeast (Yeast Extract), fungal mycelia, or bacterial cells.
    -   *Plant-Derived Elicitors:* Endogenous signaling molecules released upon stress or damage.

2.  **Abiotic Elicitors:** Non-biological factors, including physical stresses and chemical compounds.
    -   *Physical Stresses:*
        -   **UV Radiation:** UV-B or UV-C light exposure.
        -   **Osmotic Stress:** High salt (NaCl) or sugar (mannitol, sorbitol) concentrations.
        -   **Temperature Stress:** Heat shock or cold shock.
        -   **Mechanical Stress:** Wounding, high shear stress in bioreactors, sonication.
        -   **Heavy Metals:** Ions like Cu²⁺, Cd²⁺, Hg²⁺ (often toxic but can elicit responses at low concentrations).
    -   *Chemical Elicitors:*
        -   **Signaling Molecules:** Jasmonic acid (JA), Methyl jasmonate (MeJA), Salicylic acid (SA) - key plant defense hormones.
        -   **Inorganic Salts:** Vanadyl sulfate, silver nitrate.
        -   **Other Chemicals:** Ethanol, detergents (at low concentrations).

**Mechanism of Action:**
Elicitors are perceived by plant cells through specific receptors, triggering intracellular signaling cascades (often involving reactive oxygen species (ROS), calcium ions, protein kinases). These cascades lead to the activation of defense-related genes, including those encoding enzymes in secondary metabolite biosynthetic pathways (e.g., PAL, CHS in phenylpropanoid pathway; HMGR, DXS in terpenoid pathways).

**Application:**
Elicitors are typically added to the culture medium at a specific time point during the culture cycle (often late exponential or stationary phase) for a defined duration. The type of elicitor, its concentration, timing, and duration of exposure need to be carefully optimized for each specific plant culture system and target metabolite to maximize yield without causing excessive toxicity or growth inhibition.

Elicitation is a powerful strategy to significantly enhance the productivity of plant-derived secondary metabolites in controlled culture systems.
