# 1.1.1 Definition and Characteristics of Secondary Metabolites

**Definition:**
Secondary metabolites (also specialized metabolites or secondary products) are organic compounds synthesized by organisms (plants, fungi, bacteria, animals) that are **not directly involved** in the organism's normal growth, development, or reproduction. Instead, they often play roles in ecological interactions, defense, and survival.

**Characteristics:**

-   **Non-Essential for Basic Growth:** Unlike primary metabolites (e.g., carbohydrates, amino acids, lipids, nucleic acids) required for fundamental cellular processes, secondary metabolites are not essential for the organism's immediate survival under optimal conditions.
-   **Production Phase:** Typically produced during the **stationary phase** of growth (idiophase), often triggered by nutrient limitation or environmental stress, contrasting with primary metabolites produced during the active growth phase (trophophase).
-   **Chemical Diversity:** Exhibit a vast array of complex and diverse chemical structures (e.g., alkaloids, terpenoids, phenolics, polyketides). This diversity enables a wide range of biological activities and ecological functions.
-   **Low Abundance:** Generally produced in smaller quantities compared to primary metabolites, making their extraction, isolation, and characterization more challenging.
-   **Species/Group Specificity:** Often specific to certain species, genera, or taxonomic groups, reflecting specialized ecological roles or evolutionary adaptations.
