# 1.2.4 Other Classes of Secondary Metabolites

Beyond alkaloids, terpenoids, and major phenolic groups, several other important classes of secondary metabolites exist with diverse structures and biological activities.

## 1. Glycosides

Glycosides consist of a sugar moiety (**glycone**) linked via a **glycosidic bond** to a non-sugar component (**aglycone** or genin). Glycosylation often affects solubility, stability, and activity.

**Structure:**
-   **Glycone:** Usually mono- or oligosaccharides (e.g., glucose, rhamnose). Glucosides contain glucose.
-   **Aglycone:** Diverse structures (terpenoid, steroid, flavonoid, alkaloid, phenolic, etc.), determines the type of glycoside.
-   **Glycosidic Bond:** Links anomeric carbon of sugar to aglycone (O-, N-, S-, C-glycosidic bonds; O- most common).

![Glycoside Structure Example](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit I 1a63a995df8780a992d1dcb3e444b190/image%203.png>)
*Fig: Example of a glycoside structure (Salicin)*

**Types & Examples:**
-   **Cardiac Glycosides:** Steroidal aglycone. Affect heart contractility (inhibit Na+/K+ ATPase). E.g., Digoxin, Digitoxin (from *Digitalis purpurea*). Used for heart failure, atrial fibrillation.
-   **Flavonoid Glycosides:** Flavonoid aglycone. Enhance solubility/stability. E.g., Rutin, Quercetin glycosides, Anthocyanins (pigments). Antioxidant, vasoprotective.
-   **Anthraquinone Glycosides:** Anthraquinone aglycone. Laxative properties. E.g., Sennosides (from *Senna*), Aloe-emodin glycosides (from *Aloe*). Stimulate colon peristalsis.
-   **Cyanogenic Glycosides:** Release hydrogen cyanide (HCN) upon hydrolysis (defense). E.g., Amygdalin (bitter almonds), Linamarin (flax).
-   **Glucosinolates:** Sulfur-containing aglycone, glucose glycone. Found in *Brassicaceae*. Hydrolyze to isothiocyanates (pungent flavor, anticancer). E.g., Sulforaphane (from broccoli).
-   **Saponins:** (See below) Triterpenoid or steroid aglycones with sugar chains.

## 2. Saponins

Glycosides with **amphipathic** nature (hydrophobic aglycone + hydrophilic sugar chains) and **soap-like foaming** properties.

**Structure:**
-   **Aglycone (Sapogenin):** Lipophilic. Triterpenoid (C30) or Steroid (C27).
-   **Glycoside Chains:** Hydrophilic. One or more sugar chains attached to sapogenin.

![General Structure of Saponins](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit I 1a63a995df8780a992d1dcb3e444b190/image%204.png>)
*Fig: General Structure of Saponins*

**Properties & Activities:**
-   **Surfactant/Foaming:** Reduce surface tension, form stable foams.
-   **Hemolytic Activity:** Lyse red blood cells by disrupting cholesterol-containing membranes.
-   **Membrane Permeabilization/Cytotoxicity:** Disrupt other cell membranes; activity against cancer cells, microbes.
-   **Immune Modulation:** Act as adjuvants (enhance immune response). E.g., Quil-A (from *Quillaja saponaria*), Ginsenosides (from *Panax ginseng*).
-   **Cholesterol Metabolism:** Reduce cholesterol absorption, increase bile acid excretion.
-   **Antimicrobial/Antifungal:** Disrupt microbial membranes.
-   **Ecological Role:** Plant defense (bitter taste, toxicity).

**Sources:** Legumes (*Fabaceae*), Ginseng (*Araliaceae*), Quinoa, Licorice (*Glycyrrhiza glabra*), Soapbark tree (*Quillaja saponaria*), Yucca.

## 3. Polyketides

Diverse class synthesized by **Polyketide Synthases (PKSs)** from simple acyl-CoA units (similar to fatty acid synthesis but more complex).

**Biosynthesis:**
-   **PKS Types:**
    -   *Type I:* Modular, multi-domain enzymes (fungi, bacteria). Produce complex macrocycles (e.g., Erythromycin, Lovastatin).
    -   *Type II:* Dissociated, iterative enzymes (bacteria). Produce aromatic polyketides (e.g., Tetracyclines, Doxorubicin).
    -   *Type III:* Simpler, single-domain enzymes (plants, some microbes). Often involved in flavonoid synthesis.
-   **Building Blocks:** Starter units (acetyl-CoA, propionyl-CoA) and extender units (malonyl-CoA, methylmalonyl-CoA).
-   **Diversity:** Generated by choice of units, degree of reduction/modification during chain elongation, cyclization patterns. Structures: linear, aromatic, macrocyclic, polycyclic.

![Polyketide Example - Lovastatin](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit I 1a63a995df8780a992d1dcb3e444b190/image%205.png>)
*Fig: Polyketide Example - Lovostatin*

**Pharmacologically Important Examples:**
-   **Antibiotics:** Macrolides (Erythromycin), Tetracyclines, Anthracyclines (Doxorubicin - anticancer).
-   **Antifungals:** Polyenes (Amphotericin B, Nystatin).
-   **Immunosuppressants:** Rapamycin (Sirolimus), Tacrolimus (FK506 - hybrid PKS/NRPS).
-   **Cholesterol-Lowering Agents:** Statins (Lovastatin, Simvastatin - HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors).
-   **Natural Pigments:** Anthraquinones.

## 4. Lignans

Polyphenolic compounds formed by **dimerization of phenylpropanoids** (often coniferyl alcohol units), typically via β-β' (C8-C8') coupling, resulting in a **2,3-dibenzylbutane** skeleton.

**Structure:**
-   Derived from phenylpropanoid pathway.
-   Possess chiral centers, leading to stereoisomers.
-   Diversity from monomers, linkage types, further modifications.

![Lignan Structure Example - Guaiacyl lignin monomer unit](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit I 1a63a995df8780a992d1dcb3e444b190/image%206.png>)
*Fig: Phenylpropanoid monomer (Coniferyl alcohol) involved in lignan/lignin synthesis*

**Occurrence & Activities:**
-   **Sources:** Widely distributed in plants (seeds - flaxseed, sesame; whole grains, fruits, vegetables).
-   **Antioxidant Properties:** Scavenge free radicals.
-   **Phytoestrogenic Effects:** Metabolized by gut bacteria to enterolignans (enterodiol, enterolactone) which bind estrogen receptors (estrogenic/anti-estrogenic effects). Relevant for hormone-related conditions.
-   **Anticancer Potential:** Antiproliferative, apoptosis induction, antiangiogenesis, modulation of estrogen metabolism. E.g., Secoisolariciresinol diglycoside (SDG), matairesinol.
-   **Cardiovascular Benefits:** Improve lipid profile, reduce blood pressure, anti-inflammatory, antiplatelet.

## 5. Coumarins

Aromatic compounds with a **benzo-α-pyrone** ring system.

**Structure & Classification:**
-   Basic structure: Benzene fused to α-pyrone (lactone).
-   Diversity from substituents (-OH, -OCH3, isoprenyl, glycosyl).
-   **Types:** Simple coumarins (umbelliferone), Furanocoumarins (psoralen - photosensitizing), Pyranocoumarins.

**Activities & Applications:**
-   **Anticoagulant Activity:** Vitamin K antagonists (inhibit VKOR). E.g., Dicoumarol (from spoiled sweet clover), Warfarin (synthetic derivative). Used to treat/prevent blood clots.
-   **Photosensitization (Furanocoumarins):** Activated by UVA light, form DNA adducts. Used in PUVA therapy (psoriasis, vitiligo). Can cause phytophotodermatitis. E.g., Psoralens, Bergapten (in citrus, celery).
-   **Antimicrobial/Antifungal.**
-   **Antioxidant/Anti-inflammatory.**
-   **Other:** Anticancer, antiviral, neuroprotective potential.

**Sources:** *Fabaceae* (sweet clover), *Apiaceae* (celery, parsley), *Rutaceae* (citrus), *Asteraceae*.

## 6. Essential Oils

Complex, volatile, fragrant mixtures extracted from aromatic plants (distillation, expression). Primarily composed of **terpenes/terpenoids**, plus other volatile compounds (phenylpropanoids, aliphatics).

**Composition:**
-   Major components: Monoterpenes (C10), Sesquiterpenes (C15) and their oxygenated derivatives (alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, esters, phenols, ethers).
-   Minor components: Phenylpropanoids (eugenol, cinnamaldehyde), aliphatic compounds.
-   Composition varies greatly (plant species, origin, conditions, extraction method).

**Properties & Applications:**
-   **Aromatherapy:** Psychological effects (relaxing, stimulating, mood-elevating) via olfactory system and limbic system interaction. E.g., Lavender, Peppermint, Citrus oils.
-   **Antimicrobial/Antiseptic:** Broad-spectrum activity (bacteria, fungi, viruses). E.g., Tea tree, Thyme, Oregano, Clove oils. Used topically, in disinfectants.
-   **Food Preservation:** Natural preservatives (inhibit microbes, oxidation). E.g., Rosemary, Thyme, Clove oils.
-   **Fragrances & Flavors:** Used in perfumes, cosmetics, food, beverages. E.g., Citrus oils, Peppermint, Cinnamon, Vanilla.
-   **Insect Repellent/Insecticidal:** E.g., Citronella, Eucalyptus, Neem oils.

**Extraction Methods:** Steam distillation (most common), hydrodistillation, expression (citrus peels), solvent extraction (absolutes), supercritical fluid extraction (SFE with CO2).
