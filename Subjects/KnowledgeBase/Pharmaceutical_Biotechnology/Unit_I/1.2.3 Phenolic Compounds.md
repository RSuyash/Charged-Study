# 1.2.3 Phenolic Compounds

**Overview:**
Phenolic compounds (phenolics) are a diverse group of secondary metabolites characterized by one or more hydroxyl (-OH) groups attached to an aromatic ring (phenol structure). They are widespread in plants and contribute significantly to plant physiology, ecology, and human health (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory properties).

**Structure & Classification:**

1.  **Simple Phenols:** Single aromatic ring with -OH group(s) (e.g., phenol, catechol, hydroquinone).
2.  **Phenolic Acids:** Aromatic acids with a phenolic ring and carboxyl group.
    -   *Hydroxybenzoic Acids:* e.g., salicylic acid, gallic acid, vanillic acid.
    -   *Hydroxycinnamic Acids:* e.g., caffeic acid, ferulic acid, p-coumaric acid, sinapic acid. Often occur as esters.
3.  **Flavonoids:** Large group with a C6-C3-C6 skeleton (two aromatic rings linked by a 3-carbon bridge). Major subclasses include:
    -   *Flavonols:* e.g., quercetin, kaempferol.
    -   *Flavones:* e.g., apigenin, luteolin.
    -   *Flavanones:* e.g., naringenin, hesperetin.
    -   *Flavan-3-ols:* e.g., catechins (in tea), epicatechin.
    -   *Anthocyanidins/Anthocyanins:* Pigments (red, blue, purple), e.g., cyanidin, delphinidin (glycosides are anthocyanins).
    -   *Isoflavones:* e.g., genistein, daidzein (common in legumes, phytoestrogens).
4.  **Tannins:** High molecular weight polyphenols that precipitate proteins (astringency).
    -   *Hydrolyzable Tannins:* Esters of gallic acid or ellagic acid with a sugar core (e.g., tannic acid).
    -   *Condensed Tannins (Proanthocyanidins):* Polymers of flavan-3-ol units (e.g., procyanidins in grapes, berries).
5.  **Lignans:** Dimers of phenylpropanoid units (see [[1.2.4 Other Classes of Secondary Metabolites]]).
6.  **Stilbenes:** C6-C2-C6 structure (e.g., resveratrol in grapes).
7.  **Lignins:** Complex, irregular polymers of phenylpropanoid units, providing structural support in plant cell walls. Not typically considered a discrete secondary metabolite class in the same way as others.

**Biosynthesis:**
Primarily synthesized via:
-   **Shikimic Acid Pathway:** Produces aromatic amino acids (phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan), precursors for many phenolics.
-   **Phenylpropanoid Pathway:** Starts from phenylalanine/tyrosine, leads to hydroxycinnamic acids, flavonoids, lignans, stilbenes, lignin monomers. Key enzyme: Phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL).
-   **Acetate-Malonate Pathway (Polyketide Pathway):** Contributes to parts of flavonoid structure (A ring) and other phenolics.

**Sources:**
Abundant in plants:
-   Fruits (berries, apples, grapes - rich in flavonoids, anthocyanins, tannins).
-   Vegetables (onions, broccoli, spinach - rich in flavonols, phenolic acids).
-   Grains (whole grains - rich in phenolic acids, lignans).
-   Legumes (soybeans - rich in isoflavones).
-   Beverages (tea, coffee, red wine - rich in catechins, phenolic acids, tannins, resveratrol).
-   Herbs and Spices (turmeric - curcumin, oregano - phenolic acids).

**Examples & Activities:**
-   **Quercetin (Flavonol):** Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory. Found in onions, apples, berries.
-   **Anthocyanins (Flavonoid):** Pigments, strong antioxidants. Found in berries, red cabbage, purple grapes.
-   **Caffeic Acid (Phenolic Acid):** Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory. Found in coffee, fruits.
-   **Ferulic Acid (Phenolic Acid):** Antioxidant. Found in grains, seeds.
-   **Tannins:** Astringent, antioxidant, protein-binding. Found in tea, wine, nuts.
-   **Resveratrol (Stilbene):** Antioxidant, cardioprotective. Found in grapes, red wine.
-   **Curcumin (Diarylheptanoid - often grouped with phenolics):** Anti-inflammatory, antioxidant. Found in turmeric.

**Functions in Plants:**
-   **Defense:** Phytoalexins (antimicrobial), feeding deterrents (tannins), insect repellents.
-   **UV Protection:** Flavonoids absorb UV radiation.
-   **Attractants:** Pigments (anthocyanins) attract pollinators/seed dispersers.
-   **Structural Support:** Lignin strengthens cell walls.
-   **Allelopathy:** Inhibit growth of competing plants.

**Medicinal Applications & Health Benefits:**
-   **Antioxidant Activity:** Neutralize free radicals, reduce oxidative stress, linked to prevention of chronic diseases (cancer, cardiovascular disease, neurodegeneration).
-   **Anti-inflammatory Effects:** Inhibit inflammatory pathways (e.g., curcumin, quercetin).
-   **Antimicrobial Properties:** Contribute to food preservation and potential treatment of infections.
-   **Cardiovascular Health:** Improve endothelial function, reduce blood pressure, improve lipid profiles (e.g., flavonoids, resveratrol).
-   **Anticancer Potential:** Various mechanisms (antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antiproliferative, apoptosis induction).
-   **Neuroprotection:** Potential benefits in neurodegenerative diseases.
