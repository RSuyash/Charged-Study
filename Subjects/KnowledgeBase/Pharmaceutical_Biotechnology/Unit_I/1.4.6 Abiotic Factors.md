# 1.4.6 Abiotic Factors

Abiotic factors encompass the non-living environmental influences that significantly affect plant growth, development, and secondary metabolite production. These factors often act as stressors, triggering adaptive metabolic responses.

1.  **Environmental Stressors:** (Covered in more detail in [[1.4.3 Environmental Factors]])
    *   **Drought Stress:** Water scarcity leads to osmotic stress and oxidative stress, often increasing phenolics and other protective compounds.
    *   **Salinity Stress:** High salt concentration causes osmotic and ionic stress, inducing compatible solutes and altering secondary metabolism.
    *   **Temperature Stress:** Both heat and cold stress can induce specific metabolic responses, including the production of heat shock proteins or cryoprotectants, and alter secondary metabolite profiles.
    *   **Light Stress:** High light intensity or UV radiation can cause photo-oxidative stress, leading to increased production of antioxidant pigments (e.g., carotenoids, anthocyanins) and UV-screening compounds (e.g., flavonoids).
    *   **Heavy Metal Stress:** Exposure to heavy metals can induce oxidative stress and specific detoxification pathways involving secondary metabolites.

2.  **Nutritional Factors:** (Covered in more detail in [[1.4.2 Growth Factors and Nutrient Requirements]])
    *   **Nutrient Availability:** The supply of essential macro- and micronutrients directly impacts primary metabolism (growth) and the resources available for secondary metabolism.
    *   **Nutrient Deficiency/Excess:** Imbalances, such as nitrogen or phosphate limitation, can shift the balance from growth towards defense and secondary metabolite production. Conversely, excessive nutrients might suppress secondary metabolism by prioritizing growth. The carbon-to-nutrient ratio (e.g., C/N ratio) is often a key determinant.

Understanding how plants perceive and respond to these abiotic factors is crucial for predicting metabolite variations in nature and for manipulating production in agricultural or biotechnological settings.
