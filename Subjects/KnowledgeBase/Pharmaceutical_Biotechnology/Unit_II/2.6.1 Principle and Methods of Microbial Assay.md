# 2.6.1 Principle and Methods of Microbial Assay

**Principle:**
Microbial assays (or Antimicrobial Susceptibility Tests - AST) evaluate the ability of an antimicrobial agent to inhibit or kill a specific microorganism *in vitro*. The core principle involves exposing a standardized population of microorganisms to varying concentrations or applications of the antimicrobial agent under controlled conditions and observing the effect on microbial growth or viability.

**Common Methods:**

1.  **Broth Dilution Method:**
    *   **Principle:** Determines the [[2.6.2 Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC)|Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC)]] by exposing a standardized inoculum to serial dilutions of the antimicrobial in liquid broth medium.
    *   **Procedure:** Prepare two-fold serial dilutions of the drug in broth (e.g., Mueller-Hinton Broth). Inoculate with standardized bacterial suspension (~5x10⁵ CFU/mL). Incubate (35-37°C, 18-24h). Observe turbidity (growth). MIC = lowest concentration with no visible growth. Can be macro- or microdilution (96-well plates).
    *   **Advantages:** Quantitative MIC value, can determine Minimum Bactericidal Concentration (MBC) by subculturing clear wells, suitable for automation. Considered a reference method.
    - **Limitations:** Requires careful dilution preparation, may not suit all organisms, relatively labor-intensive (macro).

    ![Broth Dilution Illustration](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit II 1a63a995df87804baa28f5eb2cc772fe/image%2032.png>)
    *Fig: Broth dilution method: (a) macrodilution, (b) microdilution.*

2.  **Agar Dilution Method:**
    *   **Principle:** Similar to broth dilution, but antimicrobial is incorporated into solid agar medium at various concentrations.
    *   **Procedure:** Prepare agar plates (e.g., Mueller-Hinton Agar) containing serial dilutions of the drug. Spot-inoculate standardized bacterial suspensions onto the plates. Incubate. MIC = lowest concentration plate with no visible growth.
    *   **Advantages:** Allows simultaneous testing of multiple strains on one plate, clear visual endpoint (growth/no growth).
    - **Limitations:** More labor-intensive plate preparation than broth dilution.

    ![Agar Dilution Illustration](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit II 1a63a995df87804baa28f5eb2cc772fe/image%2033.png>)
    *Fig: Agar dilution method.*

3.  **Disk Diffusion Method (Kirby-Bauer Test):**
    *   **Principle:** Qualitative test. Paper disks impregnated with a standard amount of antibiotic are placed on an agar plate uniformly inoculated with bacteria. Drug diffuses into agar, creating a concentration gradient. Zone of inhibition around the disk indicates susceptibility.
    *   **Procedure:** Inoculate agar plate evenly (bacterial lawn). Place antibiotic disks. Incubate. Measure diameter of inhibition zones. Compare zone sizes to standard charts (e.g., CLSI, EUCAST) to categorize as Susceptible (S), Intermediate (I), or Resistant (R).
    *   **Advantages:** Simple, cost-effective, rapid qualitative results, widely used in clinical labs.
    - **Limitations:** Qualitative (S/I/R, not MIC), results affected by diffusion rate, agar depth, inoculum density.

    ![Kirby-Bauer Disk Diffusion](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit II 1a63a995df87804baa28f5eb2cc772fe/image%2034.png>)
    *Fig: Kirby-Bauer Disk Diffusion Method.*

4.  **Time-Kill Kinetics Assay:**
    *   **Principle:** Measures the rate and extent of bacterial killing by an antimicrobial agent over time.
    *   **Procedure:** Expose standardized inoculum to a specific drug concentration in broth. Sample at various time points (0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 24h). Perform serial dilutions and plate counts (CFU/mL) for each sample. Plot log CFU/mL vs. time.
    *   **Advantages:** Provides dynamic information on killing rate, clearly distinguishes bactericidal (>3-log reduction in CFU/mL) vs. bacteriostatic activity. Useful for research and PK/PD studies.
    - **Limitations:** Labor-intensive, requires multiple time points and plate counts, complex data analysis.

    ![Time-Kill Kinetics Example](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit II 1a63a995df87804baa28f5eb2cc772fe/image%2035.png>)
    *Fig: Example time-kill kinetics assay plot.*

5.  **Bioautography:**
    *   **Principle:** Combines chromatography (TLC, HPLC) with a microbial assay to identify antimicrobial compounds within a complex mixture (e.g., natural product extract).
    *   **Procedure:** Separate mixture components via chromatography. Overlay the chromatogram with agar seeded with a test microorganism (or apply microbial suspension). Incubate. Zones of inhibition on the chromatogram indicate the location of active compounds.
    *   **Advantages:** Useful for screening natural products, identifies active components in mixtures.
    - **Limitations:** Requires chromatography equipment, interpretation can be complex, primarily qualitative/semi-quantitative.

The choice of method depends on the specific question being asked (MIC vs. killing rate vs. screening), the resources available, and the nature of the antimicrobial agent and microorganism.
