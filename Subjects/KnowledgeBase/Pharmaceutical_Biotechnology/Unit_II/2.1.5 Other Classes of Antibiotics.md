# 2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics

Beyond the major classes like β-lactams, aminoglycosides, tetracyclines, and macrolides, several other important antibiotic classes are used clinically.

**2.1.5.1 Glycopeptides**
-   **Structure:** Large, complex glycosylated cyclic or polycyclic peptides.
-   **Example:** Vancomycin, Teicoplanin, Dalbavancin, Oritavancin.
-   **Mechanism:** Inhibit cell wall synthesis by binding to the D-alanyl-D-alanine terminus of peptidoglycan precursors, blocking both transglycosylation and transpeptidation. (See [[2.3.1 Interference with Cell Wall Synthesis]])
-   **Spectrum:** Primarily active against Gram-positive bacteria, including Methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA) and *Enterococcus* species (though resistance, VRE, exists). Not active against Gram-negatives (too large to penetrate outer membrane).
-   **Uses:** Serious Gram-positive infections (MRSA bacteremia, endocarditis, pneumonia), *Clostridioides difficile* infection (oral vancomycin).
-   **Side Effects:** Nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity (less common than aminoglycosides), "Red Man Syndrome" (infusion-related histamine release with vancomycin).

**2.1.5.2 Sulfonamides & Trimethoprim**
-   **Structure:** Sulfonamides are synthetic antimicrobials, structural analogs of para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA). Trimethoprim is a synthetic dihydrofolate reductase inhibitor. Often used in combination (co-trimoxazole = Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole, TMP-SMX).
-   **Mechanism:** Antimetabolites that sequentially inhibit folic acid synthesis in bacteria. Sulfonamides inhibit dihydropteroate synthase; Trimethoprim inhibits dihydrofolate reductase. Folic acid is essential for nucleotide synthesis. (See [[2.3.3 Interference with Nucleic Acid Synthesis]])
-   **Spectrum:** Broad spectrum (Gram-positives, Gram-negatives), but resistance is widespread. Active against *Staphylococcus aureus* (including some MRSA), *H. influenzae*, Enterobacteriaceae, *Pneumocystis jirovecii*, *Toxoplasma gondii*.
-   **Uses:** Urinary tract infections (UTIs), *Pneumocystis* pneumonia (PJP) treatment/prophylaxis, toxoplasmosis, nocardiosis, community-acquired MRSA skin infections.
-   **Side Effects:** Hypersensitivity reactions (rashes, Stevens-Johnson syndrome), photosensitivity, crystalluria (requires adequate hydration), hematological effects (anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia - especially with G6PD deficiency), hyperkalemia (trimethoprim).

**2.1.5.3 Quinolones / Fluoroquinolones**
-   **Structure:** Synthetic antimicrobials based on a quinolone nucleus. Fluoroquinolones have a fluorine atom, enhancing potency and spectrum.
-   **Examples:** Nalidixic acid (first-gen quinolone), Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin (fluoroquinolones).
-   **Mechanism:** Inhibit bacterial DNA gyrase (Topoisomerase II) and Topoisomerase IV, enzymes essential for DNA replication, transcription, repair, and recombination. Leads to DNA strand breaks and cell death. (See [[2.3.3 Interference with Nucleic Acid Synthesis]])
-   **Spectrum:** Broad spectrum, excellent Gram-negative activity (including *Pseudomonas* - ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin), variable Gram-positive activity (newer generations like levofloxacin, moxifloxacin have better *Streptococcus pneumoniae* coverage - "respiratory fluoroquinolones"), activity against atypical pathogens.
-   **Uses:** UTIs, respiratory infections (pneumonia), gastrointestinal infections (traveler's diarrhea), bone/joint infections, skin infections, anthrax.
-   **Side Effects:** GI upset, CNS effects (dizziness, headache, confusion, seizures - rare), photosensitivity, QT prolongation, **tendinitis and tendon rupture** (Black Box Warning), peripheral neuropathy, aortic dissection/rupture risk, dysglycemia. Use is increasingly restricted due to side effects.

**2.1.5.4 Lincosamides**
-   **Structure:** Contain an amino acid linked to an amino sugar.
-   **Example:** Clindamycin (derived from lincomycin).
-   **Mechanism:** Inhibit protein synthesis by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit, overlapping with the macrolide binding site. Inhibits peptide bond formation/translocation. Primarily bacteriostatic. (See [[2.3.2 Inhibition of Protein Synthesis]])
-   **Spectrum:** Gram-positive cocci (*Staphylococcus* including MRSA, *Streptococcus*), excellent activity against anaerobic bacteria (both Gram-positive and Gram-negative, e.g., *Bacteroides fragilis*).
-   **Uses:** Skin and soft tissue infections (especially involving MRSA or anaerobes), intra-abdominal infections, pelvic infections, acne vulgaris (topical), dental infections, alternative for penicillin-allergic patients.
-   **Side Effects:** Diarrhea, **risk of *Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI)** leading to pseudomembranous colitis (Black Box Warning), rash, potential hepatotoxicity.

**2.1.5.5 Oxazolidinones**
-   **Structure:** Synthetic class with a unique oxazolidinone ring structure.
-   **Example:** Linezolid, Tedizolid.
-   **Mechanism:** Inhibit protein synthesis at a very early stage by binding to the 50S ribosomal subunit (near the interface with the 30S subunit) and preventing the formation of the functional 70S initiation complex. Unique mechanism, less cross-resistance with other protein synthesis inhibitors. Primarily bacteriostatic. (See [[2.3.2 Inhibition of Protein Synthesis]])
-   **Spectrum:** Primarily active against Gram-positive bacteria, including resistant strains like MRSA, Vancomycin-resistant *Enterococcus* (VRE), and penicillin-resistant *Streptococcus pneumoniae*.
-   **Uses:** Infections caused by resistant Gram-positive organisms, such as complicated skin infections, pneumonia (including HAP/VAP), VRE infections. Available IV and orally with high bioavailability.
-   **Side Effects:** Myelosuppression (thrombocytopenia, anemia, neutropenia - especially with prolonged use > 2 weeks, requires monitoring), peripheral and optic neuropathy (with prolonged use), potential for serotonin syndrome if co-administered with serotonergic drugs (MAOI activity).
