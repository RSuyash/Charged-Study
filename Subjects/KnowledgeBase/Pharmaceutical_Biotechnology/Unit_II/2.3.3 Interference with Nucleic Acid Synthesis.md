# 2.3.3 Interference with Nucleic Acid Synthesis

**Overview:**
Nucleic acids (DNA and RNA) are fundamental molecules carrying genetic information and directing protein synthesis. Antimicrobial agents targeting nucleic acid synthesis interfere with DNA replication, RNA transcription, or the synthesis of nucleotide precursors, ultimately inhibiting microbial growth or causing cell death.

![DNA Synthesis Inhibition](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit II 1a63a995df87804baa28f5eb2cc772fe/image%2026.png>)
*Fig: Antibiotics interfere with DNA synthesis and replication.*

**Mechanisms of Action:**

1.  **Inhibition of DNA Replication/Topology (DNA Gyrase/Topoisomerase IV Inhibitors):**
    -   *Drug Class:* [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Quinolones / Fluoroquinolones]].
    -   *Examples:* Nalidixic acid, Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin, Moxifloxacin.
    -   *Target Enzymes:* Bacterial DNA gyrase (Topoisomerase II) and Topoisomerase IV. These enzymes are essential for managing DNA supercoiling, untangling replicated chromosomes, and repairing DNA.
    -   *Mechanism:* Fluoroquinolones bind to the enzyme-DNA complex after the enzyme has introduced double-strand breaks. They stabilize this complex (cleavable complex) and prevent the re-ligation of the DNA strands. This leads to an accumulation of lethal double-strand breaks during replication and transcription.
    -   *Effect:* Bactericidal.

    ![Fluoroquinolone Structure](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit II 1a63a995df87804baa28f5eb2cc772fe/image%2027.png>)
    *Fig: Ciprofloxacin, a fluoroquinolone.*

2.  **Inhibition of RNA Synthesis (RNA Polymerase Inhibitors):**
    -   *Drug Class:* Rifamycins.
    -   *Example:* Rifampicin (Rifampin).
    -   *Target Enzyme:* Bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (specifically the β-subunit).
    -   *Mechanism:* Rifampicin binds to the β-subunit of RNA polymerase, blocking the initiation of transcription (prevents formation of the first phosphodiester bonds) but does not inhibit elongation of already initiated RNA chains.
    -   *Effect:* Bactericidal.

3.  **Inhibition of Nucleotide Precursor Synthesis (Antimetabolites):**
    -   *Drug Classes:* [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Sulfonamides]] and Trimethoprim. Often used in combination (Co-trimoxazole).
    -   *Target Pathway:* Folic acid (tetrahydrofolate) biosynthesis pathway. Tetrahydrofolate is an essential coenzyme for the synthesis of purines and pyrimidines (DNA/RNA precursors) and certain amino acids. Bacteria must synthesize folate *de novo*, whereas humans obtain it from the diet.
    -   *Mechanism:*
        -   **Sulfonamides:** Structural analogs of para-aminobenzoic acid (PABA). They competitively inhibit **dihydropteroate synthase**, the enzyme that incorporates PABA into dihydropteroic acid (an early step in folate synthesis).
        -   **Trimethoprim:** Structural analog of dihydrofolate. It competitively inhibits **dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR)**, the enzyme that converts dihydrofolate to the active tetrahydrofolate form (a later step).
        -   **Synergy:** Inhibiting two sequential steps in the pathway leads to a synergistic and potent blockade of folate synthesis.
    -   *Effect:* Bacteriostatic individually, often bactericidal in combination.

4.  **DNA Damage:**
    -   *Drug Class:* Nitroimidazoles.
    -   *Example:* Metronidazole.
    -   *Target:* Primarily anaerobic bacteria and some protozoa.
    -   *Mechanism:* Metronidazole is a prodrug that requires reductive activation under anaerobic conditions. The reduced form causes DNA strand breaks and inhibits DNA synthesis.
    -   *Effect:* Bactericidal.

These agents exploit differences between microbial and host nucleic acid synthesis machinery or metabolic pathways for selective toxicity.
