# 2.3.2 Inhibition of Protein Synthesis

**Overview:**
Protein synthesis (translation) is an essential process where genetic information encoded in mRNA is translated into proteins by ribosomes. Bacterial ribosomes (70S, composed of 30S and 50S subunits) differ structurally from eukaryotic ribosomes (80S), making them excellent targets for selective antimicrobial action. Several classes of antibiotics inhibit bacterial protein synthesis at different stages.

![Protein Synthesis Inhibitors](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit II 1a63a995df87804baa28f5eb2cc772fe/image%2025.png>)
*Fig: Medications that inhibit protein synthesis, targeting either the 30S or 50S ribosomal subunit.*

**Mechanisms of Action:**

1.  **Aminoglycosides ([[2.1.2 Aminoglycosides|Aminoglycosides]]):**
    -   *Examples:* Gentamicin, Streptomycin, Amikacin, Tobramycin.
    -   *Target:* **30S ribosomal subunit** (specifically 16S rRNA).
    -   *Mechanism:* Bind irreversibly to the A-site on the 30S subunit. This causes:
        -   **Misreading of mRNA:** Incorporation of incorrect amino acids.
        -   **Premature Termination:** Release of incomplete proteins.
        -   **Inhibition of Translocation:** Blocking ribosome movement.
    -   *Effect:* Bactericidal (due to accumulation of non-functional proteins and potential membrane damage).

2.  **Tetracyclines ([[2.1.3 Tetracyclines|Tetracyclines]]):**
    -   *Examples:* Tetracycline, Doxycycline, Minocycline, Tigecycline.
    -   *Target:* **30S ribosomal subunit**.
    -   *Mechanism:* Bind reversibly to the 30S subunit (near the A-site).
        -   **Block Aminoacyl-tRNA Binding:** Sterically hinder the attachment of charged tRNA molecules to the A-site.
    -   *Effect:* Bacteriostatic (inhibit further protein elongation).

3.  **Macrolides ([[2.1.4 Macrolides|Macrolides]]):**
    -   *Examples:* Erythromycin, Azithromycin, Clarithromycin.
    -   *Target:* **50S ribosomal subunit** (specifically 23S rRNA near the peptidyl transferase center and exit tunnel).
    -   *Mechanism:* Bind to the 50S subunit and block the polypeptide exit tunnel.
        -   **Inhibit Translocation:** Prevent the movement of peptidyl-tRNA from the A-site to the P-site.
        -   May also interfere with peptide bond formation.
    -   *Effect:* Primarily Bacteriostatic.

4.  **Lincosamides ([[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Lincosamides]]):**
    -   *Example:* Clindamycin.
    -   *Target:* **50S ribosomal subunit** (binding site overlaps with macrolides).
    -   *Mechanism:* Similar to macrolides, bind to 23S rRNA and inhibit peptide bond formation and/or translocation.
    -   *Effect:* Primarily Bacteriostatic. (Often exhibit cross-resistance with macrolides via MLS<sub>B</sub> mechanism).

5.  **Chloramphenicol:**
    -   *Target:* **50S ribosomal subunit**.
    -   *Mechanism:* Binds to 23S rRNA and inhibits the **peptidyl transferase** activity, preventing peptide bond formation.
    -   *Effect:* Primarily Bacteriostatic. (Use limited due to potential for serious toxicity like aplastic anemia).

6.  **Oxazolidinones ([[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Oxazolidinones]]):**
    -   *Examples:* Linezolid, Tedizolid.
    -   *Target:* **50S ribosomal subunit** (unique binding site on 23S rRNA near the interface with 30S).
    -   *Mechanism:* Bind to the P-site on the 50S subunit and interfere with the formation of the **initiation complex** (binding of the first aminoacyl-tRNA, fMet-tRNA, and interaction with the 30S subunit). Blocks the very start of protein synthesis.
    -   *Effect:* Primarily Bacteriostatic. (Unique mechanism means less cross-resistance).

7.  **Streptogramins:**
    -   *Example:* Quinupristin/Dalfopristin (Synercid).
    -   *Target:* **50S ribosomal subunit** (bind at two different sites).
    -   *Mechanism:* Act synergistically. Dalfopristin (Streptogramin A) induces a conformational change enhancing Quinupristin (Streptogramin B) binding. Together they inhibit peptide elongation and cause release of incomplete peptides.
    -   *Effect:* Individually bacteriostatic, but bactericidal in combination.

These antibiotics exploit differences between bacterial and eukaryotic ribosomes to selectively inhibit bacterial protein synthesis.
