# 2.2.1 Spectrum of Activity

The **spectrum of activity** defines the range of microbial species that an antimicrobial drug is effective against. This is a key characteristic guiding the selection of appropriate therapy.

**Categories:**

1.  **Narrow-Spectrum Antibiotics:**
    *   **Definition:** Effective against a limited range or specific types of bacteria (e.g., only Gram-positives, or only specific species).
    *   **Mechanism:** Often target structures or pathways unique to that limited group.
    *   **Examples:**
        -   [[2.1.1.1 Penicillins|Penicillin G]]: Primarily Gram-positive cocci, some Gram-negative cocci.
        -   [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Vancomycin]]: Primarily Gram-positive organisms, including MRSA.
        -   Isoniazid: Primarily *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*.
        -   [[2.1.1.4 Monobactams|Aztreonam]]: Primarily aerobic Gram-negative bacteria.
    *   **Clinical Use:** Preferred when the causative pathogen is identified (targeted therapy). Minimizes disruption of normal microbiota (dysbiosis) and reduces selection pressure for resistance among bystander bacteria.

2.  **Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics:**
    *   **Definition:** Effective against a wide variety of bacteria, including both Gram-positive and Gram-negative species.
    *   **Mechanism:** Often target conserved processes common to many bacteria (e.g., ribosome function, general cell wall synthesis components).
    *   **Examples:**
        -   [[2.1.1.1 Penicillins|Amoxicillin]], [[2.1.1.1 Penicillins|Ampicillin]].
        -   [[2.1.3 Tetracyclines|Tetracyclines]] (Doxycycline).
        -   [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Fluoroquinolones]] (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin).
        -   [[2.1.1.3 Carbapenems|Carbapenems]] (Imipenem, Meropenem - very broad).
    *   **Clinical Use:** Useful for empirical therapy (when the pathogen is unknown but suspected to be within the covered range), polymicrobial infections (involving multiple types of bacteria), or when susceptibility testing is unavailable. However, their use increases the risk of disrupting normal flora and promoting resistance.

3.  **Extended-Spectrum Antibiotics:**
    *   **Definition:** Often semi-synthetic modifications of existing antibiotics, developed to have activity against a wider range of organisms than the original drug, particularly against bacteria that have developed resistance.
    *   **Mechanism:** Designed to overcome specific resistance mechanisms (e.g., stability against certain β-lactamases) or have enhanced penetration/target binding.
    *   **Examples:**
        -   Third-generation [[2.1.1.2 Cephalosporins|Cephalosporins]] (e.g., Ceftazidime) have extended Gram-negative coverage compared to first-generation.
        -   [[2.1.1.1 Penicillins|Piperacillin]]/tazobactam: Combines an extended-spectrum penicillin with a β-lactamase inhibitor.

Choosing the appropriate spectrum is crucial for effective treatment while minimizing collateral damage to the host microbiome and the emergence of resistance.
