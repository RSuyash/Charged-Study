# 2.2.3 Toxicity and Side Effects

While essential for treating infections, antimicrobial drugs can cause adverse effects, ranging from mild side effects to severe toxicity. Understanding these potential harms is crucial for safe prescribing and patient monitoring.

**Types of Toxicity:**

-   **Direct Organ Toxicity:** Some drugs can directly damage specific host organs or tissues, often due to accumulation or specific biochemical interactions.
    -   *Nephrotoxicity (Kidney Damage):* [[2.1.2 Aminoglycosides|Aminoglycosides]] (gentamicin, tobramycin), [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Vancomycin]], Amphotericin B (antifungal). Mechanism often involves accumulation in renal tubular cells. Requires dose adjustments and monitoring of renal function.
    -   *Hepatotoxicity (Liver Damage):* Certain macrolides ([[2.1.4 Macrolides|Erythromycin estolate]]), isoniazid (anti-TB), some antifungals (azoles). Can range from mild enzyme elevation to severe liver failure.
    -   *Ototoxicity (Ear Damage):* [[2.1.2 Aminoglycosides|Aminoglycosides]], [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Vancomycin]]. Can affect hearing (cochlear toxicity) and/or balance (vestibular toxicity). Often irreversible.
    -   *Bone Marrow Suppression:* Chloramphenicol (aplastic anemia - rare but fatal), [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Linezolid]] (thrombocytopenia, anemia - usually reversible), [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Sulfonamides]]. Requires blood count monitoring.
    -   *Neurotoxicity:* [[2.1.2 Aminoglycosides|Aminoglycosides]] (neuromuscular blockade - rare), [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Fluoroquinolones]] (CNS effects like dizziness, confusion, seizures), [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Linezolid]] (peripheral/optic neuropathy with prolonged use), Penicillins (seizures at very high doses, especially with renal failure).
    -   *Cartilage/Tendon Damage:* [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Fluoroquinolones]] (tendinitis, tendon rupture - Black Box Warning). Avoid in children/pregnancy generally.
    -   *Effects on Developing Tissues:* [[2.1.3 Tetracyclines|Tetracyclines]] (discoloration of teeth, bone growth inhibition). Contraindicated in pregnancy and young children (<8 years).

-   **Allergic / Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Immune-mediated reactions, unpredictable and not dose-related (usually).
    -   *Common Culprits:* [[2.1.1.1 Penicillins|Penicillins]] and other [[2.1.1 β-Lactam Antibiotics|β-lactams]], [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Sulfonamides]].
    -   *Manifestations:* Range from mild skin rash (maculopapular eruption), urticaria (hives), fever, to severe reactions like Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS), Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN), anaphylaxis (life-threatening).
    -   *Cross-Reactivity:* Possible between structurally similar drugs (e.g., penicillins and some cephalosporins).

-   **Infusion-Related Reactions:**
    -   *"Red Man Syndrome":* Histamine release caused by rapid infusion of [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Vancomycin]], leading to flushing, itching, rash on face/neck/upper torso. Prevented by slower infusion rate and antihistamines.

**Common Side Effects (Often Dose-Related or Due to Mechanism):**

-   **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** Very common with many oral antibiotics.
    -   *Mechanism:* Direct irritation of GI mucosa, disruption of normal gut microbiota (dysbiosis).
    -   *Symptoms:* Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain.
    -   ***Clostridioides difficile* Infection (CDI):** Overgrowth of *C. difficile* due to disruption of normal flora, leading to pseudomembranous colitis. Higher risk with broad-spectrum agents like [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Clindamycin]], [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Fluoroquinolones]], some [[2.1.1.2 Cephalosporins|Cephalosporins]].

-   **Photosensitivity:** Increased sensitivity to sunlight, leading to exaggerated sunburn reactions. Common with [[2.1.3 Tetracyclines|Tetracyclines]] (especially doxycycline) and [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Fluoroquinolones]]. Patients should use sun protection.

-   **QT Prolongation:** Some antibiotics can prolong the QT interval on the electrocardiogram (ECG), increasing the risk of a potentially fatal ventricular arrhythmia called Torsades de Pointes.
    -   *Examples:* [[2.1.4 Macrolides|Macrolides]] (especially erythromycin), [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Fluoroquinolones]]. Caution needed with other QT-prolonging drugs or in patients with risk factors.

-   **Drug Interactions:** Many antibiotics interact with other medications via metabolic pathways (e.g., CYP450 inhibition/induction - see [[2.2.2 Pharmacokinetic Properties]]) or other mechanisms (e.g., chelation - tetracyclines/fluoroquinolones with divalent cations).

Balancing the therapeutic benefits against potential toxicity and side effects is a key consideration in antimicrobial therapy.
