# 2.5.4 Agriculture and Horticulture Applications

Antibiotics have specific applications in agriculture and horticulture, primarily for controlling bacterial diseases that affect crops, particularly high-value fruit trees and plants.

**Applications:**

1.  **Control of Plant Bacterial Diseases:**
    -   Certain antibiotics are used as bactericides to manage specific plant diseases where other control methods may be less effective.
    -   **Fire Blight:** Caused by the bacterium *Erwinia amylovora*, affecting apples, pears, and related species. [[2.1.2 Aminoglycosides|Streptomycin]] and [[2.1.3 Tetracyclines|Oxytetracycline]] have been used (often via spraying during bloom) to control this destructive disease. However, resistance development in *E. amylovora* and environmental concerns limit their use.
    -   **Citrus Greening (Huanglongbing - HLB):** A devastating citrus disease caused by *Candidatus Liberibacter* species. Research is ongoing, and regulatory approvals have been sought or granted in some regions for limited use of antibiotics like streptomycin and oxytetracycline via trunk injection to manage HLB, though efficacy and long-term impacts are debated.
    -   **Other Bacterial Diseases:** Limited use for other specific bacterial spots, blights, or wilts in certain crops.

2.  **Plant Tissue Culture:**
    -   Antibiotics are sometimes added to plant tissue culture media to prevent or eliminate bacterial contamination, which can be a significant problem in sterile culture environments. However, antibiotics can also be phytotoxic or interfere with plant regeneration, so their use requires careful selection and concentration optimization.

**Considerations and Concerns:**
-   **Antibiotic Resistance:** Widespread use of antibiotics in agriculture raises significant concerns about the development and spread of [[2.4 Microbial Resistance to Antibiotics and Antimicrobial Agents|antibiotic resistance]] in environmental bacteria, potentially transferring to human or animal pathogens.
-   **Environmental Impact:** Concerns exist about the persistence of antibiotics in soil and water and their impact on non-target organisms and ecosystem functions.
-   **Regulatory Restrictions:** The use of medically important antibiotics in agriculture is increasingly restricted or banned in many countries due to resistance concerns. Regulatory agencies carefully evaluate the risks and benefits.
-   **Alternatives:** Emphasis is placed on integrated pest management (IPM) strategies, including resistant cultivars, biological control agents, cultural practices, and copper-based compounds, as alternatives to antibiotic use in agriculture.

The application of antibiotics in agriculture is limited and under scrutiny due to the significant public health implications of antimicrobial resistance.
