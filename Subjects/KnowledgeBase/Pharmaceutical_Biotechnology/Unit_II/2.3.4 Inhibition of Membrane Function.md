# 2.3.4 Inhibition of Membrane Function

**Overview:**
The bacterial cell membrane (cytoplasmic membrane and, in Gram-negatives, the outer membrane) is vital for maintaining cellular integrity, controlling the passage of substances, and housing key metabolic processes like electron transport and ATP synthesis. Antibiotics targeting membrane function disrupt these processes, often leading to rapid bacterial death.

*Fig: Differences in cell envelope structure between Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria.*

**Mechanisms of Action:**

1.  **Polymyxins:**
    -   *Examples:* Polymyxin B, Colistin (Polymyxin E).
    -   *Structure:* Cyclic lipopeptides (contain fatty acid tail and peptide ring). Cationic at physiological pH.
    -   *Target:* Primarily the **outer membrane** of Gram-negative bacteria.
    -   *Mechanism:*
        1.  **Bind to Lipopolysaccharide (LPS):** The cationic peptide ring interacts electrostatically with the negatively charged lipid A component of LPS in the outer membrane.
        2.  **Displace Divalent Cations:** This interaction displaces essential divalent cations (Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺) that normally stabilize the LPS layer.
        3.  **Outer Membrane Disruption:** Destabilization of the outer membrane increases its permeability.
        4.  **Cytoplasmic Membrane Interaction:** The hydrophobic fatty acid tail may then insert into the cytoplasmic membrane, disrupting its integrity like a detergent.
        5.  **Leakage & Cell Death:** Leads to leakage of essential intracellular contents (ions, metabolites) and loss of membrane potential, resulting in rapid cell death (bactericidal).
    -   *Spectrum:* Narrow, primarily active against multi-drug resistant Gram-negative bacteria (e.g., *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Acinetobacter baumannii*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*). Not active against Gram-positives (lack outer membrane/LPS) or certain Gram-negatives (*Proteus*, *Serratia*).
    -   *Toxicity:* Use limited by potential nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity. Colistin often used as a last resort.

2.  **Daptomycin:**
    -   *Structure:* Cyclic lipopeptide.
    -   *Target:* Cytoplasmic membrane of Gram-positive bacteria.
    -   *Mechanism:*
        1.  **Calcium-Dependent Binding/Insertion:** Daptomycin requires physiological concentrations of calcium ions (Ca²⁺) to bind to and insert its lipid tail into the bacterial cytoplasmic membrane.
        2.  **Oligomerization & Channel Formation (Proposed):** Upon insertion, daptomycin molecules may oligomerize, potentially forming ion channels or disrupting membrane structure.
        3.  **Rapid Membrane Depolarization:** Causes rapid efflux of potassium ions (K⁺) and depolarization of the membrane potential.
        4.  **Inhibition of Macromolecular Synthesis & Cell Death:** Loss of membrane potential disrupts ATP synthesis and inhibits DNA, RNA, and protein synthesis, leading to rapid cell death (bactericidal) without causing significant cell lysis.
    -   *Spectrum:* Narrow, active only against Gram-positive bacteria (including MRSA and VRE).
    -   *Toxicity:* Can cause muscle toxicity (myopathy, rhabdomyolysis - requires monitoring of CPK levels). Inactivated by pulmonary surfactant, so not used for pneumonia.

These membrane-active agents often exhibit rapid bactericidal activity but can also have significant host toxicity due to the similarity between bacterial and eukaryotic membranes (though polymyxins show some selectivity for LPS).
