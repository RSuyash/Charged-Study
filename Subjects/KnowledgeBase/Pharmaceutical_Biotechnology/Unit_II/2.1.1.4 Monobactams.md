# 2.1.1.4 Monobactams

Monobactams represent a unique class within the [[2.1.1 β-Lactam Antibiotics|β-lactam antibiotic]] family, distinguished by their core chemical structure.

**Chemical Structure:**
Unlike other β-lactams (penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems) which have a second ring fused to the β-lactam ring, monobactams possess only a **monocyclic β-lactam ring**. This structural difference significantly influences their spectrum of activity and stability against certain β-lactamases.

![Aztreonam Structure](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit II 1a63a995df87804baa28f5eb2cc772fe/image%207.png>)
*Fig: Aztreonam. The four-membered ring at the bottom is the β-lactam. There is a second thiazole ring, but it is not fused to the β-lactam ring.*

**Example:**
-   **Aztreonam:** The only monobactam currently available for clinical use.

**Mechanism of Action:**
Like other β-lactams, aztreonam inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding specifically to Penicillin-Binding Protein 3 (PBP3) of aerobic Gram-negative bacteria. Binding to PBP3 inhibits the transpeptidation step of peptidoglycan synthesis, leading to the formation of long, filamentous bacterial cells that eventually lyse. (See [[2.3.1 Interference with Cell Wall Synthesis]])

**Spectrum of Activity:**
Monobactams have a **narrow spectrum** of activity, primarily targeting **aerobic Gram-negative bacteria**.
-   **Active against:** Enterobacteriaceae (*E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, *Proteus*, *Serratia*, etc.), *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Neisseria* spp.
-   **NOT active against:** Gram-positive bacteria (like *Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus*, *Enterococcus*) or anaerobic bacteria. This is because it has low affinity for the PBPs of these organisms.

**Clinical Uses:**
-   Used for infections caused by susceptible aerobic Gram-negative bacteria, particularly in patients with severe allergies (anaphylaxis) to penicillins and/or cephalosporins. The lack of the fused ring structure reduces cross-reactivity.
-   Treats infections like pneumonia, meningitis, sepsis, urinary tract infections, skin and soft tissue infections, intra-abdominal infections (usually in combination with agents covering Gram-positives/anaerobes).
-   Can be administered intravenously or intramuscularly; also available as an inhaled formulation for cystic fibrosis patients with *P. aeruginosa* lung infections.

**Resistance Mechanisms:**
-   Stable against hydrolysis by many common β-lactamases (including penicillinases and some cephalosporinases).
-   Can be hydrolyzed by some Extended-Spectrum β-Lactamases (ESBLs) and carbapenemases.
-   Mutations in PBP3 can reduce binding affinity.
-   Efflux pumps can contribute to resistance.

**Production:**
Aztreonam was originally isolated from the bacterium *Chromobacterium violaceum*, but the clinically used drug is produced synthetically.
