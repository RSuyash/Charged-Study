# 2.1.1 β-Lactam Antibiotics

**Overview:**
β-Lactam antibiotics are a major class characterized by the presence of a **β-lactam ring** in their molecular structure. They are primarily bactericidal, working by inhibiting bacterial cell wall synthesis, specifically by interfering with the cross-linking of peptidoglycan layers. This inhibition leads to a weakened cell wall and eventual cell lysis.

**Mechanism:** See [[2.3.1 Interference with Cell Wall Synthesis]]

**Sub-classes:**
This broad class includes several important sub-groups:
-   [[2.1.1.1 Penicillins]]
-   [[2.1.1.2 Cephalosporins]]
-   [[2.1.1.3 Carbapenems]]
-   [[2.1.1.4 Monobactams]]

**Resistance:** A major mechanism of resistance against β-lactams is the production of β-lactamase enzymes by bacteria, which hydrolyze the β-lactam ring.
