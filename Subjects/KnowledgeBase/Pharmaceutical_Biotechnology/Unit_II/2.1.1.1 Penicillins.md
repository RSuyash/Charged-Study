# 2.1.1.1 Penicillins

Penicillins are a cornerstone class of [[2.1.1 β-Lactam Antibiotics|β-lactam antibiotics]], historically significant and widely used.

**Chemical Structure:**
Contain a thiazolidine ring fused to a β-lactam ring. Variations in the side chain (R group) attached to the β-lactam ring determine the specific properties (spectrum, stability) of different penicillins.

**Examples:**
-   **Natural Penicillins:**
    -   *Penicillin G (Benzylpenicillin):* Effective against many Gram-positive cocci (*Streptococcus*, some *Staphylococcus*), Gram-positive rods, some Gram-negative cocci (*Neisseria*), and spirochetes. Acid-labile, usually given parenterally.
    -   *Penicillin V (Phenoxymethylpenicillin):* Similar spectrum to Pen G but more acid-stable, allowing oral administration.
-   **Aminopenicillins (Broad-spectrum):**
    -   *Amoxicillin:* Wider spectrum including some Gram-negatives (*Haemophilus influenzae*, *E. coli*, *Salmonella*). Often combined with β-lactamase inhibitors (e.g., clavulanic acid -> Augmentin). Good oral absorption.
    -   *Ampicillin:* Similar spectrum to amoxicillin.
-   **Penicillinase-Resistant Penicillins:**
    -   *Methicillin, Nafcillin, Oxacillin, Cloxacillin, Dicloxacillin:* Designed to resist hydrolysis by staphylococcal penicillinase. Used primarily for infections caused by penicillinase-producing *Staphylococcus aureus* (but not MRSA).
-   **Extended-Spectrum Penicillins (Antipseudomonal):**
    -   *Piperacillin, Ticarcillin:* Broader spectrum including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and other Gram-negatives. Often combined with β-lactamase inhibitors (e.g., piperacillin/tazobactam -> Zosyn).

**Mechanism of Action:**
Inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to and inactivating Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs), enzymes essential for the final transpeptidation step in peptidoglycan cross-linking. This leads to a weakened cell wall and osmotic lysis of the bacteria. (See [[2.3.1 Interference with Cell Wall Synthesis]])

**Resistance Mechanisms:**
-   **β-Lactamase Production:** Most common mechanism. Enzymes hydrolyze the β-lactam ring. (e.g., penicillinase produced by *S. aureus*).
-   **Altered PBPs:** Mutations reduce binding affinity for penicillins (e.g., PBP2a in MRSA).
-   **Reduced Permeability/Efflux:** Primarily in Gram-negative bacteria, preventing drug access to PBPs.

**Production:**
Produced industrially by large-scale fermentation of high-yielding strains of the fungus *Penicillium chrysogenum*. (See [[Industrial_Biotechnology/Unit_III/3.3.1 Penicillin#3.3.1.2 Fermentation Process for Penicillin Production|Penicillin Fermentation]])
