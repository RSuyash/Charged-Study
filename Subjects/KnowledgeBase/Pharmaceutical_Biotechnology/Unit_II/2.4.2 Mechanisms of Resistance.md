# 2.4.2 Mechanisms of Resistance

Bacteria employ several biochemical and genetic strategies to resist the effects of antimicrobial agents. Understanding these mechanisms is crucial for developing countermeasures and new drugs.

![Antibiotic Resistance Strategies](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit II 1a63a995df87804baa28f5eb2cc772fe/image%2030.png>)
*Fig: Antibiotic resistance strategies in bacteria.*

**1. Enzyme Inactivation / Modification:**
-   **Overview:** Bacteria produce enzymes that chemically modify or destroy the antibiotic molecule, rendering it inactive. This is a very common resistance mechanism.
-   **Mechanisms & Examples:**
    -   **β-Lactamases:** Hydrolyze the β-lactam ring of [[2.1.1 β-Lactam Antibiotics|β-lactams]] (penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems). Numerous types exist, including penicillinases, cephalosporinases (AmpC), Extended-Spectrum β-Lactamases (ESBLs), and carbapenemases (KPC, NDM, etc.). Genes often plasmid-borne.
    -   **Aminoglycoside-Modifying Enzymes (AMEs):** Modify [[2.1.2 Aminoglycosides|aminoglycosides]] via acetylation (AAC), phosphorylation (APH), or adenylation/nucleotidylation (ANT), preventing ribosomal binding. Genes often on mobile elements.
    -   **Chloramphenicol Acetyltransferases (CATs):** Acetylate chloramphenicol, preventing ribosomal binding.
    -   **Esterases:** Can hydrolyze the lactone ring of [[2.1.4 Macrolides|macrolides]] (less common).

**2. Modification of Antibiotic Target Sites:**
-   **Overview:** Alterations in the bacterial target molecule (e.g., enzyme, ribosome) reduce the binding affinity of the antibiotic, making it less effective even if it reaches the target.
-   **Mechanisms & Examples:**
    -   **Altered Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs):** Mutations in chromosomal PBP genes or acquisition of new PBP genes (e.g., *mecA* encoding PBP2a in MRSA) reduce affinity for [[2.1.1 β-Lactam Antibiotics|β-lactams]].
    -   **Ribosomal Alterations:**
        -   *Methylation of rRNA:* Erm methyltransferases methylate specific sites on 23S rRNA, reducing binding of [[2.1.4 Macrolides|macrolides]], [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|lincosamides]], and streptogramin B (MLS<sub>B</sub> resistance). Genes (*erm*) often plasmid-borne.
        -   *Mutations in rRNA genes:* Point mutations in 16S rRNA (for [[2.1.2 Aminoglycosides|aminoglycosides]], [[2.1.3 Tetracyclines|tetracyclines]]) or 23S rRNA (for macrolides, [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|oxazolidinones]]) can decrease drug binding.
        -   *Mutations in ribosomal proteins:* Less common, but can affect drug binding.
    -   **Altered DNA Gyrase / Topoisomerase IV:** Mutations in *gyrA*, *gyrB*, *parC*, or *parE* genes reduce binding affinity of [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|fluoroquinolones]].
    -   **Altered RNA Polymerase:** Mutations in the *rpoB* gene reduce binding affinity of [[2.3.3 Interference with Nucleic Acid Synthesis|rifampicin]].
    -   **Altered D-Ala-D-Ala Target:** Modification of peptidoglycan precursors from D-Ala-D-Ala to D-Ala-D-Lactate or D-Ala-D-Serine prevents binding of [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|vancomycin]] (VRE, VRSA). Mediated by *van* gene clusters.

**3. Reduced Permeability / Increased Efflux:**
-   **Overview:** Bacteria limit the intracellular concentration of the antibiotic, preventing it from reaching its target at effective levels.
-   **Mechanisms & Examples:**
    -   **Reduced Permeability (Primarily Gram-negatives):**
        -   *Porin Channel Modifications:* Mutations leading to loss, decreased expression, or altered structure of outer membrane porin proteins (e.g., OmpF, OmpC in *E. coli*; OprD in *P. aeruginosa*) reduce the influx of hydrophilic antibiotics (like β-lactams, carbapenems, fluoroquinolones).
    -   **Increased Efflux (Active Transport Out):**
        -   *Efflux Pumps:* Membrane-bound transporter proteins actively pump antibiotics out of the cell. Often have broad substrate specificity, contributing significantly to Multidrug Resistance (MDR).
        -   *Major Families:* Resistance-Nodulation-Division (RND) family (e.g., AcrAB-TolC in *E. coli*, MexAB-OprM in *P. aeruginosa*), Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS) (e.g., Tet pumps for tetracycline), ATP-Binding Cassette (ABC) transporters, Small Multidrug Resistance (SMR) family.
        -   *Regulation:* Expression of efflux pumps can be upregulated in response to antibiotic exposure or other stresses.

**4. Target Bypass Mechanisms / Metabolic Bypass:**
-   **Overview:** Bacteria develop or utilize alternative metabolic pathways that circumvent the action of the antibiotic, making the inhibited target non-essential.
-   **Mechanisms & Examples:**
    -   **Acquisition of Resistant Enzymes:** Acquiring plasmid-borne genes encoding drug-resistant versions of essential enzymes (e.g., resistant dihydrofolate reductase or dihydropteroate synthase conferring resistance to [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|trimethoprim/sulfonamides]]).
    -   **Overproduction of Target:** Increasing the amount of the target enzyme can sometimes overcome inhibition if the antibiotic concentration is insufficient to inhibit all enzyme molecules (less common).
    -   **Using Alternative Pathways:** Developing new pathways that achieve the same metabolic outcome without involving the inhibited step (rare).

A single bacterial strain can possess multiple resistance mechanisms, often leading to high-level or multidrug resistance.
