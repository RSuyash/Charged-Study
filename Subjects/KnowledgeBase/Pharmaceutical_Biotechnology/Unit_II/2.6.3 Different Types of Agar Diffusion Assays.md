# 2.6.3 Different Types of Agar Diffusion Assays

Agar diffusion assays are common methods for evaluating antimicrobial activity. They rely on the diffusion of an antimicrobial agent through a solid agar medium inoculated with a test microorganism, creating a concentration gradient. Inhibition of microbial growth around the source of the agent results in a measurable zone of inhibition.

**General Principle:** Antimicrobial agent diffuses from a source (disk, well, plug, spot) into inoculated agar, creating a concentration gradient. If the concentration exceeds the [[2.6.2 Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC)|MIC]] at a certain distance, growth is inhibited, forming a clear zone. Zone size correlates with susceptibility/potency (larger zone = more susceptible/potent), but is also affected by diffusion characteristics.

**Common Types:**

**1. Well Diffusion Method:**
-   **Principle:** Wells are cut into inoculated agar, and liquid antimicrobial samples are placed into the wells. Diffusion occurs radially from the well.
-   **Methodology:**
    1.  Prepare standardized inoculum (0.5 McFarland) and spread-plate onto agar (e.g., MHA).
    2.  Aseptically cut wells (e.g., 6-8 mm diameter) using a sterile cork borer or similar tool.
    3.  Add a defined volume (e.g., 20-100 µL) of the liquid antimicrobial agent/extract into each well.
    4.  Incubate (e.g., 35°C, 18-24h).
    5.  Measure the diameter of the zone of inhibition around each well.
-   **Advantages:** Suitable for testing liquid samples (extracts, solutions) directly, allows testing multiple concentrations/samples per plate. Clear visual results.
-   **Limitations:** More manual steps than disk diffusion, results sensitive to well size/volume added, primarily qualitative/semi-quantitative (zone size comparison).

**2. Agar Plug Diffusion Method:**
-   **Principle:** Used to test for antimicrobial compounds secreted by a microorganism (producer strain). An agar plug from a producer culture is placed on a plate inoculated with a test (indicator) strain.
-   **Methodology:**
    1.  Culture the producer microorganism on agar to allow metabolite secretion.
    2.  Aseptically cut an agar plug from the producer culture.
    3.  Place the plug (producer side down) onto an agar plate freshly inoculated with the indicator strain.
    4.  Incubate.
    5.  Observe for a zone of inhibition around the plug, indicating antimicrobial production by the producer strain against the indicator strain.
-   **Advantages:** Useful for screening microbial antagonism and discovering secreted antimicrobial compounds. Direct visual evidence of interaction.
-   **Limitations:** Labor-intensive (two cultures + plug transfer), qualitative/semi-quantitative, results depend on producer growth, metabolite production/diffusion.

**3. Spot Assay:**
-   **Principle:** Rapid screening method where small volumes of test solutions are directly spotted onto an inoculated agar surface.
-   **Methodology:**
    1.  Prepare inoculated agar plate.
    2.  Spot small, defined volumes (e.g., 10 µL) of test solutions onto the surface at distinct locations.
    3.  Incubate.
    4.  Observe for clear zones of inhibition directly under/around the spots.
-   **Advantages:** Rapid, simple, requires minimal sample volume, suitable for high-throughput screening.
-   **Limitations:** Primarily qualitative, less sensitive than other diffusion methods, results highly dependent on spot volume/concentration/diffusion from the spot.

**4. Cross-Streaking Method:**
-   **Principle:** Qualitative method to assess antagonism between two microorganisms. One (producer) is streaked first, then the other (indicator) is streaked perpendicularly across the first streak.
-   **Methodology:**
    1.  Streak the producer strain in a single line on agar, incubate partially.
    2.  Streak the indicator strain perpendicular to the producer streak.
    3.  Incubate fully.
    4.  Observe growth patterns at the intersection. Inhibition of the indicator strain near the producer indicates antagonism.
-   **Advantages:** Simple visual screening for microbial interactions/antagonism.
-   **Limitations:** Qualitative, subjective interpretation, doesn't quantify activity, results specific to tested strains/conditions.
