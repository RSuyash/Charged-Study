# 2.1.3 Tetracyclines

**Overview:**
Tetracyclines are a class of broad-spectrum, primarily bacteriostatic antibiotics that inhibit bacterial protein synthesis. They were discovered in the 1940s from *Streptomyces* species.

**Chemical Structure:**
Characterized by a linear, fused four-ring (tetracyclic) naphthacene carboxamide core. Different tetracyclines have various substitutions on these rings, affecting their pharmacokinetic properties (absorption, half-life) and spectrum. They are amphoteric and can chelate divalent metal ions (like Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe²⁺), which impacts absorption and distribution.

**Examples:**
-   **Tetracycline:** The original compound.
-   **Doxycycline:** Longer half-life, better oral absorption (less affected by food/calcium), good tissue penetration. Preferred for many indications.
-   **Minocycline:** Good penetration into tissues, including CNS. Used for acne, sometimes MDR infections.
-   **Tigecycline:** A newer glycylcycline derivative, designed to overcome common tetracycline resistance mechanisms (efflux, ribosomal protection). Broader spectrum including MRSA, VRE, MDR Gram-negatives. Administered IV.
-   *Older examples:* Chlortetracycline, Oxytetracycline, Demeclocycline.

**Mechanism of Action:**
Tetracyclines inhibit protein synthesis by binding reversibly to the bacterial 30S ribosomal subunit. (See [[2.3.2 Inhibition of Protein Synthesis]])
1.  **Binding to 30S Ribosome:** Bind primarily to the 16S rRNA within the 30S subunit, near the A-site (aminoacyl-tRNA binding site).
2.  **Blocking Aminoacyl-tRNA Attachment:** This binding sterically hinders the incoming aminoacyl-tRNA from binding correctly to the A-site codon on the mRNA.
3.  **Inhibition of Peptide Elongation:** Prevents the addition of new amino acids to the growing peptide chain, thus halting protein synthesis.
4.  **Bacteriostatic Effect:** Inhibition of protein synthesis stops bacterial growth and replication.

**Spectrum of Activity:**
Broad-spectrum, active against:
-   Many Gram-positive bacteria (*Staphylococcus*, *Streptococcus* - though resistance is common).
-   Many Gram-negative bacteria (*E. coli*, *H. influenzae*, *Neisseria* - resistance varies).
-   **Atypical bacteria:** *Chlamydia*, *Mycoplasma*, *Rickettsia* (drugs of choice for infections caused by these).
-   Spirochetes (*Borrelia burgdorferi* - Lyme disease, *Treponema pallidum* - syphilis alternative).
-   Some protozoa (e.g., malaria prophylaxis - doxycycline).

**Clinical Uses:**
-   Atypical pneumonia (*Mycoplasma*, *Chlamydia*).
-   Sexually transmitted infections (*Chlamydia*, gonorrhea alternative).
-   Rickettsial infections (Rocky Mountain spotted fever, typhus).
-   Lyme disease.
-   Acne vulgaris (due to activity against *Propionibacterium acnes* and anti-inflammatory effects).
-   Malaria prophylaxis/treatment (doxycycline).
-   Community-acquired respiratory infections.
-   Anthrax, plague, tularemia alternatives.
-   Tigecycline used for complicated skin/intra-abdominal infections, often involving MDR organisms.

**Side Effects:**
-   **Gastrointestinal Disturbances:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea (common).
-   **Photosensitivity:** Increased susceptibility to sunburn (especially doxycycline, demeclocycline).
-   **Effects on Teeth and Bones:** Tetracyclines chelate calcium and incorporate into developing bones and teeth, causing permanent yellow/brown discoloration of teeth and potential bone growth inhibition. **Contraindicated in pregnant women (after first trimester), breastfeeding mothers, and children under 8 years old.**
-   **Hepatotoxicity:** Rare, but can occur, especially with high doses or in pregnancy.
-   **Vestibular Toxicity:** Dizziness, vertigo (more common with minocycline).
-   **Esophageal Ulceration:** If taken without sufficient water or before lying down.

**Resistance Mechanisms:**
Resistance is widespread.
-   **Efflux Pumps:** Most common mechanism. Active transport of tetracycline out of the bacterial cell (e.g., TetA, TetK pumps). Genes often on plasmids/transposons.
-   **Ribosomal Protection Proteins (RPPs):** Proteins (e.g., TetM, TetO) bind to the ribosome near the tetracycline binding site, causing conformational changes that dislodge the drug or prevent its binding, while still allowing protein synthesis to proceed.
-   **Enzymatic Inactivation:** Less common mechanism.

**Production:**
Produced industrially by fermentation using various species of *Streptomyces* bacteria (e.g., *S. aureofaciens*, *S. rimosus*). Semi-synthetic derivatives like doxycycline and minocycline are made by chemical modification of natural tetracyclines.
