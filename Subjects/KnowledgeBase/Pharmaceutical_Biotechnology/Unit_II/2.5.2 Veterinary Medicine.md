# 2.5.2 Veterinary Medicine Applications

Antibiotics are widely used in veterinary medicine for treating and preventing bacterial infections in both livestock (cattle, swine, poultry) and companion animals (dogs, cats).

**Applications:**

1.  **Treatment of Animal Infections:**
    -   Used to treat a wide range of bacterial diseases affecting animals, similar to human medicine.
    -   Examples include respiratory infections (e.g., shipping fever in cattle), mastitis (inflammation of mammary glands in dairy cows), skin and soft tissue infections, gastrointestinal infections, and urinary tract infections.
    -   Commonly used classes include [[2.1.3 Tetracyclines|Tetracyclines]], [[2.1.1.1 Penicillins|Penicillins]], [[2.1.1.2 Cephalosporins|Cephalosporins]], [[2.1.4 Macrolides|Macrolides]], and [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Sulfonamides]]. Choice depends on the animal species, infection type, and causative agent.

2.  **Disease Prevention (Metaphylaxis/Prophylaxis):**
    -   Antibiotics may be administered to groups of animals (metaphylaxis) to control the spread of disease when infection is diagnosed in some individuals within the group, or prophylactically during periods of high risk (e.g., transport, weaning).

3.  **Growth Promotion (Historical/Controversial Use):**
    -   Historically, sub-therapeutic doses of antibiotics were added to animal feed to promote faster growth and improve feed efficiency, particularly in poultry and swine production.
    -   **Mechanism:** Thought to involve suppression of gut bacteria that compete for nutrients or cause subclinical infections.
    -   **Concerns:** This practice is highly controversial and increasingly restricted or banned in many regions (including the EU and US) due to concerns about promoting the development and spread of [[2.4 Microbial Resistance to Antibiotics and Antimicrobial Agents|antimicrobial resistance]] (AMR) in bacteria that can potentially transfer to human pathogens via the food chain or environment.
    -   **Alternatives:** Focus is shifting towards improved animal husbandry, hygiene, vaccination, and non-antibiotic growth promoters.

**Considerations:**
-   **Withdrawal Periods:** Regulations require specific withdrawal periods after antibiotic treatment before animals or their products (milk, eggs) can enter the food supply, ensuring drug residues are below safe limits.
-   **Antimicrobial Stewardship:** Similar to human medicine, responsible use of antibiotics (veterinary stewardship) is crucial to preserve their effectiveness and minimize AMR development. This involves accurate diagnosis, appropriate drug selection, correct dosing, and avoiding unnecessary use, especially for growth promotion.
