# 2.5.1 Medical Applications

The primary and most significant application of antibiotics is in human medicine for combating infectious diseases and, in some cases, treating cancer or preventing infections.

**1. Treatment of Bacterial Infections:**
-   **Core Use:** Antibiotics are the cornerstone of treating infections caused by bacteria. They target specific bacterial processes like cell wall synthesis, protein synthesis, or nucleic acid replication, leading to bacterial death (bactericidal) or growth inhibition (bacteriostatic).
-   **Selection:** Choice depends on the identified or suspected pathogen, its susceptibility profile (See [[2.6 Assaying Antimicrobial Activity]]), site of infection, patient factors (allergies, organ function, age, pregnancy), and [[2.2.1 Spectrum of Activity|spectrum of activity]].
-   **Examples by Mechanism:**
    -   *Cell Wall Inhibitors:* [[2.1.1 β-Lactam Antibiotics|β-Lactams]] (Penicillins, Cephalosporins), [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Vancomycin]].
    -   *Protein Synthesis Inhibitors:* [[2.1.2 Aminoglycosides|Aminoglycosides]], [[2.1.3 Tetracyclines|Tetracyclines]], [[2.1.4 Macrolides|Macrolides]], [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Clindamycin]], [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Linezolid]].
    -   *Nucleic Acid Inhibitors:* [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Fluoroquinolones]], [[2.3.3 Interference with Nucleic Acid Synthesis|Rifampicin]], [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Metronidazole]].
    -   *Folate Synthesis Inhibitors:* [[2.1.5 Other Classes of Antibiotics|Sulfonamides/Trimethoprim]].

**2. Antitumor Antibiotics:**
-   **Mechanism:** Certain antibiotics, primarily derived from *Streptomyces*, exhibit cytotoxic activity against cancer cells, often by interfering with DNA structure or function. They are used as chemotherapeutic agents.
-   **Examples:**
    -   *Anthracyclines:* [[1.2.4 Other Classes of Secondary Metabolites|Doxorubicin]], Daunorubicin (intercalate DNA, inhibit topoisomerase II, generate ROS).
    -   *Actinomycin D (Dactinomycin):* Intercalates DNA, inhibits transcription.
    -   *Bleomycin:* Causes DNA strand breaks.
    -   *Mitomycin C:* DNA alkylating agent.
-   **Use:** Typically used in combination chemotherapy regimens for various cancers (leukemias, lymphomas, solid tumors).

**3. Prophylactic Use:**
-   **Definition:** Use of antibiotics to *prevent* infection in individuals at high risk, rather than treating an existing infection.
-   **Indications:**
    -   *Surgical Prophylaxis:* Administered shortly before surgery (e.g., Cefazolin for clean surgeries) to reduce the risk of surgical site infections (SSIs). Choice depends on surgical site and likely pathogens.
    -   *Dental Procedures:* Prevention of infective endocarditis in high-risk cardiac patients (e.g., prosthetic valves, previous endocarditis) undergoing certain dental procedures that can cause bacteremia. Amoxicillin is common; clindamycin or azithromycin used for penicillin allergy.
    -   *Opportunistic Infections:* Prevention in immunocompromised patients (e.g., TMP-SMX for *Pneumocystis* pneumonia prophylaxis in HIV/AIDS patients or transplant recipients).
    -   *Close Contacts:* Preventing infection after exposure to certain contagious diseases (e.g., Rifampicin for close contacts of patients with meningococcal meningitis).
-   **Considerations:** Prophylactic use should be targeted and short-term to minimize resistance development and side effects.
