# 4.4.2 Other Drug Delivery Systems

Besides [[4.4.1 Liposomes (Structure, Properties, and Applications)|liposomes]], numerous other drug delivery systems (DDS) are employed to improve drug efficacy, safety, and patient compliance.

![Other DDS Examples](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit IV 1a63a995df8780e5ae11ea18abd28f46/image%2032.png>)
*Fig: Examples of Other Drug Delivery Systems.*

**1. Nanoparticles (Non-Liposomal):** (1-1000 nm)
-   **Advantages:** EPR effect for passive tumor targeting, surface functionalization for active targeting, drug protection, controlled release.
-   **Materials:**
    -   *Polymers:* Biodegradable (PLA, PGA, PLGA, chitosan). Versatile for controlled release.
    -   *Lipids:* Solid Lipid Nanoparticles (SLNs) - lipid core, good for lipophilic drugs.
    -   *Metals:* Gold, silver nanoparticles - imaging, theranostics.
    -   *Inorganic:* Silica, quantum dots - imaging, stability.
    -   *Carbon-based:* Graphene, nanotubes - unique properties.

**2. Microparticles:** (1-1000 µm)
-   **Uses:** Often for longer-term controlled release (days to months), local delivery (implants, injections), sometimes oral delivery (protection from GI environment).
-   **Materials:** Similar to nanoparticles (e.g., PLGA).

**3. Dendrimers:**
-   **Structure:** Highly branched, tree-like macromolecules with defined structure.
-   **Advantages:** High drug loading capacity (surface & interior), potential for controlled release and targeting (surface modification).

**4. Hydrogels:**
-   **Structure:** Water-swellable polymer networks.
-   **Forms:** Injectable (gel *in situ*), implantable, transdermal patches.
-   **Advantages:** Biocompatible, high water content, suitable for sustained release, can be stimuli-responsive (pH, temp).

**5. Polymeric Micelles:**
-   **Structure:** Self-assembled nanoparticles from amphiphilic block copolymers (hydrophobic core, hydrophilic shell).
-   **Advantages:** Solubilize hydrophobic drugs, prolonged circulation (stealth effect from shell), potential for targeting.

**6. Drug Conjugates:**
-   **Principle:** Covalent attachment of drug to a carrier molecule.
-   **Examples:**
    -   *Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs):* Antibody targets specific cells (e.g., cancer), linked cytotoxic drug provides potency. Highly targeted therapy.
    -   *Polymer-Drug Conjugates:* Drug linked to polymer (e.g., PEG) to improve PK (half-life, solubility).

**7. Implants:**
-   **Principle:** Surgically placed devices for long-term drug delivery (weeks to years). Biodegradable or non-biodegradable.
-   **Uses:** Chronic conditions requiring sustained therapy (e.g., hormone release, contraception).

**8. Transdermal Patches:**
-   **Principle:** Deliver drugs through the skin for systemic effect. Non-invasive.
-   **Limitations:** Suitable only for potent, lipophilic drugs that can penetrate the stratum corneum.

**9. Inhalers:**
-   **Principle:** Deliver drugs directly to lungs for respiratory diseases (local effect) or sometimes systemic absorption.
-   **Types:** Metered-Dose Inhalers (MDIs), Dry Powder Inhalers (DPIs), Nebulizers.

**10. Microneedles:**
-   **Principle:** Tiny needles penetrate the outermost skin layer (stratum corneum), creating microchannels for drug delivery. Minimally invasive.
-   **Advantages:** Can deliver larger molecules (peptides, proteins) that don't cross skin easily.

**11. Ocular Delivery Systems:**
-   **Challenge:** Delivering drugs effectively to the eye (protective barriers).
-   **Systems:** Eye drops (conventional, often poor bioavailability), ointments, inserts (sustained release), contact lenses, injections (intravitreal).

**12. Nasal Delivery Systems:**
-   **Uses:** Local treatment (rhinitis) or systemic delivery (bypasses first-pass metabolism, rapid absorption for some drugs, potential brain delivery).

**13. Buccal/Sublingual Delivery Systems:**
-   **Principle:** Absorption through oral mucosa (under tongue or cheek). Bypasses first-pass metabolism.
-   **Forms:** Tablets, films, sprays.

**14. Gastrointestinal (GI) Delivery Systems:**
-   **Goal:** Optimize oral delivery - protect drug from stomach acid, control release site/rate, target specific GI regions.
-   **Examples:** Enteric-coated tablets (release in intestine), colon-targeted systems.

The choice of DDS depends heavily on the drug's properties (solubility, stability, potency), the desired therapeutic outcome (target site, release profile), and patient factors.
