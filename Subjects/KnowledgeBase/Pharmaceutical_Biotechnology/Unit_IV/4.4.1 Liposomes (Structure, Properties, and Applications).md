# 4.4.1 Liposomes (Structure, Properties, and Applications)

Liposomes are microscopic, spherical vesicles composed of one or more lipid bilayers enclosing an aqueous core. They mimic cell membrane structure and are highly versatile drug delivery carriers.

![Liposomes in Drug Delivery](<../../../V2/Pharmaceutical Biotechnology/Unit IV 1a63a995df8780e5ae11ea18abd28f46/image%2030.png>)
*Fig: Liposomes in Drug Delivery.*

**1. Structure:**
-   **Lipid Bilayer(s):** Composed of amphipathic lipids (e.g., phospholipids like phosphatidylcholine, cholesterol) arranged with hydrophobic tails inward and hydrophilic heads outward. Forms the vesicle shell.
-   **Aqueous Interior:** Core compartment enclosed by the bilayer(s), capable of encapsulating hydrophilic drugs.
-   **Lipid Composition:** Can be varied using different phospholipids, cholesterol (affects rigidity/stability), charged lipids (cationic/anionic), or functionalized lipids (e.g., PEGylated lipids for longer circulation).
-   **Types:**
    -   *Lamellarity:*
        -   Unilamellar Vesicles (ULVs): Single bilayer.
            -   Small Unilamellar Vesicles (SUVs): <100 nm.
            -   Large Unilamellar Vesicles (LUVs): >100 nm.
        -   Multilamellar Vesicles (MLVs): Multiple concentric bilayers ("onion-like").
        -   Multivesicular Vesicles (MVVs): Multiple non-concentric internal compartments.
    -   *Size:* Range from nm to µm. Influences biodistribution and uptake.
    -   *Charge:* Neutral, cationic, or anionic depending on lipids used.

**2. Properties:**
-   **Biocompatibility/Biodegradability:** Generally well-tolerated as they are made of natural lipid components. Degraded by endogenous enzymes.
-   **Encapsulation Versatility:** Can encapsulate both hydrophilic drugs (in aqueous core) and hydrophobic drugs (within lipid bilayer). Suitable for small molecules, proteins, nucleic acids.
-   **Targeted Delivery:** Surface can be modified with targeting ligands (antibodies, peptides, vitamins) for active targeting to specific cells/tissues. Can also passively target tumors via the EPR effect.
-   **Improved Drug Stability:** Protect encapsulated drugs from degradation in the body.
-   **Controlled/Sustained Release:** Drug release rate can be modulated by lipid composition, size, or by designing stimuli-responsive liposomes (pH, temperature sensitive).
-   **Enhanced Cellular Uptake:** Can facilitate drug entry into cells via endocytosis or membrane fusion.
-   **Reduced Toxicity:** By altering biodistribution and targeting drug delivery, liposomes can reduce exposure of healthy tissues to toxic drugs (e.g., chemotherapy).
-   **Improved Pharmacokinetics:** Can increase drug circulation time (especially with PEGylation - "stealth liposomes") and alter distribution patterns.

**3. Applications:**
-   **Cancer Therapy:** Delivery of cytotoxic drugs (e.g., Doxil/Caelyx - liposomal doxorubicin) to tumors, leveraging EPR effect and reducing cardiotoxicity.
-   **Gene Therapy:** Delivery of DNA/RNA (especially siRNA) using cationic liposomes (lipofection).
-   **Vaccine Delivery:** Encapsulation of antigens or adjuvants to enhance immune response.
-   **Protein/Peptide Delivery:** Protecting labile biologics and improving delivery.
-   **Antifungal Therapy:** Delivery of drugs like Amphotericin B (e.g., Ambisome) to reduce nephrotoxicity.
-   **Ophthalmic Delivery:** Eye drops or inserts for sustained release.
-   **Topical/Transdermal Delivery:** Enhancing skin penetration.
-   **Pulmonary Delivery:** Inhaled liposomal formulations for respiratory diseases.
-   **Diagnostics & Imaging:** Encapsulating contrast agents (MRI, CT) or radiopharmaceuticals.

**4. Challenges and Future Directions:**
-   **Stability:** Physical/chemical instability during storage or *in vivo*. Addressed by lipid choice, cholesterol, PEGylation, lyophilization.
-   **Targeting Efficiency:** Achieving high specificity remains challenging.
-   **Manufacturing Scale-up & Cost:** Can be complex and expensive.
-   **Drug Leakage:** Premature release before reaching the target.
-   **Immunogenicity:** Potential immune responses to certain components.
-   **Future:** Stimuli-responsive liposomes, multifunctional liposomes (theranostics), improved targeting strategies.

Liposomes are a clinically validated and versatile platform technology in drug delivery.
