# 4.3.1 Concept of Pharmacokinetics (ADME)

**Pharmacokinetics (PK)** describes the time course of drug concentrations in the body. It encompasses the processes of **Absorption**, **Distribution**, **Metabolism**, and **Excretion** (ADME) – collectively determining "what the body does to the drug." Understanding PK is essential for designing effective and safe dosing regimens.

**1. Absorption:**
-   **Definition:** Movement of drug from administration site into systemic circulation.
-   **Bioavailability (F):** Fraction of administered dose reaching systemic circulation unchanged (IV = 100%).
-   **Factors:**
    -   *Route of Administration:* IV, Oral (PO), IM, SC, Transdermal, Inhalation, etc. Oral absorption is variable (affected by GI factors, food, first-pass metabolism).
    -   *Drug Properties:* Lipophilicity, hydrophilicity, ionization (pKa vs. pH), molecular size, formulation (dissolution).
    -   *Physiological Factors:* Gastric emptying, intestinal motility, blood flow, disease states.

**2. Distribution:**
-   **Definition:** Reversible transfer of drug from blood to tissues/fluids.
-   **Factors:**
    -   *Blood Flow:* Higher flow = faster distribution to organs (brain, liver, kidney).
    -   *Plasma Protein Binding:* Only unbound (free) drug is active and distributes. High binding limits distribution, acts as reservoir, potential for drug interactions (displacement).
    -   *Tissue Permeability:* Ability to cross membranes (lipophilicity, transporters). Barriers like Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB) restrict entry to CNS.
    -   *Volume of Distribution (Vd):* Apparent volume the drug distributes into. High Vd = extensive tissue distribution; Low Vd = mainly in blood. `Vd = Dose / Plasma Concentration`.

**3. Metabolism (Biotransformation):**
-   **Definition:** Chemical modification of drugs, primarily in the liver, usually to increase water solubility for excretion. Can activate prodrugs.
-   **Phases:**
    -   *Phase I:* Oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis (introduce/expose functional groups). Often via Cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes.
    -   *Phase II:* Conjugation (attach polar molecules like glucuronide, sulfate). Catalyzed by transferases.
-   **Factors:**
    -   *Liver Function:* Impairment reduces metabolism.
    -   *CYP Enzymes:* Major family, genetic polymorphisms cause inter-individual variability. Subject to induction (e.g., by rifampicin) or inhibition (e.g., by erythromycin), leading to drug interactions.
    -   *First-Pass Metabolism:* Significant metabolism of oral drugs in gut wall/liver before reaching systemic circulation, reducing bioavailability.

**4. Excretion (Elimination):**
-   **Definition:** Irreversible removal of drug/metabolites from the body.
-   **Routes:**
    -   *Renal (Kidney):* Primary route for many drugs (glomerular filtration, tubular secretion, reabsorption). Requires water solubility. Impaired function requires dose adjustment.
    -   *Hepatic/Biliary (Liver/Bile):* Excretion into bile -> feces. May involve enterohepatic recirculation (reabsorption from gut).
    -   *Other:* Pulmonary (volatile drugs), breast milk, sweat.

**Key Pharmacokinetic Parameters:**
-   **Bioavailability (F):** Fraction absorbed systemically.
-   **Volume of Distribution (Vd):** Apparent volume drug distributes into.
-   **Clearance (CL):** Volume of plasma cleared of drug per unit time (efficiency of elimination).
-   **Half-life (t½):** Time for plasma concentration to decrease by 50%. Determines dosing interval.
-   **Area Under the Curve (AUC):** Measure of total drug exposure over time.
