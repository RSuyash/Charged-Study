# Antimicrobial Activity

This note describes the biological activity of compounds that inhibit the growth of or kill microorganisms, including bacteria (antibacterial), fungi (antifungal), viruses (antiviral), and protozoa. Many [[Secondary Metabolites]] exhibit antimicrobial properties, often as a defense mechanism for the producing organism.

## Mechanisms of Action

Antimicrobial agents work through various mechanisms, including:
*   Inhibition of cell wall synthesis (e.g., Penicillins, Cephalosporins - derived from microbial sources)
*   Disruption of cell membrane function (e.g., [[Polyketides|Polyenes]] like Amphotericin B, some [[Essential Oils]])
*   Inhibition of protein synthesis (e.g., [[Polyketides|Tetracyclines]], [[Polyketides|Macrolides]], Aminoglycosides)
*   Inhibition of nucleic acid synthesis (e.g., Quinolones, Rifampicin)
*   Inhibition of metabolic pathways (e.g., Sulfonamides, Trimethoprim)

## Secondary Metabolite Classes with Antimicrobial Activity

*   **[[Polyketides]]:** (from [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Malonyl-CoA Pathway (Polyketide Synthesis)|Malonyl-CoA Pathway]]) A major source of antibiotics (e.g., Erythromycin, Tetracycline) and antifungals (e.g., Amphotericin B).
*   **[[Alkaloids]]:** (from [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Amino Acid Derived Pathways|Amino Acid Derived Pathways]]) Many exhibit antibacterial and antifungal properties (e.g., Berberine, Quinine).
*   **[[Terpenoids]]:** (from [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Mevalonate Pathway (MVA)|MVA]] / [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Methylerythritol Phosphate Pathway (MEP)|MEP]] Pathways) Components of [[Essential Oils]] (e.g., Thymol, Carvacrol, Tea Tree Oil components) show broad-spectrum activity.
*   **[[Phenolic Compounds]]:** (primarily from [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Shikimic Acid Pathway|Shikimic Acid Pathway]]) Including simple phenols, phenolic acids, and flavonoids, often possess antimicrobial effects (e.g., Tannins, Eugenol).
*   **[[Coumarins]]:** Some show antibacterial and antifungal activity.
*   **[[Glycosides]]:** Activity often depends on the aglycone, but some classes like Saponins can have antimicrobial effects.

## Significance

*   **Medicine:** Foundation of treating infectious diseases.
*   **Ecology:** Role in microbial competition and host defense.
*   **Food Preservation:** Natural antimicrobials used to extend shelf life.
*   **Resistance:** A major challenge due to overuse and microbial evolution.
