# Alkaloids

## Overview

Alkaloids are a **major class of secondary metabolites**, typically derived from amino acids via [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Amino Acid Derived Pathways]], characterized by their nitrogen-containing structure and significant pharmacological activities. They are a diverse group of naturally occurring organic compounds that predominantly contain basic nitrogen atoms. While primarily derived from plant sources, they can also be found in fungi and some animals. This diverse distribution across organisms reflects their important ecological roles and has significant implications for pharmaceutical discovery and production. Alkaloids are known for their significant pharmacological effects, making them crucial in both traditional and modern medicine.

## Definition
=======

Alkaloids are a diverse group of naturally occurring organic compounds that predominantly contain basic nitrogen atoms. They are primarily derived from plant sources but can also be found in fungi and some animals. Alkaloids are known for their significant pharmacological effects, making them crucial in both traditional and modern medicine.

## Biological Sources

Alkaloids are found across various organisms, with plants being the primary producers. For a more comprehensive list of plant sources, see [[Plant Sources of Secondary Metabolites]].

### Plant Sources (Major)
- **Opium Poppy (*Papaver somniferum*)**: Source of morphine and codeine
- **Cinchona Bark (*Cinchona officinalis*)**: Source of quinine
- **Coca Plant (*Erythroxylum coca*)**: Source of cocaine
- **Madagascar Periwinkle (*Catharanthus roseus*)**: Source of vincristine
- **Daffodil (*Galanthus*)**: Source of galantamine

### Fungal Sources (Minor)
- **Ergot Fungus (*Claviceps purpurea*)**: Produces ergot alkaloids
- Various endophytic fungi producing novel alkaloids 

### Animal Sources (Limited)
- **Poison Dart Frogs**: Produce batrachotoxins
- **Marine Organisms**: Various unique alkaloid structures

## Key Principles

- Diverse structures with nitrogen in heterocyclic ring
- True alkaloids, protoalkaloids, pseudoalkaloids classifications
- Sourced primarily from plants, with some fungal and animal sources
- Wide range of pharmacological activities
