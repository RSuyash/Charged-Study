# Essential Oils

## Overview

Essential oils are complex, volatile, and fragrant mixtures of secondary metabolites extracted from aromatic plants. They represent the **odoriferous principles** of plants, responsible for their characteristic scents. Essential oils are predominantly composed of **terpenes** and **terpenoids**, along with other volatile aromatic and aliphatic compounds. They have diverse applications in aromatherapy, perfumery, cosmetics, food flavoring, and preservation.

## Composition and Chemical Diversity

-   **[[Terpenoids|Terpenes and Terpenoids]]:** Major constituents, derived from isoprene units produced via the [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Mevalonate Pathway (MVA)|MVA]] and [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Methylerythritol Phosphate Pathway (MEP)|MEP]] pathways. Rich in monoterpenes (C10) and sesquiterpenes (C15) and their oxygenated derivatives (alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, etc.). Examples: menthol, linalool, citral, camphor, thymol, eugenol, 1,8-cineole.
-   **Other Volatile Organic Compounds:** May contain phenylpropanoids (e.g., eugenol, cinnamaldehyde), aliphatic compounds, and volatile sulfur compounds.
-   **Complex Mixtures:** Typically contain dozens to hundreds of chemical constituents. Composition varies based on plant species, origin, conditions, extraction method, etc.

## Properties and Applications

-   **Aromatherapy and Psychological Effects:** Used for mood, emotions, well-being via inhalation. Effects linked to olfactory system and limbic system interaction.
    -   *Relaxing*: Lavender, chamomile.
    -   *Stimulating*: Peppermint, rosemary, citrus.
    -   *Mood-Elevating*: Bergamot, jasmine.
-   **Antimicrobial and Antiseptic Properties:** Broad-spectrum activity against bacteria, fungi, viruses. Examples: Tea tree oil, thyme oil, oregano oil, lavender oil. Mechanisms include membrane disruption, enzyme inhibition.
-   **Food Preservation:** Natural preservatives due to antimicrobial and antioxidant properties. Examples: Rosemary extract, thyme oil, clove oil.
-   **Fragrances and Flavors:** Widely used in perfumes, cosmetics, and as natural flavoring agents in food/beverages. Examples: Citrus oils, peppermint oil, vanilla extract.
-   **Insect Repellent and Insecticidal Properties:** Used in natural repellents. Examples: Citronella oil, eucalyptus oil, neem oil.

## Extraction Methods

-   **Steam Distillation:** Most common method.
-   **Hydrodistillation:** Plant material in boiling water.
-   **Expression (Cold Pressing):** Mainly for citrus peels.
-   **Solvent Extraction:** For delicate oils (produces absolutes, resinoids).
-   **Supercritical Fluid Extraction (SFE):** Uses supercritical CO2.
