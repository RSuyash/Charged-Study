# β-Lactam Antibiotics

β-Lactam antibiotics are a class of antibiotics characterized by the presence of a β-lactam ring in their molecular structure. They primarily inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis, leading to cell lysis and death.

## Types

*   [[Penicillins]]
*   [[Cephalosporins]]
*   [[Carbapenems]]
*   [[Monobactams]]

## Mechanism of Action

β-Lactam antibiotics inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding to [[Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs)]] and preventing the cross-linking of peptidoglycans.

## Resistance Mechanisms

*   [[β-Lactamase]] production: Enzymes that hydrolyze the β-lactam ring.
*   Altered [[Penicillin-Binding Proteins (PBPs)]]: Mutations in PBPs reduce binding affinity for β-Lactams.
*   Efflux pumps: Bacteria expel β-Lactam antibiotics before they can exert their effects.

## Bacterial Targets

β-Lactam antibiotics are effective against a wide range of bacteria, including:

*   Gram-positive bacteria
*   Gram-negative bacteria

## Production

β-Lactam antibiotics are produced through [[Fermentation]] processes using various microorganisms, including:

*   *[[Penicillium]]*
*   *[[Acremonium]]*
*   *[[Streptomyces]]*
