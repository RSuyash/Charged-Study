# Malonyl-CoA Pathway (Polyketide Synthesis)

The Malonyl-CoA Pathway is central to the biosynthesis of fatty acids and a structurally diverse class of [[Secondary Metabolites]] known as [[Polyketides]]. It involves the sequential condensation of activated carboxylic acid units, typically Acetyl-CoA (as a starter unit) and Malonyl-CoA (as extender units).

## Mechanism: Polyketide Synthases (PKS)

The synthesis is carried out by large, multi-domain enzymes or enzyme complexes called **Polyketide Synthases (PKS)**. These function similarly to Fatty Acid Synthases (FAS) but exhibit key differences allowing for greater structural variation:

1.  **Starter Unit Selection:** PKS can utilize various starter units besides Acetyl-CoA.
2.  **Extender Unit Selection:** Primarily uses Malonyl-CoA, but variations exist.
3.  **Degree of Reduction:** Unlike FAS which fully reduces keto groups after each condensation, PKS can perform incomplete reduction (or no reduction), leaving keto, hydroxyl, or double bond functionalities in the growing chain.
4.  **Chain Length Control:** PKS determines the final length of the polyketide chain.
5.  **Cyclization/Modification:** PKS domains or associated enzymes can catalyze cyclizations, aromatizations, glycosylations, etc., leading to immense structural diversity.

## Types of PKS

*   **Type I PKS:** Large, multifunctional proteins with multiple domains arranged in modules (common in bacteria and fungi).
*   **Type II PKS:** Complexes of discrete, monofunctional proteins (common in bacteria, responsible for aromatic polyketides).
*   **Type III PKS:** Smaller, homodimeric enzymes, distinct from Type I/II (common in plants, e.g., chalcone synthase for flavonoids).

## Significance

*   **Fatty Acid Synthesis:** Essential for producing fatty acids for membranes and energy storage (via FAS).
*   **[[Polyketides]] Production:** Generates a vast array of bioactive [[Secondary Metabolites]], including many antibiotics (erythromycin, tetracycline), antifungals (amphotericin B), immunosuppressants (rapamycin), and cholesterol-lowering agents (lovastatin).
*   **[[Phenolic Compounds]] in Plants:** Type III PKS are crucial for flavonoid and stilbene biosynthesis (linking this pathway to [[Shikimic Acid Pathway]] products).

## Connections

*   **Upstream:** Linked to Acetyl-CoA metabolism (from glycolysis, fatty acid oxidation). Acetyl-CoA carboxylase converts Acetyl-CoA to Malonyl-CoA.
*   **Downstream:** Leads to fatty acids and diverse [[Polyketides]]. Can intersect with other pathways (e.g., Type III PKS using coumaroyl-CoA from the Shikimic Acid Pathway).
*   **Related Metabolites:** [[Phenolic Compounds]] (flavonoids via Type III PKS), [[Terpenoids]] (some polyketides undergo [[Structural Features/Meroterpenoids and Prenylation|prenylation]]).
