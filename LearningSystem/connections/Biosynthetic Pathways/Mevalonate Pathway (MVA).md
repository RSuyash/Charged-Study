# Mevalonate Pathway (MVA)

The Mevalonate (MVA) Pathway is a key metabolic pathway that produces Isopentenyl Pyrophosphate (IPP) and Dimethylallyl Pyrophosphate (DMAPP), the universal five-carbon precursors for [[Terpenoids]] and steroids.

## Key Steps & Intermediates

1.  Three molecules of **Acetyl-CoA** condense to form **HMG-CoA**.
2.  HMG-CoA is reduced to **Mevalonate** by the enzyme HMG-CoA reductase (a key regulatory step and target of statin drugs).
3.  Mevalonate is phosphorylated twice and then decarboxylated to yield **IPP**.
4.  IPP can be isomerized to **DMAPP** by IPP isomerase.

## Significance

*   **[[Terpenoids]] Synthesis:** Provides the building blocks for the vast array of terpenoids (e.g., [[Essential Oils]], carotenoids, gibberellins).
*   **Steroid Synthesis:** In animals and fungi, this pathway is the sole source of precursors for cholesterol and other steroids.
*   **Protein [[Structural Features/Meroterpenoids and Prenylation|Prenylation]]:** IPP and DMAPP derivatives are used to modify proteins and other molecules.
*   **Drug Target:** HMG-CoA reductase is the target of statin drugs used to lower cholesterol.

## Connections

*   **Upstream:** Linked to Acetyl-CoA metabolism (from glycolysis, fatty acid oxidation).
*   **Downstream:** IPP and DMAPP feed directly into [[Terpenoids]] biosynthesis.
*   **Alternative Pathway:** Operates alongside or instead of the [[Methylerythritol Phosphate Pathway (MEP)]] in different organisms/compartments. Eukaryotic cytoplasm typically uses MVA.
