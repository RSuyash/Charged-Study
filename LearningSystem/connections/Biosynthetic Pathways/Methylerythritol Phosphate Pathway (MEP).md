# Methylerythritol Phosphate Pathway (MEP)

The Methylerythritol Phosphate (MEP) Pathway, also known as the non-mevalonate pathway or the DOXP/MEP pathway, is an alternative metabolic route for the synthesis of Isopentenyl Pyrophosphate (IPP) and Dimethylallyl Pyrophosphate (DMAPP), the precursors for [[Terpenoids]].

## Key Steps & Intermediates

1.  Condensation of **Pyruvate** and **Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate (G3P)** to form **1-deoxy-D-xylulose 5-phosphate (DOXP)**.
2.  Rearrangement and reduction of DOXP to **Methylerythritol Phosphate (MEP)**.
3.  Several subsequent steps involving CMP intermediates lead to the formation of **IPP** and **DMAPP**.

## Significance

*   **[[Terpenoids]] Synthesis in Plants/Bacteria:** Provides the IPP and DMAPP precursors primarily in plant plastids (for monoterpenes, diterpenes, carotenoids, chlorophyll side chains) and in many bacteria.
*   **Essential Pathway:** Essential for organisms that rely on it, as it provides vital isoprenoid precursors.
*   **Drug/Herbicide Target:** Enzymes unique to the MEP pathway (not found in animals using the MVA pathway) are attractive targets for developing specific antibiotics and herbicides (e.g., Fosmidomycin targets DXR, the enzyme converting DOXP to MEP).
*   **[[Structural Features/Meroterpenoids and Prenylation|Prenylation]] Precursors:** Provides the IPP/DMAPP used for prenylating other molecules in organisms utilizing this pathway.

## Connections

*   **Upstream:** Linked to glycolysis (Pyruvate, G3P).
*   **Downstream:** IPP and DMAPP feed directly into [[Terpenoids]] biosynthesis (e.g., [[Essential Oils]], carotenoids).
*   **Alternative Pathway:** Operates alongside or instead of the [[Mevalonate Pathway (MVA)]] in different organisms/compartments. Plant plastids typically use MEP, while the cytoplasm uses MVA. Many bacteria exclusively use MEP.
