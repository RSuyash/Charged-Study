# Anti-inflammatory Activity

This note describes the biological activity of compounds that reduce inflammation or swelling. Inflammation is a complex biological response of body tissues to harmful stimuli, such as pathogens, damaged cells, or irritants. Anti-inflammatory agents work by inhibiting various mediators or pathways involved in the inflammatory process.

## Mechanisms of Action

Anti-inflammatory agents can act through diverse mechanisms, including:
*   Inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes (e.g., NSAIDs)
*   Inhibition of lipoxygenase (LOX) enzymes
*   Inhibition of pro-inflammatory cytokine production (e.g., TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6)
*   Modulation of transcription factors involved in inflammation (e.g., NF-κB)
*   Scavenging of reactive oxygen species (ROS) that contribute to inflammation

## Secondary Metabolite Classes with Anti-inflammatory Activity

*   **[[Phenolic Compounds]]:** (primarily from [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Shikimic Acid Pathway|Shikimic Acid Pathway]]) Many phenolics, including flavonoids (e.g., Quercetin, Apigenin) and phenolic acids (e.g., Caffeic acid, Ferulic acid, Salicylic acid - precursor to Aspirin), exhibit significant anti-inflammatory effects. Curcumin (a polyphenol) is well-known for this.
*   **[[Terpenoids]]:** (from [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Mevalonate Pathway (MVA)|MVA]] / [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Methylerythritol Phosphate Pathway (MEP)|MEP]] Pathways) Various terpenoids show anti-inflammatory potential (e.g., Limonene, Beta-caryophyllene, components of [[Essential Oils]]). Some steroidal compounds (related to triterpenes) are potent anti-inflammatories (e.g., corticosteroids, though often considered primary or modified primary metabolites in animals).
*   **[[Alkaloids]]:** (from [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Amino Acid Derived Pathways|Amino Acid Derived Pathways]]) Some alkaloids possess anti-inflammatory properties (e.g., Colchicine, Berberine).
*   **[[Coumarins]]:** Certain coumarins have demonstrated anti-inflammatory activity.
*   **[[Polyketides]]:** Some polyketides may have anti-inflammatory effects, although less commonly highlighted than their antibiotic or anticancer roles.

## Significance

*   **Medicine:** Treatment of inflammatory diseases (e.g., arthritis, asthma, inflammatory bowel disease).
*   **Health:** Chronic inflammation is linked to various diseases, and dietary components with anti-inflammatory activity are of interest.
