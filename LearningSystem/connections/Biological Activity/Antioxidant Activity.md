# Antioxidant Activity

This note describes the biological activity of compounds that inhibit oxidation. Oxidation is a chemical reaction that can produce free radicals, leading to chain reactions that may damage cells (oxidative stress). Antioxidants terminate these chain reactions by removing free radical intermediates or inhibiting other oxidation reactions.

## Mechanisms of Action

Antioxidants act through various mechanisms:
*   **Free Radical Scavenging:** Directly neutralizing free radicals (e.g., donating a hydrogen atom).
*   **Metal Ion Chelation:** Binding metal ions (like iron and copper) that can catalyze oxidative reactions.
*   **Inhibition of Oxidative Enzymes:** Blocking enzymes like xanthine oxidase that generate reactive oxygen species.
*   **Upregulation of Endogenous Antioxidant Defenses:** Stimulating the body's own antioxidant systems (e.g., glutathione, superoxide dismutase).

## Secondary Metabolite Classes with Antioxidant Activity

*   **[[Phenolic Compounds]]:** (primarily from [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Shikimic Acid Pathway|Shikimic Acid Pathway]]) This is a major class known for potent antioxidant activity due to the ability of the phenolic hydroxyl group to donate hydrogen atoms. Includes:
    *   *Flavonoids* (e.g., Quercetin, Catechins in tea, Anthocyanins in berries)
    *   *Phenolic Acids* (e.g., Caffeic acid, Ferulic acid, Gallic acid)
    *   *Tannins*
    *   *Stilbenes* (e.g., Resveratrol)
*   **[[Terpenoids]]:** (from [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Mevalonate Pathway (MVA)|MVA]] / [[Biosynthetic Pathways/Methylerythritol Phosphate Pathway (MEP)|MEP]] Pathways) Particularly carotenoids (e.g., Beta-carotene, Lycopene) and some components of [[Essential Oils]]. Vitamin E (tocopherols) is a crucial lipid-soluble antioxidant with a terpenoid side chain.
*   **[[Alkaloids]]:** Some alkaloids possess antioxidant properties, though it's less defining for the class as a whole compared to phenolics.
*   **[[Coumarins]]:** Certain coumarins exhibit antioxidant effects.
*   **Vitamins:** Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid) is a primary water-soluble antioxidant (though often considered a primary metabolite or essential nutrient rather than a typical secondary metabolite).

## Significance

*   **Health:** Protection against oxidative stress implicated in aging and various chronic diseases (cardiovascular disease, neurodegenerative disorders, cancer).
*   **Food Preservation:** Used to prevent oxidation of fats and oils, extending shelf life.
*   **Cosmetics:** Protection against skin damage caused by UV radiation and environmental pollutants.
