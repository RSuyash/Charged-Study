# Antimalarial Activity

This note describes the biological activity of compounds that are effective against malaria parasites (*Plasmodium* species). Malaria is a life-threatening disease transmitted through the bites of infected female *Anopheles* mosquitoes. Antimalarial drugs target different stages of the parasite's life cycle.

## Mechanisms of Action

Antimalarial agents work through various mechanisms, including:
*   Inhibition of heme detoxification (e.g., Chloroquine, Quinine)
*   Generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) that damage the parasite (e.g., Artemisinin derivatives)
*   Inhibition of protein synthesis
*   Inhibition of folate synthesis

## Secondary Metabolite Classes with Antimalarial Activity

*   **[[Alkaloids]]:** Quinine (from *Cinchona* bark) was a historically important antimalarial.
*   **[[Terpenoids]]:** Artemisinin (a sesquiterpene lactone from *Artemisia annua*) and its derivatives are currently first-line treatments.
*   Other classes are under investigation.

## Significance

*   **Medicine:** Essential for treating and preventing malaria, particularly in endemic regions.
*   **Drug Resistance:** A major challenge, necessitating the discovery of new antimalarial agents.
